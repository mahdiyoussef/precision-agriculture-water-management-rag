34   1       Introduction [SOURCE: Page_6]

35   Orographic precipitation and intense convective systems are common in the [SOURCE: Page_6]

36   Mediterranean region. They can potentially lead to flash floods, creating [SOURCE: Page_6]

37   significant environmental and socio-economic impacts. Prediction of these [SOURCE: Page_6]

38   systems is a challenge due to the complex interaction between oceanic, atmo- [SOURCE: Page_6]

39   spheric, and hydrological processes (Ducrocq et al., 2010). The Hydrological [SOURCE: Page_6]

40   cycle in Mediterranean Experiment (HyMeX) is an international initiative [SOURCE: Page_6]

41   launched in 2007 aiming at a better understanding of the hydrological cycle [SOURCE: Page_6]

42   and processes in the Mediterranean basin (Drobinski et al., 2013; Ducrocq [SOURCE: Page_6]

43   et al., 2013). One of the focuses includes high impact weather events involv- [SOURCE: Page_6]

44   ing heavy precipitation and flash flooding. [SOURCE: Page_6]

45   The Mediterranean region is characterized by a hydrological cycle bring- [SOURCE: Page_6]

46   ing long dry summers where drought often occurs, and wet fall and winter [SOURCE: Page_6]

47   periods (Drobinski et al., 2013). Typical to this highly variable hydrologi- [SOURCE: Page_6]

48   cal cycle is the occurrence of heavy precipitation causing flash flooding and [SOURCE: Page_6]

49   floods (Gaume et al., 2004; Delrieu et al., 2005; Borga et al., 2007; Gaume [SOURCE: Page_6]

50   et al., 2009). The FloodScale project, which is centered around the Cévennes- [SOURCE: Page_6]

51   Vivarais region in southern France, contributes to the HYMEX initiative and [SOURCE: Page_6]

52   aims to deepen the understanding of flash flood occurrences and the con- [SOURCE: Page_6]

53   tributing hydrological processes. [SOURCE: Page_6]

55      Soil moisture conditions are of particular importance for predicting hydro- [SOURCE: Page_7]

56   logical processes because they can influence the relative proportion of rainfall [SOURCE: Page_7]

57   input among the possible overland and subsurface pathways (Massari et al., [SOURCE: Page_7]

58   2013). Root zone soil moisture has been shown to influence the dynamics of [SOURCE: Page_7]

59   evapotranspiration and drainage processes (Albertson and Kiely, 2000) lead- [SOURCE: Page_7]

60   ing to impacts on the partitioning of latent and sensible heat exchanges to [SOURCE: Page_7]

61   the atmosphere. Furthermore, the antecedent soil moisture conditions of a [SOURCE: Page_7]

62   catchment have been shown in previous studies to be very influential in pre- [SOURCE: Page_7]

63   dicting flood occurrence (De Michele and Salvadori , 2002; Norbiato et al., [SOURCE: Page_7]

64   2009; Sangati et al., 2009; Tramblay et al., 2010), also specific for Mediter- [SOURCE: Page_7]

65   ranean regions (Massari et al., 2013; Aronica and Candela, 2004). [SOURCE: Page_7]

66 [SOURCE: Page_7]

67      Obtaining representative catchment scale soil moisture measurements, [SOURCE: Page_7]

68   even in small catchments, can be difficult given the dynamic spatial and [SOURCE: Page_7]

69   temporal behaviour of soil moisture (Teuling and Troch, 2005; Brocca et al., [SOURCE: Page_7]

70   2009a). Previous studies have shown the influence of topographical features [SOURCE: Page_7]

71   (Famiglietti et al., 1998; Brocca et al., 2007) and soil properties (Teuling and [SOURCE: Page_7]

72   Troch, 2005) on soil moisture values found at the field scale. In a theoretical [SOURCE: Page_7]

73   study done by Albertson and Montaldo (2003), the temporal dynamics of soil [SOURCE: Page_7]

74   moisture were explored in the context of the relative influences of parameters [SOURCE: Page_7]

80      Despite soil moisture being highly variable at small scales, soil moisture [SOURCE: Page_8]

81   fields have been known to display temporal stability. This concept was first [SOURCE: Page_8]

82   introduced by Vachaud et al. (1985) who noticed that, although soil moisture [SOURCE: Page_8]

83   variability can be quite high, deviations from the spatial mean show a strong [SOURCE: Page_8]

84   temporal persistence. Chen (2006) introduced the term rank stability to de- [SOURCE: Page_8]

85   scribe the temporal stability of soil moisture. In a review on soil moisture [SOURCE: Page_8]

86   observation studies, Vanderlinden et al. (2012) show that rank stability in [SOURCE: Page_8]

87   soil moisture has been observed under a wide range of conditions; at different [SOURCE: Page_8]

88   spatial scales, different temporal scales, and for different soil and vegetation [SOURCE: Page_8]

89   types, although Martı́nez et al. (2013) showed that a relation exists between [SOURCE: Page_8]

90   rank stability and climate and soil properties. From this concept, it follows [SOURCE: Page_8]

91   that a limited number of point measurements might be sufficient to infer [SOURCE: Page_8]

92   areal or catchment mean values for soil moisture (Teuling et al., 2006; Brocca [SOURCE: Page_8]

93   et al., 2012). [SOURCE: Page_8]
