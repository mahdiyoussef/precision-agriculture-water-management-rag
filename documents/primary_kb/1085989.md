### 5.3. New categories transformed politics and, by consequence waterscapes

As water science evolves, it might change the social and political order giving rise to the [SOURCE: Page_26]

dominant framing (Jasanoff, 2004). Before the PIREN Seine started, stakeholders in water [SOURCE: Page_26]

management had little idea of the extent to which they could be held responsible for the [SOURCE: Page_26]

production of ammonia, nitrates, phosphorous, organic matter, and algae at the watershed [SOURCE: Page_26]

level. They were interested in the possibility of results showing that they were not responsible [SOURCE: Page_26]

for the lion’s share of pollution. They agreed to make their data available to researchers, and [SOURCE: Page_26]

to finance the research project in order to modify their justifications for funding according to [SOURCE: Page_26]

a changing array of problems. Results unexpectedly weakened the positions of those asking [SOURCE: Page_26]

no equivalent. Modelling the Seine River as a series of bioreactors created a space of [SOURCE: Page_26]
