613   Conclusions [SOURCE: Page_26]

614   The results of the study presented in this paper expose that rivalries which took place in the past in the [SOURCE: Page_26]

615   Thau coastal lagoon are declared by stakeholders to be related to water quality issues in the lagoon. [SOURCE: Page_26]

616   Today, the water quality restoration is the result of twenty years of several multi-disciplinary programs [SOURCE: Page_26]

617   alternately conducted by scientists and by the local public bodies. Moreover, this has been possible [SOURCE: Page_26]

618   through two main factors. Firstly, as confirmed by the SNA outputs, through the central position of the [SOURCE: Page_26]

619   public body SMBT and the strong focus of water management towards lagoon water quality and [SOURCE: Page_26]

620   shellfish farming. Secondly, through the creation by the LR region of the LMN in 2000 to provide a [SOURCE: Page_26]

621   common knowledge base between stakeholders (both water users and water managers), experts and [SOURCE: Page_26]

622   scientists for implementing integrated management solutions for the transitional waters. [SOURCE: Page_26]

623   Climate change is likely to bring the context of relations between users of the water resource in tense [SOURCE: Page_26]

624   situations similar to those happened in the past. Specifically, 2050s projections indicate that yearly [SOURCE: Page_26]

625   mean precipitation is expected to decrease from about 2% to about 15%, while conversely [SOURCE: Page_26]

626   temperature is expected to increase on average from about 2°C to about 3.5°C. The temperature [SOURCE: Page_26]

627   increase will exacerbate the evapotranspiration process, leading to a dryer soil moisture conditions, [SOURCE: Page_26]

628   which will require larger water quantity for irrigation, while precipitation decrease will be obviously [SOURCE: Page_26]

26 [SOURCE: Page_26]

in : Ocean & Coastal Management, vol. 118, n° Part B, december 2015. p^234-246 [SOURCE: Page_27]

629   reflected in lower runoff production. These critical changes will imply potential negative impacts on [SOURCE: Page_27]

630   water quality comprising anoxic crises. [SOURCE: Page_27]

631   In order to answer to climate change impacts, the LR region managed the Aqua Domitia project to [SOURCE: Page_27]

632   conduct freshwater from the Rhône to the Thau catchment to supply irrigation needs. This water is [SOURCE: Page_27]

633   already used for drinking water production supplying industrial, domestic and tourism water uses. [SOURCE: Page_27]

634   While the SMBT is devoted to control the urban spread and the regulation of human activities [SOURCE: Page_27]

635   development on its territory with the two main tools represented by the SCoT and the SAGE Thau, this [SOURCE: Page_27]

636   provision of an “unlimited” water resource can be considered as incoherent. Moreover, as this paper [SOURCE: Page_27]

637   demonstrates, the absence of perception of both water transfer and climate changes impacts, the [SOURCE: Page_27]

638   dissemination of threats due to climate change and the use of the untreated water of the Rhône River [SOURCE: Page_27]

639   or the water quality of the lagoon is really needed. The specificities of the Aqua Domitia requires a [SOURCE: Page_27]

640   trans-basin governance which could have relied on the existing LMN. However, the LR region decided [SOURCE: Page_27]

641   to end its financial support to the LMN in 2013! Since then, stakeholders no longer have contact that [SOURCE: Page_27]

642   can meet with an integrated approach to their problems. More importantly, the methods used to [SOURCE: Page_27]

643   resolve situations in the past could be inefficient for future situations. In the present state of knowledge [SOURCE: Page_27]

644   on the impact of climate change, it is likely that the chains of cause and effect for achieving the same [SOURCE: Page_27]

645   situation of deterioration of water quality are different. Thus, the tools previously developed, including [SOURCE: Page_27]

646   those offered by the LMN, although operational and valid today for some regional coastal lagoons, can [SOURCE: Page_27]

647   become obsolete if not updated continuously with the increase in knowledge about all the criteria [SOURCE: Page_27]

648   explaining the chain of cause and effect between climate change and water quality. [SOURCE: Page_27]

649   The political decision to end this network, beyond demonstrating a lack of appreciation of the issues of [SOURCE: Page_27]

650   the territory, can be much more problematic for some time as it will induce rearrangements of this [SOURCE: Page_27]

651   network. There is every reason to think the establishment of regional versus local water governance [SOURCE: Page_27]

652   will be challenging in the coming years. Indeed, by denying the territory to continue in developing its [SOURCE: Page_27]

653   own methods of water quality management, it increases the risk of development outside the [SOURCE: Page_27]

654   challenges of climate change, that is to say, unsustainable development or maladaptation. This [SOURCE: Page_27]

655   contributes to increase the vulnerabilities of the Thau territory to climate change impacts. [SOURCE: Page_27]
