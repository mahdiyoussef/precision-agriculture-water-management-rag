This work aims at studying the spatial 24 25       16 variability of grapevine phenology at the within field scale. It was conducted on two fields, 26 27 17 one of cv Cabernet Sauvignon of 1.56 ha and the other of cv Chardonnay of 1.66 ha, both 28       18 located in Maule Valley, Chile. Within each vine field, a regular sampling grid was designed, 29 30       19 to carry out weekly measurements of phenology and maturation. The main results show that 31 32       20 there is a significant spatial variability in the phenological development and maturation at 33 34       21 the within field scale for both fields. This variability is spatially organised and temporaly 35 36       22 stable from the beginning of the season (post-budburst) to harvest and over the years. A 37 38 23 cluster analysis allowed us to define two clearly contrasted zones in terms of phenology and 39       24 maturation in both fields, explained by the microclimate. The magnitude of difference 40 41       25 between zones varied from 4 to 9 days depending on phenological stages and from 5 to 43 42 43       26 days for maturation. These differences are similar and comparable to that observed at larger 44 45       27 scales or under scenarios of climate change. These results highlight the necessity to better 46 47       28 take into account this variability to improve sampling and to base decisions of production 48       29 activities (spraying, harvest, pruning, etc.) application on more relevant information. Further 49 50       30 investigations should determine the environmental factors that determine the observed spatial 51 52       31 variability. 53 54       32 55 56       33 Keywords: Vitis vinifera, berry maturity, within field variability, temporal variability, 57 58       34 management zones, climate change. 59       35 60 61 62                                                                                                        1 63 64 65 [SOURCE: Page_2]
