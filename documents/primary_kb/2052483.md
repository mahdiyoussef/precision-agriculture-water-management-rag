Abstract [SOURCE: Page_2]

Sustainable treatment and management of fecal sludge in rural area requires adapted [SOURCE: Page_2]

solutions. Rustic and simple operating processes such as sludge treatment reed beds [SOURCE: Page_2]

(STRB) have been increasingly considered for this purpose. The biggest full scale septage [SOURCE: Page_2]

treatment unit in France had been built in Nègrepelisse with the final objectives of reusing [SOURCE: Page_2]

treated sludge and leachates for agriculture spreading and trees irrigation, respectively. The [SOURCE: Page_2]

aim of this investigation was to validate the treatment chain of this installation. The [SOURCE: Page_2]

obtained field data showed firstly that the overall removal efficiencies of STRB were [SOURCE: Page_2]

satisfactory and stable. Removal rates higher than 98% for COD and SS and a bit lower for [SOURCE: Page_2]

KN represented so far a beneficial septage treatment by STRB. The highlighted necessity [SOURCE: Page_2]

of a suitable complementary leachate treatment (before tree irrigation) justified the [SOURCE: Page_2]

presence of the second stage of vertical flow constructed wetland. The sludge deposit [SOURCE: Page_2]

drying and mineralization efficiencies were on the right track. According to hydrotextural [SOURCE: Page_2]

diagram analysis, surface deposit was however found to have high deformability probably [SOURCE: Page_2]

due to the youngness of the installation. An in-depth understanding of STRB system needs [SOURCE: Page_2]

continuous long-term studies. Further investigations would therefore be worth to evaluate [SOURCE: Page_2]

sustainability of the whole installation. [SOURCE: Page_2]

## INTRODUCTION

Faced with the increase of septage flow in rural area (Troesch et al., 2009), which has been induced [SOURCE: Page_3]

by the action of public services for non-collective wastewater treatment, sustainable treatments and [SOURCE: Page_3]

managements of fecal sludge has become important for small communities in France. This need is [SOURCE: Page_3]

also true for developing countries where latrines are widely used (Kengne et al., 2009). If [SOURCE: Page_3]

conventional treatment technics rely on transporting septage to large wastewater treatment plants, [SOURCE: Page_3]

they induce high costs and environmental impacts due to liquid sludge transportations. In this [SOURCE: Page_3]

context, rustic and simple operating processes such as sludge treatment reed beds (STRB, or sludge [SOURCE: Page_3]

drying reed bed) have been increasingly considered as adapted solutions (Kengne et al., 2009; Paing [SOURCE: Page_3]

and Voisin, 2005; Troesch et al., 2009; Uggetti et al., 2009; Vincent et al., 2011; Vincent et al., [SOURCE: Page_3]

2012) to provide dedicated optimum septage treatments units nearby fecal sludge production areas. [SOURCE: Page_3]

Previously initiated for activated sludge volume reduction in Denmark since 1988 (Nielsen, 1990), [SOURCE: Page_3]

the STRB system has proven its sludge treatment efficiency (Nassar et al., 2006; Nielsen, 2007; [SOURCE: Page_3]

Obarska-Pempkowiak et al., 2003; Troesch et al., 2009; Uggetti et al., 2009), allowing long-term [SOURCE: Page_3]

sludge reduction via dewatering and mineralization processes. Since the early 1990s in France [SOURCE: Page_3]

(Liénard et al., 1995), the use of STRB has been progressively adapted for septage treatment [SOURCE: Page_3]

(Troesch et al., 2009; Vincent et al., 2011; Vincent et al., 2012). Several French research programs [SOURCE: Page_3]

carried out during recent years (2007-2012) have proposed suitable design, operation and [SOURCE: Page_3]

management rules for the septage treatment by STRB (Molle et al., 2013). However, a few full [SOURCE: Page_3]

scale systems have been monitored in France, as well as design and operating rules need to be [SOURCE: Page_3]

validated. [SOURCE: Page_3]

A full scale STRB unit treating 11,000 m^3 y-1 of septage has been built in Nègrepelisse (south of [SOURCE: Page_3]

France) with final objectives of reusing treated sludge and leachates for agriculture spreading and [SOURCE: Page_3]

trees irrigation (poplar and eucalyptus used for the municipal heating system), respectively. This [SOURCE: Page_3]

septage treatment unit has been implemented on the basis of design rules established on pilot scale [SOURCE: Page_3]

experiments (Troesch et al., 2009; Molle et al., 2013). Such installation is the biggest trial in France [SOURCE: Page_3]

representing an ecological solution for satisfactory local treatment of fecal sludge and appropriate [SOURCE: Page_4]

reuses of residual products. It needed therefore investigations to validate the treatment efficiencies [SOURCE: Page_4]

and precise operation modes. For this purpose, this treatment plant was monitored since its [SOURCE: Page_4]

inception with a focus on: (i) fecal sludge characterization, (ii) performance assessment (septage by [SOURCE: Page_4]

STRB and leachate by VFCW) and (iii) sludge deposit evolution (dewatering, mineralization and [SOURCE: Page_4]

hydrotextural properties). [SOURCE: Page_4]

## MATERIALS AND METHODS

Description of the treatment plant [SOURCE: Page_4]

Installation. The full scale STRB unit located in Nègrepelisse (Tarn-et-Garonne, France; Figure 1, [SOURCE: Page_4]

Table 1) has been in operation since the end of 2013. It can receive up to 131 t of SS per year. This [SOURCE: Page_4]

treatment capacity corresponds to 3500 emptied septic tanks per year. The treatment line consists [SOURCE: Page_4]

of: (i) a stone trap followed by an automatic screening (10 mm mesh), (ii) an emptying tank [SOURCE: Page_4]

(20 m^3), (iii) an aerated buffer tank (capacity of 180 m^3), (iv) 8 sludge treatment reed beds planted [SOURCE: Page_4]

with Phragmites australis (325 m^2 each), (v) 2 vertical flow constructed wetlands (VFCW, 50 m^2 [SOURCE: Page_4]

each) for leachates treatment and (vi) a treated leachate storage basin for irrigation (140 m^3). In fact, [SOURCE: Page_4]

the VFCW stage receives not only leachates but also a part of outlet effluent of a nearby wastewater [SOURCE: Page_4]

treatment plant in order to have enough water volume for irrigation. To prevent clogging of the [SOURCE: Page_4]

installation, the filter layer of the VFCW was composed of a mixture of sand (0/4 mm) and gravel [SOURCE: Page_4]

(2/6.3 mm), with a d^50 of about 2 mm (Table 1). [SOURCE: Page_4]

## CONCLUSION

The results shown in this study were obtained from field monitoring conducted during almost two [SOURCE: Page_12]

years to evaluate the performance of STRB designed and operated for the treatment of the [SOURCE: Page_12]

municipal septage collect of Nègrepelisse (France). The field data thus obtained showed firstly that [SOURCE: Page_12]

the overall removal efficiencies of STRB were satisfactory and stable. Removal rates higher than [SOURCE: Page_12]

90% for COD and SS and a bit lower for KN represented so far a beneficial septage treatment by [SOURCE: Page_12]

STRB, simple and easy to operate. The necessity of a suitable complementary leachate treatment [SOURCE: Page_12]

was however highlighted (before tree irrigation), justifying the presence of the second stage of [SOURCE: Page_12]

VFCW. The sludge deposit drying and mineralization efficiencies were on the right track. [SOURCE: Page_12]

According to hydrotextural diagram analysis, surface deposit was however found to have high [SOURCE: Page_12]

deformability probably due to the youngness of the installation. [SOURCE: Page_12]

As other constructed wetlands, an in-depth understanding of STRB system needs continuous long- [SOURCE: Page_12]

term studies. Further investigations would therefore be worth to evaluate sustainability of the whole [SOURCE: Page_12]

installation. [SOURCE: Page_12]
