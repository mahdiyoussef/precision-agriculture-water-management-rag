Introduction [SOURCE: Page_3]

No-tillage (NT) is an ecological approach of soil management which has a higher [SOURCE: Page_3]

efficiency than conventional tillage (CT) to improve soil quality. However, the response [SOURCE: Page_3]

to NT depends largely on the climatic conditions, mulch quantity on the soil surface and [SOURCE: Page_3]

soil management. Tillage largely influences pore size distribution. Soils under CT [SOURCE: Page_3]

generally have lower bulk density and associated with a higher total porosity within the [SOURCE: Page_3]

ploughed layer than no tillage (Liepic et al. 2006). The relationships between soil pore [SOURCE: Page_3]

structure induced by tillage and infiltration play an important role in water and solutes [SOURCE: Page_3]

transport characteristics in soil. An important function of soil is transmission of water, [SOURCE: Page_3]

which directly affects plant productivity and environment. In NT, greater infiltration [SOURCE: Page_3]

rate was attributed to greater contribution of flow-active macro-pores made by soil [SOURCE: Page_3]

microorganisms, worms, and roots of preceding crops (Lampurlanes and Cantero- [SOURCE: Page_3]

Martinez 2006). As cited by Sasal et al. (2006) such bio-pores are more effective for [SOURCE: Page_3]

water and air movement and root growth, because they are more continuous, less [SOURCE: Page_3]

tortuous, and more stable than macro-pores created during ploughing (Hubbard et al. 2 [SOURCE: Page_3]

2001). However the effects of soil tillage and management on transmission properties [SOURCE: Page_4]

Conclusion [SOURCE: Page_12]

Soil tillage is defined as mechanical or soil-stirring actions exerted on soil to modify [SOURCE: Page_12]

soil conditions for the purpose of nurturing crops. Results of various investigations from [SOURCE: Page_12]

almost all world climatic zones suggest that plowing often reveals common soil-related [SOURCE: Page_12]

problems such as soil compaction, soil erosion, deteriorated water percolation, and high [SOURCE: Page_12]

energy and time requirement (El Titi 2003). As a response to those problems, [SOURCE: Page_12]

conservation tillage, including no-tillage (NT) was proposed. This system has often [SOURCE: Page_12]

higher efficiency than conventional tillage (CT) to improve soil properties. [SOURCE: Page_12]

Modifications of soil structure by soil tillage cause changes in conductivity and [SOURCE: Page_12]

permeability characteristics for water and solute transport in soils and their spatial [SOURCE: Page_12]

distribution. Using tillage resulted in increasing some hydraulic characteristics of [SOURCE: Page_12]

topsoil, however these effects of tillage are short lived. So that in the environmental [SOURCE: Page_12]

point of view there is higher risk of pollution transport in CT system especially after soil [SOURCE: Page_12]

operation. The differences in CT and NT can be related to different soil layer which [SOURCE: Page_12]

exist in the topsoil of NT which can cause a spatial heterogenity of hydraulic properties [SOURCE: Page_12]

of the porous media while tillage has the homogenization effects in the topsoil of CT [SOURCE: Page_12]

system. More study is necessary to better understand the porous media under NT system [SOURCE: Page_12]

especially over the growing season. 11 [SOURCE: Page_12]
