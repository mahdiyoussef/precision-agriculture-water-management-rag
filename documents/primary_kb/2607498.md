## REPRESENTATIVE OF THE SITUATION AROUND THE MEDITERRANEAN RIM

Jordan, France & Tunisia) - both from a practical perspective and from a number of environmental and human [SOURCE: Page_5]

sciences viewpoints - to highlight the diversity of experiences and bottlenecks. For Italy, Jordan, France and [SOURCE: Page_5]

Tunisia, respectively, these experiences are described in four parts: a) Elements of Context & Figures, b) [SOURCE: Page_5]

economic aspects. [SOURCE: Page_5]

a)   A survey of Italian treatment plants estimated the total effluent flow (potentially available for reuse) at [SOURCE: Page_6]

about 2400 Mm$_3$/yr (TYPSA 2013). Nowadays, reclaimed water is used in Italy mainly for agricultural [SOURCE: Page_6]

irrigation, covering over 4,000 ha. Although there is no organized national network of “WW reusers”, [SOURCE: Page_6]

several case studies on the practical implementation of the reuse of TWW in Italy include relevant [SOURCE: Page_6]

applications in both the north and south of the country. In regions like Emilia Romagna (WWTP of [SOURCE: Page_6]

Reggio Emilia), Lombardia (WWTP of Milano-Nosedo) and Piemonte (WWTP of Torino), large [SOURCE: Page_6]

volumes of treated effluent are currently produced and delivered to local users. In southern regions such [SOURCE: Page_6]

as Puglia and Sicily, a number of pilot-scale projects and actual applications have been carried out that [SOURCE: Page_6]

are specifically aimed at compensating for the lack of natural resources typical of Mediterranean areas [SOURCE: Page_6]

(Lopez et al. 2006; Lonigro et al. 2015). [SOURCE: Page_6]

b) The controlled reuse of municipal wastewater in agriculture has not yet been developed in many Italian [SOURCE: Page_6]

regions. Compliance with new Italian standards requires advanced treatment, entailing consequences on [SOURCE: Page_6]

the economic viability of reclamation. Another negative aspect is the plethora of parameters to be [SOURCE: Page_6]

monitored - more than 50 items - that often require high measurement frequencies. Furthermore, no [SOURCE: Page_6]

regulatory distinction has been made between the different crops to be irrigated with reclaimed [SOURCE: Page_6]

wastewater (restricted/unrestricted irrigation) and no attention has been paid to the influence of different [SOURCE: Page_6]

irrigation options in reducing sanitary risks (e.g.: subsurface, drip or sprinkle irrigation). [SOURCE: Page_6]

c)   In coastal areas, treated wastewater reuse is recognized as a possible tool to mitigate groundwater [SOURCE: Page_6]

salinization caused by overexploitation of underground water resources and consequent seawater [SOURCE: Page_6]

intrusion. However, injection of treated effluents into deep aquifers is forbidden in Italy, therefore [SOURCE: Page_6]

groundwater recharge can only be achieved through surface spreading and infiltration systems. [SOURCE: Page_6]

d) Water reuse for irrigation in Italy has been regulated since 1977 by the Water Protection Act (CITAI), [SOURCE: Page_6]

but a new set of regulations was promulgated in 2003 (Decree of the Ministry of Environment 185 [SOURCE: Page_6]

2003), applicable to agriculture, non-potable urban and industrial water reuse. A rather restrictive [SOURCE: Page_6]

approach was adopted such that many quality standards for reclaimed water are the same as for drinking [SOURCE: Page_6]

water. The relevance of nutrient recovery, and the advisability of adopting standards and technologies [SOURCE: Page_6]

tailored to the different reuse applications, have been highlighted as important findings of the recent [SOURCE: Page_6]

pilot experiments (Vergine et al. 2015, 2016). [SOURCE: Page_6]

a)   The scarcity of water in Jordan, which has always already attained one of the highest four levels of [SOURCE: Page_7]

annual water budget that is currently 1027 Mm$_3$. The TWW covers approximately 25% of the irrigation [SOURCE: Page_7]

content is an issue that needs to be addressed at WWTPs that receive a share of industrial WW and [SOURCE: Page_7]

brine from desalination. Farmers mainly use mulch (plastic cover on soil) and drip irrigation to avoid [SOURCE: Page_7]

excessive evaporation and effectively enhance the microbiological quality of the crops as well as [SOURCE: Page_7]
