36     1 INTRODUCTION 37             Reducing the use of pesticides and coping with water scarcity are two of the main challenges in [SOURCE: Page_3]

38   Mediterranean horticulture. The use of less drought susceptible plant varieties and the implementation of regulated [SOURCE: Page_3]

39   deficit irrigation represent possible solutions to decrease water consumption in horticulture. Interestingly, due to [SOURCE: Page_3]

40   cross tolerance mechanisms between abiotic and biotic stresses, the implementation of deficit irrigation can also [SOURCE: Page_3]

41   reduce host plant suitability for pests and especially aphids (Foyer et al., 2016). Host plant suitability for aphids [SOURCE: Page_3]

42   encompasses multi-aspects, which can be modulated by plant water status. The four main ones are (i) nutrition (or [SOURCE: Page_3]

43   settlement) site availability, i.e. the number of growing apices (Forrest and Dixon, 1975), (ii) phloem nutritional [SOURCE: Page_3]

44   quality, e.g. the secondary metabolites (Czerniewicz et al., 2011) and the amino acid profiles (Ryan et al., 2014), Version postprint [SOURCE: Page_3]

45   (iii) phloem accessibility (Mody et al., 2009) and (iv) phyllosphere microenvironment (Pangga et al., 2012). As the [SOURCE: Page_3]

46   effects of water scarcity on host plant characteristics vary with stress timing, intensity, and duration (Tariq et al., [SOURCE: Page_3]

47   2012), the published results on the effect of host plant water stress on aphid performance, are often contradictory. [SOURCE: Page_3]

48   Water stress has been shown to have a positive (Archer et al., 1995; Mewis et al., 2012), a negative (Agele et al., [SOURCE: Page_3]

49   2006; King et al., 2006; Simpson et al., 2012) or no effect (Bethke et al., 1998; King et al., 2006; Mewis et al., [SOURCE: Page_3]

50   2012) on aphid performance. The plant genotype is also a factor that is expected to influence the plant-aphid [SOURCE: Page_3]

51   interaction under water restriction. Yet, the studies evaluating the effects of drought stress on aphid performance on [SOURCE: Page_3]

52   different plant genotypes generally considered genotypes contrasted for their resistance to the insect (Agele et al., [SOURCE: Page_3]

53   2006; Dardeau et al., 2015; Verdugo et al., 2015) rather than genotypes contrasted for their response to drought [SOURCE: Page_3]

54   stress. [SOURCE: Page_3]

55             In the present work, the apple tree-rosy apple aphid system [Malus domestica Borkh. – Dysaphis [SOURCE: Page_3]

56   plantaginea (Passerini)] was chosen as the study case, because apple trees are cultivated worldwide under a wide [SOURCE: Page_3]

57   range of climatic conditions and also in semi-arid areas such as the Mediterranean basin. Moreover apple is the [SOURCE: Page_3]

58   major deciduous fruit tree production worldwide (FAO 2016). Dysaphis plantaginea is a major apple tree pest [SOURCE: Page_3]

59   (Forrest and Dixon, 1975). It causes leaf roll (Forrest and Dixon, 1975), shoot and fruit deformations (Marchetti et [SOURCE: Page_3]

60   al., 2009), and populations resistant to pesticides have already appeared (Delorme et al., 1999). Two apple [SOURCE: Page_3]

62   cross progeny (Lauri et al., 2016). The first genotype (referred to as DAG: Drought Avoidance Genotype) is [SOURCE: Page_4]

68   be modulated by the genotype. Other determinants of plant suitability to aphids, such as leaf water potential and [SOURCE: Page_4]

74          Thus, aphid abundance has been positively correlated to vegetative growth at the shoot scale (Stoeckli et al., [SOURCE: Page_4]

2008; Rousselin et al., 2016). Yet, the existence of a similar relationship at the tree scale remains unclear. Indeed, [SOURCE: Page_4]

77   fruiting and non-fruiting shoots (Simon et al., 2011). This could affect the patterns of aphid dispersion within the [SOURCE: Page_4]

78   crown. We will therefore consider simultaneously two study scales: the infested shoot and the whole tree, thus [SOURCE: Page_4]

257     4 DISCUSSION 258   Our results showed a positive relationship between vegetative growth and aphid abundance whatever the study [SOURCE: Page_10]

259   scale, the genotype or the water supply. At the shoot scale, tree genotype played a role on aphid abundance in [SOURCE: Page_10]

260   interaction with water supply. Thus, the effects of water restriction on the two genotypes were opposite: it [SOURCE: Page_10]

261   increased aphid abundance on DAG and reduced it on GCG. At the tree scale, aphid abundance was penalized by [SOURCE: Page_10]

262   water restriction and the effect of genotype on aphid abundance was less clear. [SOURCE: Page_10]

263        The positive relationship between D. plantaginea abundance and both shoot and tree vegetative growths is [SOURCE: Page_10]

264   consistent with the Plant Vigour Hypothesis that predicts a better performance of phytophagous insects on vigorous [SOURCE: Page_10]

265   plants or organs (Price 1991). Our result, at the shoot scale, is consistent with another study conducted on apple [SOURCE: Page_10]

266   tree, evidencing that Aphis pomi abundance on a current-year shoot was well correlated to the shoot growth but [SOURCE: Page_10]

267   was independent of trunk diameter and median shoot length that are indicators of tree vigour (Stoeckli et al. 2008). [SOURCE: Page_10]

268   Final tree vegetative growth (FTVG) was positively correlated to the percentage of tree leaves inserted on shoot [SOURCE: Page_10]

269   and also to the number of shoots. However, there was no relation between final apical shoot vegetative growth (or [SOURCE: Page_10]
