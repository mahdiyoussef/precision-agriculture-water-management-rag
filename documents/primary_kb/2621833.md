Stable isotopes are frequently used as environmental or artificial tracers to study water flow, transport, and biogeochemical processes in soils and the unsaturated zone. In recent years, new analytical and technical developments have transformed the field of isotope hydrology and isotope biogeochemistry and widened the scope of questions that can now be tackled. In particular, it is now possible to measure and parameterize isotope dynamics within soils at increasingly finer temporal resolution or at increas- ingly larger scales. At the same time, smaller sample volumes are required for stable isotope analysis, and the cost of analysis itself has become much cheaper with new laser technologies relative to isotope ratio mass spectrometry in the past. In addition, new automated systems that can provide high-resolution in situ measurements and real-time data analysis have now been developed. These technological breakthroughs are thus poised to improve our understanding and the modeling of water and matter fluxes in the atmosphere–plant–soil–groundwater continuum.
