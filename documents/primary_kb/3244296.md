With this position paper, the IWA Task Group (TG) on Membrane Bioreactor 1   Modelling and Control aims at establishing a next step towards standardised MBR 2   modelling. This paper will mainly focus on so-called integrated MBR models which 3 4   jointly take into account biological and physical (membrane filtration) processes. 5   Modelling of the latter is often accomplished by resistance-in-series (RIS) models for 6   membrane fouling. 7 8   Building upon previous and recent literature reviews (Chang et al., 2009; Di Bella and 9   Di Trapani, 2019; Hamedi et al., 2019; Naessens et al., 2012a, 2012b) a brief 10   summary and update is given to identify current trends in MBR modelling with 11 special regard to integrated MBR models and the temporal and spatial scale of 12 13   modelling applications in research and engineering. 14     In modelling of biological wastewater treatment processes issues with ambiguous 15 16   terminologies and nomenclature have been addressed previously (Corominas et al., 17   2010; Rieger et al., 2013). It is examined which of these issues persist in the used 18   MBR models. Based upon the approach of Rieger et al. (2013) a way to provide a 19   common and unambiguous terminology for variables, parameters and processes is 20 21 proposed. 22 23   Updated Literature Review 24 25   Physico-chemical or mechanical unit operations. Various computational models 26   have been used to describe and master (physico-chemical or mechanical) unit 27 28 processes of MBR operations under dynamic conditions. Simple mechanistic 29   approaches have been used to model energy consumption of MBRs based on heuristic 30   rules and models on pumping and aeration energy (Verrecht et al., 2008). Although 31   they can provide information on various design options, these models generally do not 32   predict filtration performance based on membrane fouling. 33 34   Biodegradation. Activated sludge models (ASM) are well established and widely 35   used (Langergraber et al., 2004; Rieger et al., 2013) and have been applied to simulate 36 37   biomass kinetics in MBR systems (Fenu et al., 2010). Additional sub-processes or 38   complementary models on different or additional biological pathways can be 39   implemented to describe e.g. greenhouse-gas (GHG) emissions (Mannina et al., 2018; 40   Massara et al., 2018; Wisniewski et al., 2018) and energy consumption (Grau et al., 41 2007). ASMs have also been modified to include the presence and fate of Soluble 42 43   Microbial Products (SMPs) which allegedly play an important role in membrane 44   fouling, in so-called hybrid ASM models (Zuthi et al., 2012). Hybrid ASM models 45   could also be used to model the fate of extracellular polymeric substances (EPS) or 46   diluted organic matter. 47 48   Filtration. Different MBR models have been focusing on the physical aspects of the 49   fouling process by various methods with the aim of describing several processes 50 51 involved in membrane fouling. Among them, mathematical models are the most 52   widely developed which include empirical hydrodynamic models, conventional mass 53   transfer and tangential filtration models; fractal permeation models, sectional 54   resistance models and RIS Models (Chang et al., 2009; Naessens et al., 2012a; Ng and 55   Kim, 2007). 56 57     Regarding the number of publications, RIS models seem to be highly popular. 58   Based on an application of Darcy’s law non-stationary mathematical equations are 59 60   used to describe the total hydraulic resistance.
