7     Abstract [SOURCE: Page_1]

8     Oxygen management in wine is one of most challenging issue for winemakers. Indeed, an [SOURCE: Page_1]

9     excess of dissolved oxygen (O$_2$) can prematurely age wine and leads to organoleptic [SOURCE: Page_1]

10     deviation. [SOURCE: Page_1]

11     Membrane contactors composed of hydrophobic hollow fiber are becoming a relevant [SOURCE: Page_1]

12     technology especially for gas-liquid mass transfer operations. Nevertheless, their utilization in [SOURCE: Page_1]

13     an oenological context to manage oxygen in wine has not been comprehensively studied. [SOURCE: Page_1]

14     Fouling phenomena could be problematic and may limit their utilization during winemaking. [SOURCE: Page_1]

15     The aim of this study is to develop (i) a simple and rapid method to characterize membrane [SOURCE: Page_1]

16     contactors and (ii) to investigate the effects of two types of membrane contactor composed [SOURCE: Page_1]

17     either of polypropylene (PP) or of PP with a dense coat layer (Teflon). The specific [SOURCE: Page_1]

18     methodology proposed using volumetric transfer coefficient (KL.a) is used to investigate the [SOURCE: Page_1]

19     performance of the membranes on gas transfer in terms of wetting and fouling, as well as the [SOURCE: Page_1]

20     effect of the membrane material. After liquid flow rate optimization, which was necessary to [SOURCE: Page_1]

21     determine a suitable value (300 mL.min-1), the membrane contactors’ performances were [SOURCE: Page_1]

22     characterized with water, a wine model solution and two red wines with different fouling [SOURCE: Page_1]

23     index. After several hydrodynamic optimization operations, we consider that the membrane [SOURCE: Page_1]

24     contactors studied could remove oxygen while carbon dioxide is dissolved with KL.aCO$_2$ close [SOURCE: Page_1]

25     to 20 ×10-5 s-1 for both prototypes. The addition of a dense coat layer in an oenological context [SOURCE: Page_1]

26     may be necessary for a non-prepared wine with a high fouling index. [SOURCE: Page_1]

57   1. Introduction [SOURCE: Page_3]

58   Bottling is the last but not the least important step in winemaking. One of the most important [SOURCE: Page_3]

59   objectives is to limit the introduction of dissolved oxygen during this step (<0.5 mg.L-1). This [SOURCE: Page_3]

60   condition is a primary factor ensuring wine conservation [1]. Meanwhile, since the perception [SOURCE: Page_3]

61   of carbonation in the mouth contributes significantly to the quality of white and rosé wines [SOURCE: Page_3]

62   [2], a level of dissolved carbon dioxide (CO$_2$) is also desired, and varies from 0.6 g.L-1 to [SOURCE: Page_3]

63   1.5 g.L-1 according to the type of wine. Saturation levels of oxygen in wines are equal to [SOURCE: Page_3]

64   8.4 mg.L-1 at 20°C. The presence of dissolved carbon dioxide in wine strongly affects the [SOURCE: Page_3]

65   efficiency of oxygen transfer [3,4]. [SOURCE: Page_3]

66   The use of membrane contactors has many applications in different industries, such as power [SOURCE: Page_3]

67   plants, pharmaceuticals, oil extraction, etc. [5] [6] [7]. In a gas-liquid membrane contactor, [SOURCE: Page_3]

68   the gas stream and the liquid solution flow on different sides of the membrane; the gas can be [SOURCE: Page_3]

69   diffused through the microporous membrane and be absorbed by the liquid phase. The [SOURCE: Page_3]

70   membrane is used as an interfacial barrier between the gas and liquid phase and could allow [SOURCE: Page_3]

71   the regulation of oxygen and carbon dioxide in the wine industry. Membrane contactors offer [SOURCE: Page_3]

72   several advantages, such as a high surface area per unit volume and high compactness. Their [SOURCE: Page_3]

73   compact structure provides good flexibility for scaling up and they could replace traditional [SOURCE: Page_3]

74   techniques for gas management in the wine sector. The removal of dissolved oxygen in wine [SOURCE: Page_3]

75   can be achieved with either physical methods or chemical methods. To limit the impact of [SOURCE: Page_3]

76   oxygen consumption during winemaking, sulfur dioxide (SO$_2$) is often used for the chemical [SOURCE: Page_3]

77   reduction process. However, the legislation tends to limit the use of SO$_2$ and nitrogen (N$_2$), [SOURCE: Page_3]

3 [SOURCE: Page_3]

78   and bubbling is now largely employed among physical treatments. Indeed, a two-step nitrogen [SOURCE: Page_4]

79   bubbling treatment for O$_2$ removal and CO$_2$ addition is possible to obtain a bottled wine with [SOURCE: Page_4]

80   the desired levels of dissolved O$_2$ and CO$_2$ [8]. The big disadvantages of this process are the [SOURCE: Page_4]

81   high gas consumption (N$_2$ and CO$_2$), the time required and the difficulty of obtaining a precise [SOURCE: Page_4]

82   level of CO$_2$. Recently, membrane contactors have attracted the attention of winemaking [SOURCE: Page_4]

83   process developers and could be a useful device to achieve gas/liquid mass transfers, but [SOURCE: Page_4]

84   fouling phenomena are still a problem that limit their use [9]. These processes are already [SOURCE: Page_4]

85   used in several applications and also offer a numbers of application possibilities for the wine [SOURCE: Page_4]

86   industry: partial dealcoholization [10] [11] and micro-oxygenation during aging [12] [13]. By [SOURCE: Page_4]

87   using membrane contactors, O$_2$ and CO$_2$ concentrations can be adjusted to the precise desired [SOURCE: Page_4]

88   levels in one step before bottling. Besides, some studies have shown that after membrane [SOURCE: Page_4]

89   contactor treatment, alcohol percentage, volatile acidity, pH values, free and total SO$_2$ data [SOURCE: Page_4]

90   and absorbance values at 420, 520 and 620 nm are not modified [14]. Gas chromatography [SOURCE: Page_4]

91   analysis of higher alcohols and esters of wine show that only some esters undergo significant [SOURCE: Page_4]

92   losses of up to 40 %, especially ethyl octanoate, ethyl hexanoate, isoamyl acetate and hexyl [SOURCE: Page_4]

93   acetate, which are the most volatile molecules and which thus have the most affinity for the [SOURCE: Page_4]

94   membrane passage. Comparatively, deoxygenation of wines by nitrogen sparging with a [SOURCE: Page_4]

95   porous diffuser causes large losses of the same molecules because of their volatility. During [SOURCE: Page_4]

96   triangular test tasting, no significant difference was revealed in the α risk of 5 % for the wine [SOURCE: Page_4]

97   (white and red) before and after deoxygenation with membrane contactor, in spite of the ester [SOURCE: Page_4]

98   losses cited above [14]. [SOURCE: Page_4]

99   However, in spite of its big advantages, this membrane technology suffers from two frequent [SOURCE: Page_4]

100   problems during long-term operation: membrane wetting and membrane fouling [15] [16] [SOURCE: Page_4]

101   [17]. The wetting of membrane pores significantly impacts mass transfer coefficients in the [SOURCE: Page_4]

102   membrane module, leading to a sharp increase in membrane resistance and a rapid decline in [SOURCE: Page_4]

4 [SOURCE: Page_4]

103   absorption performance [18]. It has been demonstrated that even a marginal wetting (<2 %) of [SOURCE: Page_5]

104   the pores could result in an increase in membrane resistance as high as 60 % of the total mass- [SOURCE: Page_5]

105   transfer resistance [19]. Membrane fouling could be a bigger problem with pressure-driven [SOURCE: Page_5]

106   devices than with concentration-driven ones such as membrane contactors [9]. Several anti- [SOURCE: Page_5]

107   wetting and anti-fouling modifications are possible: amelioration of hydrodynamic factors [SOURCE: Page_5]

108   based on the traditional mass transfer theories [20], utilization of a high hydrophobicity [SOURCE: Page_5]

109   membrane, hydrophobic modification of the membrane surface, or utilization of a composite [SOURCE: Page_5]

110   membrane [17] [5]. [SOURCE: Page_5]

111   In an oenological context, the matrix of wine with 12-15 % alcohol could also contain [SOURCE: Page_5]

112   different fouling agents such as polysaccharides, polyphenols and proteins [21] [22]. [SOURCE: Page_5]

113   These compounds are widely recognized as contributing to the rapid fouling of classical filter [SOURCE: Page_5]

114   media (ceramic membrane, polyethersulfone membrane, polysulfone membrane, pad filter, [SOURCE: Page_5]

115   etc.). Thus, the frequency and ease of cleaning and the cost of periodic membrane [SOURCE: Page_5]

116   replacement should be taken into account in the application of this technology. In other [SOURCE: Page_5]

117   applications and to avoid these limiting phenomena, a composite hollow fiber with a [SOURCE: Page_5]

118   protective dense layer has displayed high anti-wetting and anti-fouling performances in other [SOURCE: Page_5]

119   industries and could be investigated [5]. The objective of this study is to develop a new, rapid [SOURCE: Page_5]

120   membrane contactor characterization method in order to compare the performances of [SOURCE: Page_5]

121   selected hollow fibers with and without a protective dense layer for final utilization in an [SOURCE: Page_5]

122   oenological context. For the comparison, the development of a novel methodology that [SOURCE: Page_5]

123   characterizes membrane contactors is proposed. The membrane contactor material is then [SOURCE: Page_5]

124   investigated. The membranes are composed of polypropylene (PP) or PP coated with Teflon [SOURCE: Page_5]

125   AF2400 (PPT). Teflon AF2400 is a copolymer of tetrafluoroethylene and 2,2- [SOURCE: Page_5]

126   bis(trifluoromethyl)-4,5-difluoro-1,3-dioxole, used as membrane materials. These materials [SOURCE: Page_5]

127   are similar to other amorphous polymers in terms of optical clarity and mechanical properties, [SOURCE: Page_5]

5 [SOURCE: Page_5]

128   including strength. They also resemble fluoropolymers in their performance over a wide range [SOURCE: Page_6]

129   of temperatures, and have outstanding electrical properties and chemical resistance. They are [SOURCE: Page_6]

130   distinct from other fluoropolymers in that they are soluble in selected solvents and have high [SOURCE: Page_6]

131   gas permeability, high compressibility, high creep resistance, and low thermal conductivity. [SOURCE: Page_6]

132   The hydrophobicity of PP and PPT is different (water contact angle respectively 102° and [SOURCE: Page_6]

133   109°) and could optimize the gas transfer rate in an oenological application [23]. [SOURCE: Page_6]

134   In this research, a specific methodology using KL.a is used to investigate the effect of wetting [SOURCE: Page_6]

135   and fouling on the gas transfer efficiency of the two membrane materials. The mass transfer [SOURCE: Page_6]

136   capacity, the cycle time and fouling are studied. [SOURCE: Page_6]

272   3. Results and discussion [SOURCE: Page_12]

273   3.1.    Optimization of liquid flow rate [SOURCE: Page_12]

274   We noted that an optimization of the liquid flow rate is necessary in order to obtain an [SOURCE: Page_12]

275   optimal gas transfer according to the membrane contactors tested. The liquid flow rate needs [SOURCE: Page_12]

276   to be adjusted as a function of the contact area of the membrane contactor cartridge. In this [SOURCE: Page_12]

277   part, we optimized the liquid flow rate in order achieve optimal hydrodynamic conditions [SOURCE: Page_12]

278   ensuring gas exchange over the entire membrane contactor surface. [SOURCE: Page_12]

279   In our experimental setup, the membrane contactor response must come within an acceptable [SOURCE: Page_12]

280   time lapse to ensure acceptance by end users (under 4 hours). Both 150 mL.min-1 and [SOURCE: Page_12]

281   300 mL.min-1 flow rates were investigated and the liquid entered the shell side in both [SOURCE: Page_12]

282   membrane contactors. The gas flow rate was adjusted to 120 mL.min-1. The gas concentration [SOURCE: Page_12]

283   (O$_2$ and CO$_2$) was measured at the inlet and the outlet, and the purity was constant. No [SOURCE: Page_12]

284   significant difference was noted and repeatability was ensured within this flow rate. The PPT [SOURCE: Page_12]

285   coating features on the outside of the membrane and the comparison should be made using [SOURCE: Page_12]

286   this configuration. With this methodology, the main difference will concern the material [SOURCE: Page_12]

287   structure, and thus membrane contactor performances can be discussed. [SOURCE: Page_12]

293   and around 58 % for the PPT. Conversely, a liquid flow rate set at 150 mL.min-1 only [SOURCE: Page_12]

295   experiment, for both membrane contactors, the final O$_2$ concentration was close to 0.2 mg.L-1 [SOURCE: Page_12]

296   at a liquid flow rate of 300 mL.min-1 against a final O$_2$ concentration of around 0.65 mg.L-1 at [SOURCE: Page_13]

313   3.2.    Hydrodynamic characterization [SOURCE: Page_13]

314   The liquid flow rate is important but must be linked with the retention time distribution. Each [SOURCE: Page_13]

315   membrane contactor has a specific geometry (volume, compactness etc.) and the contact times [SOURCE: Page_13]

316   between the membrane surface and the liquid should be comparable. Hydrodynamic [SOURCE: Page_13]

317   characterization according to residence time distribution (RTD) is presented in Fig. 4. We [SOURCE: Page_13]

318   note that the experimental RTD is higher than the theoretical RTD for both membrane [SOURCE: Page_13]

319   contactors. Additionally, the experimental RTD of the PPT prototype is five times higher than [SOURCE: Page_13]

320   the commercial PP module. This result reflects a hydrodynamic difference between the [SOURCE: Page_13]

13 [SOURCE: Page_13]

321   membrane contactors in the presence of dead volume in the PPT prototype due to its shape, as [SOURCE: Page_14]

322   we can see in Fig. 2. However, the experimental RTD is under 60 sec for both membrane [SOURCE: Page_14]

323   contactors, which is negligible in relation to the time of the experiment (5 hours) and the [SOURCE: Page_14]

324   standard deviation are low which indicates a good repeatability of the experimental setup. [SOURCE: Page_14]

325   This dead volume of the PPT membrane contactor could impact upon gas transfer capacities if [SOURCE: Page_14]

326   there is a contact with the membrane. Nevertheless, in Fig. 3 we note that there is no clear [SOURCE: Page_14]

327   difference in gas transfer between the two modules at a liquid flow rate set at 300 mL.min-1. [SOURCE: Page_14]

328   By using these operating parameters, it is possible to evaluate the two membrane contactors in [SOURCE: Page_14]

329   terms of gas transfer performance. [SOURCE: Page_14]

330   3.3.    Initial characterization of gas transfer capacities of membrane contactors [SOURCE: Page_14]

331   The initial characterization of the gas transfer capacities of the two membrane contactors with [SOURCE: Page_14]

332   ultra-pure water shows that there is no significant difference (Fig. 5). Indeed, the carbonation [SOURCE: Page_14]

333   of pure water reaches a stationary phase after 150 min for both membranes. Regarding [SOURCE: Page_14]

334   deoxygenation, we noted a similar behavior by both types of membrane. After 300 min, O$_2$ [SOURCE: Page_14]

335   elimination reaches 86 % and 93 % of the total amount for the PP and PPT membrane [SOURCE: Page_14]

336   contactors, respectively. Finally, the mean volumetric transfer coefficient KL.aCO$_2$ for the PP [SOURCE: Page_14]

337   module is 21.9 ±0.7 ×10-5 s-1 and 20.3 ±1.6 ×10-5 s-1 for the PPT prototype. Both membrane [SOURCE: Page_14]

338   contactors, PP and PPT, have the same gas transfer capacity with water. This initial [SOURCE: Page_14]

339   characterization is very important because it corresponds to the initial gas transfer [SOURCE: Page_14]

340   performance. It could be determined in order to control whether membrane regeneration is [SOURCE: Page_14]

341   correctly performed after a wine treatment. [SOURCE: Page_14]

342   3.4.    Wettability and fouling study [SOURCE: Page_14]

343   The wettability resistance of both types of membrane contactor using a wine model solution [SOURCE: Page_14]

344   was evaluated over time with the evolution of the volumetric transfer coefficient KL.aCO$_2$. The [SOURCE: Page_14]

14 [SOURCE: Page_14]

345   phenomenon of membrane wetting in gas-liquid membrane contactors involves liquid input [SOURCE: Page_15]

346   into the pores of porous membranes and substantially affects gas transfer capacities [29]. If [SOURCE: Page_15]

347   membranes are wetted, the gas to be absorbed has to diffuse through the stagnant liquid [SOURCE: Page_15]

348   insides pores and thus the overall mass-transfer coefficient will be reduce (about 20%) even if [SOURCE: Page_15]

349   membranes pores are wetted at 5 % [30]. [SOURCE: Page_15]

350   Table 3 shows that the circulation of the wine model solution has no impact on gas transfer [SOURCE: Page_15]

351   capacities for either type of membrane. Indeed, the initial KL.aCO$_2$ is 20 ×10-5 s-1 for PP and [SOURCE: Page_15]

352   19 ×10-5 s-1 for PPT, then after 12 days of wine model solution circulation in the PP module, [SOURCE: Page_15]

353   the KL.aCO$_2$ is 22 ×10-5 s-1 and is 21.6 ×10-5 s-1 after 16 days for PPT. Therefore, there is no [SOURCE: Page_15]

354   influence of the ethanol content (12 % v/v) of the wine model solution has no influence on [SOURCE: Page_15]

355   membrane wettability in our running conditions because we didn’t notice a reduction of KL.a [SOURCE: Page_15]

356   after 12 or 16 days compared to the initial values with water. In fact, water and solutions of [SOURCE: Page_15]

357   inorganic substances in water have such high surface tension values (γL > 72 ×10-3 N.m-1) that [SOURCE: Page_15]

358   for a number of hydrophobic microporous membranes with pores in the range of 1 pm or less, [SOURCE: Page_15]

359   such as polypropylene (PP) and polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE, Teflon), this non-wetting [SOURCE: Page_15]

360   condition is guaranteed. Nevertheless, ethanol has a low surface tension (γL= 30 ×10-3 N.m-1) [SOURCE: Page_15]

361   [31], which can easily wet the hydrophobic pores, and performances could be changed. In our [SOURCE: Page_15]

362   case, the impact of ethanol concentration on gas transfer capacities in membrane contactors in [SOURCE: Page_15]

363   an oenological context (wine model solution) did not emerge. Some authors proved that [SOURCE: Page_15]

364   contact angles decrease with alcohol concentration and hence with the surface tension of the [SOURCE: Page_15]

365   liquid [32]. In general, straight-line relation between the cosine of angle θ, and liquid surface [SOURCE: Page_15]

366   tension is observed even for the liquids having different chemical structure. As shown in table [SOURCE: Page_15]

367   3, the mass transfer coefficient obtained in both case are very similar. These results can be [SOURCE: Page_15]

368   explained by similar wetting of the membrane. The very low pressure applied (0.05 bar) is [SOURCE: Page_15]

369   another parameter that could explain the very low wetting of the membrane. [SOURCE: Page_15]

15 [SOURCE: Page_15]

370   The fouling of the membrane contactors by two different red wines (with high and low [SOURCE: Page_16]

371   fouling indices) was also assessed over time. In Table 4, we can see that the PP and PPT [SOURCE: Page_16]

372   contactors have a constant KL.aCO$_2$ for 8 days in the wine with FI < 20 (filtration on [SOURCE: Page_16]

373   membrane). However, when the wine has a higher FI index, the KL.aCO$_2$ is quite different for [SOURCE: Page_16]

374   the PP and PPT contactors (Table 5). We note that a significant difference appears after 1 day [SOURCE: Page_16]

375   of treatment and a lower gas transfer capacity is observed with the PP membrane contactor [SOURCE: Page_16]

376   (25 % lower than the PPT material). The same difference in percentage of efficiency is [SOURCE: Page_16]

377   maintained after 4 days of treatment. The fouling agent was not determined in this study, but [SOURCE: Page_16]

378   several hypotheses could be put forward. We could consider that components of red wine like [SOURCE: Page_16]

379   polyphenols especially tannins (1.76 g.L-1), have no effect on gas transfer capacity for either [SOURCE: Page_16]

380   type of membrane, because the concentration is relatively high in both cases. Thus, we do not [SOURCE: Page_16]

381   assume any adsorption phenomena between polyphenols and membrane materials, unlike El [SOURCE: Page_16]

382   Rayess et al (2011) [21]. In their research, these authors consider that, according to their [SOURCE: Page_16]

383   increasing effect on permeate flux decline, the most significant fouling agents in wine could [SOURCE: Page_16]

384   be ranked as mannoproteins < tannins < pectins in the case of filtration with a ceramic [SOURCE: Page_16]

385   membrane [22]. In our case, we could assume that other compounds contribute to the fouling, [SOURCE: Page_16]

386   such as mannoproteins or pectins. These compounds were not considered and could explain [SOURCE: Page_16]

387   the difference between the two membranes in terms of gas transfer capacity. We also observe [SOURCE: Page_16]

388   that if the wine is well prepared before bottling (meaning that FI is lower than 20), no [SOURCE: Page_16]

389   polarization – concentration or clogging phenomena appear. This confirms our supposition [SOURCE: Page_16]

390   about the potential fouling agent involved. Finally, after a final cleaning operation and an [SOURCE: Page_16]

391   initial characterization with ultra-pure water, we obtain the same KL.aCO$_2$ value as previously, [SOURCE: Page_16]

392   and easily reach values higher than 20 ×10-5 s-1 for the PP and PPT membranes. This means [SOURCE: Page_16]

393   that no irreversible clogging occurs and the membrane regeneration could be performed using [SOURCE: Page_16]

394   classical techniques. [SOURCE: Page_16]

16 [SOURCE: Page_16]

395   Finally, using a dense layer (Teflon) on a hydrophobic membrane like PP could be an [SOURCE: Page_17]

396   efficient way to limit fouling phenomena for problematic or unprepared wines [6]. Indeed, the [SOURCE: Page_17]

397   difference in degree of water contact angle (7°) between PP and PPT materials could permit a [SOURCE: Page_17]

398   significant improvement in gas transfer capacity using PPT membrane contactors with a high [SOURCE: Page_17]

399   contact surface. [SOURCE: Page_17]

401   4. Conclusions [SOURCE: Page_17]

402   In this study, we developed a simple method using the KL.a approach to compare the [SOURCE: Page_17]

403   efficiency of membrane contactors in managing oxygen in an oenological context. We [SOURCE: Page_17]

404   highlighted that the liquid flow rate should be adapted to improve gas transfer. Moreover, the [SOURCE: Page_17]

405   utilization of a matrix like a wine model solution and red wine serves to investigate [SOURCE: Page_17]

406   membrane behavior in terms of gas transfer with wetting and fouling. [SOURCE: Page_17]

407   This methodology could be used to study various hydrophobic hollow fiber membrane types [SOURCE: Page_17]

408   (PP and PTFE) or the addition of a dense layer and their efficiency according to lifespan, gas [SOURCE: Page_17]

409   transfer capacities, and cleaning ability. Additionally, membrane contactors clearly offer a [SOURCE: Page_17]

410   way to remove oxygen that has previously been incorporated during winemaking, and to [SOURCE: Page_17]

411   incorporate CO$_2$. We proved that our methodology can be used to compare membrane [SOURCE: Page_17]

412   performances. The influence of coating with Teflon has been demonstrated. Indeed, our [SOURCE: Page_17]

413   results prove that wettability is limited for both hydrophobic membranes even if the solution [SOURCE: Page_17]

414   used contains alcohol, which decreases the surface tension value. We can also suppose that [SOURCE: Page_17]

415   fouling agents are not necessarily tannins but may be other compounds such as pectins or [SOURCE: Page_17]

416   mannoproteins. We also demonstrate that no irreversible fouling occurs with either wine [SOURCE: Page_17]

417   treated (IF < 20 or otherwise), because the initial KL.aCO$_2$ value (close to 20 ×10-5s-1) is easily [SOURCE: Page_17]

418   reached after classical regeneration. [SOURCE: Page_17]

17 [SOURCE: Page_17]

419   However, further research should be carried out with several other wines, with a longer [SOURCE: Page_18]

420   running time and with a bigger effective surface area. [SOURCE: Page_18]
