## 21   ABSTRACT

22   Although irrigation water is frequently assessed for the presence of plant pathogens, large spatial [SOURCE: Page_2]

23   and temporal surveys that provide clues on the diversity and circulation of pathogens is missing. [SOURCE: Page_2]

24   We evaluate the diversity of soft rot Pectobacteriaceae (SRP) of the genera Dickeya and [SOURCE: Page_2]

25   Pectobacterium over two years in a temperate, mixed use watershed. The abundance of isolated [SOURCE: Page_2]

26   strains correlates with the agricultural gradient along the watershed with a positive correlation found [SOURCE: Page_2]

27   with temperature, nitrate and dissolved organic carbon water concentration. We characterized 582 [SOURCE: Page_2]

28   strains by amplification and sequencing of the gapA gene. MLSA analysis performed with 3 [SOURCE: Page_2]

29   housekeeping genes for 99 strains and core genome analysis of 38 sequenced strains confirmed for [SOURCE: Page_2]

30   all the strains but one the taxonomic assignation obtained with the sole gapA sequence. [SOURCE: Page_2]

31   Pectobacterium spp. (549 isolates) were far more abundant than Dickeya spp. (33 isolates). Dickeya [SOURCE: Page_2]

32   spp. were only observed in the lower part of the river when water temperature was above 19°C and [SOURCE: Page_2]

33   we experimentally confirmed a decreased fitness of several Dickeya spp. at 8°C in river water. D. [SOURCE: Page_2]

34   oryzae dominates the Dickeya spp. P. versatile and P. aquaticum dominate the Pectobacterium spp. [SOURCE: Page_2]

35   but their repartition along the watershed was different, P. versatile being the only species regularly [SOURCE: Page_2]

36   recovered all along the watershed. Excepting P. versatile, Dickeya and Pectobacterium spp. [SOURCE: Page_2]

37   responsible for disease outbreak on crops were less abundant or rarely detected. This work sheds [SOURCE: Page_2]

38   light on the various ecological behaviours of different SRP in stream water and indicates that SRP [SOURCE: Page_2]

39   occupation is geographically structured. [SOURCE: Page_2]

## 41   INTRODUCTION

42   Soft rot Pectobacteriaceae (SRP) belonging to the Dickeya, Pectobacterium and Musicola genera [SOURCE: Page_3]

43   are plant pathogenic bacteria that collectively infect a wide range of plant species, infecting at least [SOURCE: Page_3]

44   35 % of angiosperm plant orders all over the world (Ma et al. 2007; Charkowski 2018; Portier et al. [SOURCE: Page_3]

45   2020; Hugouvieux-Cotte-Pattat et al. 2021). The virulence of SRP relies mainly on the secretion of [SOURCE: Page_3]

46   cell wall degrading enzymes (PCWDE) provoking maceration symptoms (Charkowski 2018; [SOURCE: Page_3]

47   Hugouvieux-Cotte-Pattat et al. 2014). Latent infections, where the pathogen is present on the plant [SOURCE: Page_3]

48   in the absence of symptoms, are common, symptoms being expressed only when environmental [SOURCE: Page_3]

49   conditions are conducive (Toth et al. 2021a) and the main route of infection and dissemination [SOURCE: Page_3]

50   occurs via latently contaminated plant material. However, environmental sources of contamination [SOURCE: Page_3]

51   also play an important role and it has been demonstrated on the potato host that axenically grown [SOURCE: Page_3]

52   seed stocks, when planted on the field could rapidly become contaminated when the environmental [SOURCE: Page_3]

53   conditions are conducive (Van Gijsegem et al. 2021). [SOURCE: Page_3]

54   Plants can become contaminated with SRP from a variety of environmental sources including [SOURCE: Page_3]

55   insects, soil, aerosols, water or rainwater (Toth et al. 2021a). Identifying the major source(s) of these [SOURCE: Page_3]

56   primary infections is complex and has still not been fully achieved. Particular attention has been [SOURCE: Page_3]

57   paid to surface water that could serve for irrigation purposes. SRP are rare in surface water and [SOURCE: Page_3]

58   neither Pectobacterium genus or Dickeya genus are detected in 16S metabarcoding studies [SOURCE: Page_3]

59   performed on fresh water (Pédron et al. 2020). However, the development of an efficient semi [SOURCE: Page_3]

60   selective medium (Burr and Schroth 1977; Hélias et al. 2012) means SRP can be isolated from fresh [SOURCE: Page_3]

61   water and early reports observed the frequent contamination of surface water by SRP and the [SOURCE: Page_3]

62   potential contamination of plants via water reservoirs (Cappaert et al. 1988; Gudmestad and Secor [SOURCE: Page_3]

63   1983; McCarter‐Zorner et al. 1984; Peltzer and Sivasithamparam 1988; Franc G.D. and Harrison [SOURCE: Page_3]

64   M.D. 1987). Serological analysis performed in these early works identified up to 21 serogroups, and [SOURCE: Page_3]

65   a significant proportion of the isolates did not belong to known serogroups, pointing out the wide [SOURCE: Page_3]

## 312   DISCUSSION

313           This work represents a comprehensive view of SRP dissemination and diversity at the scale [SOURCE: Page_13]

314   of a large watershed covering a surface of 14 280 km². This watershed was interesting to follow [SOURCE: Page_13]

315   bacterial plant pathogens such as SRP because its runs along 323 km from an alpine area devoted [SOURCE: Page_13]

13 [SOURCE: Page_13]

Hajar Ben Moussa Phytopathology. 2022 Feb 27. doi: 10.1094/PHYTO-12-21-0515-R. [SOURCE: Page_14]

316   to pastoralism and hiking to the agricultural plain of Avignon where various crop species are [SOURCE: Page_14]

317   cultivated. Therefore, following distribution and diversity of SRP along this watershed over more [SOURCE: Page_14]

318   than two years along the four seasons helped to differentiate species that could be isolated across [SOURCE: Page_14]

319   the four seasons, those that are found in pristine areas and those that are more associated with crop [SOURCE: Page_14]

320   culture. Characterization of the sampled water confirmed the increasing importance of agriculture [SOURCE: Page_14]

321   along the watershed as nitrite, nitrate and DOC negatively correlates with altitude of the sampled [SOURCE: Page_14]

322   water. Water temperature from the top to the bottom was also an interesting parameter to follow as [SOURCE: Page_14]

323   it varied from 0.3°C to 25.6°C with a mean of 10.8°C. [SOURCE: Page_14]

324          Our study showed that SRP were rare in water. This is in agreement with Pédron et al (Pédron [SOURCE: Page_14]

325   et al, 2020) that previously showed that SRP are not detected through metabarcoding in river water [SOURCE: Page_14]

326   although they are detected when an efficient semi-selective medium is used. This indicates that SRP [SOURCE: Page_14]

327   are not indigenous planktonic species in surface water and are only sporadically passing by in water. [SOURCE: Page_14]

328   The biological continuity between soil and stream microbial communities via surface water runoff [SOURCE: Page_14]

329   has been shown elsewhere (Le et al. 2022) and this link probably explains the low and sporadic [SOURCE: Page_14]

330   prevalence. [SOURCE: Page_14]

331          Our phylogenetic analysis, through the sequencing of the single gapA housekeeping gene [SOURCE: Page_14]

332   proved efficient for roughly classifying 582 of the recovered strains within 15 SRP species. This [SOURCE: Page_14]

333   classification was congruent with that performed with 3 housekeeping genes for 99 strains for all [SOURCE: Page_14]

334   the analysed strains but one. Full genome analysis of 38 strains also confirmed the species [SOURCE: Page_14]

335   assignation based on the sole gapA gene. This indicates that sequencing the housekeeping gene [SOURCE: Page_14]

336   gapA gene is an efficient tool to rapidly classify large sets of strains within Pectobacterium and [SOURCE: Page_14]

337   Dickeya genera. [SOURCE: Page_14]

338          No strain of the recently described Musicola genus were observed during our survey [SOURCE: Page_14]

339   (Hugouvieux-Cotte-Pattat et al. 2021). This new genus was described following an in depth genomic [SOURCE: Page_14]

340   analysis showing that genomes of the formerly Dickeya paradisiaca species aligned with genomes [SOURCE: Page_14]

14 [SOURCE: Page_14]

Hajar Ben Moussa Phytopathology. 2022 Feb 27. doi: 10.1094/PHYTO-12-21-0515-R. [SOURCE: Page_15]

341   of other Dickeya species on less than one third of their genomes. The newly created genus was [SOURCE: Page_15]

342   named Musicola with reference to the isolation of most strains from Musa paradisiaca. The fact [SOURCE: Page_15]

343   that members of Musicola paradisiaca species were mostly isolated from tropical and subtropical [SOURCE: Page_15]

344   samples may explain their absence in a temperate watershed such as the one studied here. [SOURCE: Page_15]

345          We found that bacteria belonging to the Dickeya genus were restricted to the lower part of the [SOURCE: Page_15]

346   watershed and were only isolated during spring and summer when temperature was superior to 19.5°C [SOURCE: Page_15]

347   and we experimentally confirmed the difficulty of several Dickeya spp. to grow and survive at low [SOURCE: Page_15]

348   temperature in river water. This is in line with a previous survey from an Australian river that detected [SOURCE: Page_15]

349   Dickeya spp. after an enrichment procedure on water samples with temperature superior to 16.2°C [SOURCE: Page_15]

350   (Cother and Gilbert 1990) and with the fact that Dickeya spp. were historically described as being [SOURCE: Page_15]

351   present mainly in tropical and subtropical regions although they now appear to be expanding their [SOURCE: Page_15]

352   global distribution. With the prospect of global warming, it is expected that Dickeya spp. population [SOURCE: Page_15]

353   will increase in river water in the future. Regular water monitoring when water temperature is high [SOURCE: Page_15]

354   could help to mitigate the risk of crop cultures infection by Dickeya spp. through irrigation. [SOURCE: Page_15]

355          The isolation of Dickeya spp., D. undicola, D. aquatica and D. lacustris from surface water [SOURCE: Page_15]

356   of rivers, lakes and irrigation canals have recently been described (Hugouvieux-Cotte-Pattat et al. [SOURCE: Page_15]

357   2019; Oulghazi et al. 2019b; Parkinson et al. 2014). Surprisingly, none of these species were isolated [SOURCE: Page_15]

358   during our 2 year survey suggesting that these species are sporadically associated with water. In [SOURCE: Page_15]

359   2016 and 2017 D. undicola strains were isolated from a small irrigation canal at Monfavet (Oulghazi [SOURCE: Page_15]

360   et al. 2019b). This sampling point is close to the points 20 and 21 sampled in this study, suggesting [SOURCE: Page_15]

361   that extensive sampling in the lower Durance sites during spring and summer when water [SOURCE: Page_15]

362   temperature is high, will likely extend the number of Dickeya spp. recovered. Indeed, we also [SOURCE: Page_15]

363   isolated strains of D. fangzhondai, a highly aggressive Dickeya spp. (Alič et al. 2017), from [SOURCE: Page_15]

364   additional sampling from a small irrigation canal not included in the present study in the lower [SOURCE: Page_15]

365   Durance watershed. [SOURCE: Page_15]

15 [SOURCE: Page_15]

Hajar Ben Moussa Phytopathology. 2022 Feb 27. doi: 10.1094/PHYTO-12-21-0515-R. [SOURCE: Page_16]

366          During our survey, the main isolated Dickeya spp. were D. orizae followed by D. [SOURCE: Page_16]

367   chrysanthemi. Similarly, a survey focusing on Dickeya spp. previously performed in Poland also [SOURCE: Page_16]

368   identified D. zea followed by D. chrysanthemi as the main Dickeya species circulating in surface [SOURCE: Page_16]

369   water (Potrykus et al. 2016). Since this 2016 publication, the D. zeae clade has been split in two [SOURCE: Page_16]

370   species D. zeae and D. oryzae (Wang et al. 2020). In our work, we found that D. orizae was more [SOURCE: Page_16]

371   abundant than D. zeae. Interestingly, neither D. zeae/D.orizae nor D. chrysanthemi have been [SOURCE: Page_16]

372   reported to cause crop disease outbreaks in Europe. D. zea and D. oryzae were, respectively, [SOURCE: Page_16]

373   described as infecting maize and rice, both of which are monocotyledon plants. Therefore, it is [SOURCE: Page_16]

374   plausible that their detection in river water could be due to their association with some common [SOURCE: Page_16]

375   herbaceous monocotyledons unrelated to crop culture found on the riverbanks. Indeed, while some [SOURCE: Page_16]

376   symptomless weeds in the vicinity of potato fields were found to harbour SRP (Tsror et al. 2019; [SOURCE: Page_16]

377   Zoledowska et al. 2018), plants along riverbanks have not been investigated in detail, although D. [SOURCE: Page_16]

378   lacustris has been isolated from the rhizosphere the pond edge plant Solanum dulcamara [SOURCE: Page_16]

379   (Hugouvieux-Cotte-Pattat et al. 2019). As well, P. carotovorum has been isolated from Solanum [SOURCE: Page_16]

380   dulcamara in Poland (Fikowicz-Krosko et al. 2017). Enlarging sampling of common plants found [SOURCE: Page_16]

381   along river banks could help to decipher if these plants are important drivers of SRP circulation in [SOURCE: Page_16]

382   river water, particularly as the distance of plants from the stream course has been shown to be [SOURCE: Page_16]

383   important in structuring aquatic microbial communities (Le et al. 2018). [SOURCE: Page_16]

384          Among SRP, our survey indicated that Pectobacterium spp. were far more frequently isolated [SOURCE: Page_16]

385   than Dickeya spp. in river water. This is in line with previous surface water surveys performed along [SOURCE: Page_16]

386   the Colorado rivers in the USA where no Dickeya spp. were reported (Maddox and Harrison 1988). [SOURCE: Page_16]

387   Therefore, in temperate streams, both in Europe and USA, Pectobacterium spp. largely dominate. [SOURCE: Page_16]

388   This could be linked with the fact that Pectobacterium spp., in contrast with Dickeya spp., could be [SOURCE: Page_16]

389   recovered across a large range of water temperatures. Pectobacterium spp. are also known to survive [SOURCE: Page_16]

390   better in soils than Dickeya spp. (Perombelon and Hyman 1989) and this better survival could also [SOURCE: Page_16]

391   contribute to the higher detection of Pectobacterium spp. in river water through leaching and surface [SOURCE: Page_17]

392   water runoff during rain events. [SOURCE: Page_17]

393          Among the Pectobacterium recovered spp., two recently described species, P. aquaticum [SOURCE: Page_17]

394   and P. versatile dominated, representing respectively 46.6% and 39.9% of the recovered [SOURCE: Page_17]

395   Pectobacterium spp. A recent taxonomic update of 265 Pectobacterium strains hosted at the CIRM- [SOURCE: Page_17]

396   CFBP international collection that gathers strains isolated since 1944, showed that P. versatile is a [SOURCE: Page_17]

397   broad spectrum species regularly isolated from a large number of cultivated plants. In contrast, no [SOURCE: Page_17]

398   strain of P. aquaticum isolated from plants are hosted in this collection (Portier et al. 2020). [SOURCE: Page_17]

399   Therefore, the abundance of both species in river water might have different origins. Interestingly, [SOURCE: Page_17]

400   while P. versatile was recovered all along the stream course and was regularly isolated in the upper [SOURCE: Page_17]

401   part of the watershed in Alpine pristine water area, P. aquaticum was mostly found in the lower part [SOURCE: Page_17]

402   of the watershed and more than 50% of the strains were isolated from only two sampling sites. These [SOURCE: Page_17]

403   two sampling sites were characterized by abundant presence of aquatic plants on their banks, [SOURCE: Page_17]

404   suggesting that P. aquaticum could be associated with some of these plants on the riverbank. In [SOURCE: Page_17]

405   contrast, the large circulation of P. versatile all along the stream course, both in the pristine area [SOURCE: Page_17]

406   and the lower, agriculturally dominated part of the watershed, is reminiscent of what was observed [SOURCE: Page_17]

407   another plant bacterial pathogen P. syringae. Actually, bacteria belonging to the P. syringae [SOURCE: Page_17]

408   complex population were regularly isolated from alpine lakes and stream and their ecology was [SOURCE: Page_17]

409   proposed to be linked to the water cycle (Morris et al. 2013). However, strains of P. versatile are [SOURCE: Page_17]

410   far less abundant in Alpine pristine water than strains of the P. syringae complex (Pédron et al. [SOURCE: Page_17]

411   2020), and their presence in clouds or rain has not been reported (Failor et al. 2017). In addition, P. [SOURCE: Page_17]

412   versatile occurrence along the watershed strongly correlates with environmental variables such as [SOURCE: Page_17]

413   nitrate and DOC indicating a larger occurrence in the lower part of the watershed while previous [SOURCE: Page_17]

414   work indicated that strains of the P. syringae complex are abundant in the upper part of the [SOURCE: Page_17]

415   watershed (Monteil et al. 2014). Therefore, while P. versatile is the SRP species with the largest [SOURCE: Page_17]

416   observed prevalence both on plant and in river stream it behave quite differently from strains of the [SOURCE: Page_18]

417   P. syringae complex. [SOURCE: Page_18]

418          P. versatile, despites its large prevalence has only recently been described. This is principally [SOURCE: Page_18]

419   due to its close genomic proximity with P. carotovorum which explains the previous mix up of the [SOURCE: Page_18]

420   two species (Portier et al. 2019, 2020). This mix up was also favoured by the fact that P. [SOURCE: Page_18]

429          SRP pathogens regularly isolated from crop disease outbreaks such as P. atrosepticum and [SOURCE: Page_18]

438          The large majority of strains isolated during this survey belonged to two species, P. [SOURCE: Page_18]

439   aquaticum and P. versatile, that are not known to be associated with severe outbreaks for crop [SOURCE: Page_18]

440   culture. This suggests that the risk of infection may be overestimated when surveys do not include [SOURCE: Page_18]
