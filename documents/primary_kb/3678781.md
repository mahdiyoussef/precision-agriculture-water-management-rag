Impacts of curd washing on the development of the functional properties of Raclette cheese Émilien Delormea,c, Benoît Paysanta, Philippe Trossata, Yves Gaüzèreb, Éric Beuvierc, Christine Achilleosc a ACTALIA Pôle Expertise Analytique, 39800, Poligny, France ; b ISBA Franche-Comté, ENILBio, 39800, Poligny, France ; c INRAE, URTAL, 39800, Poligny, France [SOURCE: Page_1]

Introduction A cheese variety is partly characterized by its functional properties, such as its rheological and cooking properties. Semi-hard cheese varieties like Raclette present a soft and homogenous matrix with good melting properties. Curd washing is a crucial manufacturing stage for the development of these properties because it allows to manage the early composition of the aqueous phase of the cheese curd, mainly the lactose content. Nevertheless, it is an expensive stage of the manufacture because it needs addition of water, which increase the amount of water consumed and the amount of whey to manage for the factory, whose management is expensive in energy. This stage of the cheese manufacture could be optimized through two parameters, among others, we chose to study. The first is the level of curd washing (the intensity of dilution) that could be adjusted according to the desired properties of the cheese. The other is the mode of curd washing, i.e. the volumes of whey withdrawn and water added to the vat to reach the level that could be adjusted to reduce water consumption and the volume of whey produced. However, the optimization of these parameters requires a better understanding of their impacts, which are poorly described in the literature. The aim of this study was to determine the impact of the level and mode of curd washing on the functional properties of cheese and to describe the mechanisms by which these parameters contribute to the development of these properties. These parameters have been studied in the context of cheeses with similar dry matter contents to specifically study the impact of the modification of the aqueous phase by curd washing. Conclusion At a determined dry matter content, the modification of the composition of the aqueous phase of the curd by the level of curd washing affects the microstructure of the ripened cheese through variables such as pH, the content of INSOL Ca, the proportion of degraded caseins and the content of hydrophobic peptides. Hence, the increase of the curd washing level leads to the development of a more deformable matrix with a higher mechanical resistance. Since the curd washing mode has no impact on all these variables, our study opens up the prospect of controlling the development of the functional properties of cheese while economically optimizing this stage of production with the mode.
