29        Abstract [SOURCE: Page_2]

30        As human activities caused a dramatic decline of European eel population since the 1970s, the 31   European Union has set targets to ensure a 40 % escapement to the sea of the silver eel biomass by 32   considerably reducing anthropogenic impact. Thus, human obstacles to fish migration like dams and 33   hydropower plants should enable efficient management measures ensuring safe passage for eels during 34   the migration. In order to provide a quick and efficient assessment of eel conservation measure applied 35   to the sluice gates of a large floodplain lake, we implemented a novel evaluation method using predictions 36   on past migration seasons when no management measure were applied. For this purpose, we collected 37   acoustic telemetry monitoring data over three migration seasons and fitted a predictive model based 38   on Boosted Regression Trees (BRTs) to describe the influence of environmental parameters on migration. 39   The water level difference over two days proved to be decisive, along with early migration occasions in 40   the season, as an increase of at least 10 cm water level was associated with a sharp increase of migration 41   probability. We then used the BRT model to predict migration occasions at the dam over 8 past seasons 42   and forecast the impact on escapement of management measures if they had been applied. Thanks to 43   this original prospective assessment of dam management measures, we identified an enhanced opening 44   decision rule, capable of increasing the eel escapement for each year of the study. The management 45   measure is particularly efficient during the years with poor hydrological conditions (i.e. droughts), that 46   is the periods with the lowest initial escapement rates. Finally, efficient management measures to 47   increase silver eel escapement were based on increasing the number of gate opening days by only a few 48   days per year (c.a. 15 %). However, the management measure only focused on silver eel migration, so 49   that further investigations should consider the impacts of other threats occurring during the life cycle. 50   This study also provides a highly operational approach for fast evaluation of conservation measures, 51   avoiding lengthy and expensive monitoring campaigns of classical ex-post assessments. [SOURCE: Page_2]

55        Introduction [SOURCE: Page_3]

56        The worldwide fragmentation of river networks represents a major concern for conservation and 57   restoration of continental aquatic ecosystems (Barbarossa et al., 2020). Alteration of river continuity is 58   particularly damaging for diadromous fish that have to migrate in both upstream and downstream 59   directions for achieving their biological cycles (van Puijenbroek, Buijse, Kraak, & Verdonschot, 2019). 60   Among them, the European eel (Anguilla anguilla, L. 1758) is highly sensitive to this threat throughout 61   its continental freshwater phase. After spawning and hatching, in the north Atlantic Convergence Zone 62   (Chang, Feunteun, Miyazawa, & Tsukamoto, 2020; Miller et al., 2015), leptocephalus larvae are oriented 63   by marine currents towards European coasts. Early continental stages colonize coastal waters, rivers 64   and lakes, where they grow between 3 and 30 years. After this continental phase, eels undergo 65   morphological and anatomical changes, reaching the silver eel stage, and undertake a long migration 66   back to their breeding areas (Feunteun, 2002; Aarestrup et al. 2008; Righton et al., 2016). At this stage, 67   the disturbances of river continuity greatly affect the downstream migration toward the sea and can 68   cause immediate and / or delayed mortality (Besson et al., 2016; Drouineau et al., 2017; Trancart et al., 69   2020; Winter, Jansen, & Breukelaar, 2007) or delayed timing of migration (Behrmann-Godel & Eckmann, 70   2003). [SOURCE: Page_3]

71        As recruitment rate of European eel has declined dramatically by a factor of ten since the late 72   1970s (Dekker et al., 2003; ICES, 2018), it is considered since 2014 as a critically endangered species by 73   the International Union for Conservation of Nature (Jacoby & Gollock, 2014). With a view to recovering 74   the European eel stock, the European Union has adopted a regulation which mandates the 75   establishment in each Member State of Eel Management Plans describing measures to reduce 76   anthropogenic impact on eels (e.g. reducing commercial fishing activity, taking measures to make rivers 77   passable or temporary switching-off of hydro-electric power turbines, restoring habitats). A common 78   objective of an escapement to the sea of at least 40 % of the silver eel biomass relative to the best 79   estimate of escapement that would have existed if no anthropogenic influences had impacted the stock 80   was also set (UE Regulation No.1100/2007, Council of the European Union, 2007). [SOURCE: Page_3]

81        Several studies dealt with efficient fisheries management measures (Beaulaton & Briand, 2007) 82   and hydroelectric dams management (Larinier & Travade, 2002; Gosset et al., 2005; Watene & Boubee, 83   2005; Winter, Jansen, & Bruijs, 2006; Trancart et al., 2013). In contrast, few cared about optimizing 84   management of closed (or semi-closed) water systems like lagoons, regulated lakes and reservoirs 85   where fisheries reduction is sometimes necessary but must not supplant the need of a consistent 86   management (Lagarde et al., 2021; Trancart et al., 2018). These ecosystems generally offer suitable 87   growing area where eels are subjected to recreational or professional fisheries (Dekker, 2003). Lakes, [SOURCE: Page_3]

However, the artificial control of water exchanges by sluice gates can also impair the 91   seaward migrating silver eels, so that explicit management rules are required for ensuring silver eel 92   escapement. The selective opening of hydroelectric dam spillway gates which can provide safe 93   downstream passage for migrant eels (Watene & Boubee, 2005) should thus be applied to various 94   human hydraulic structures, raising the necessity to forecast efficiently eel migration peaks. Silver eel 95   migration generally occurs during the night (e.g. Aarestrup et al. 2010) and is triggered by various 96   environmental parameters like discharge, temperature, conductivity, turbidity or lunar phase (e.g. 97   Cullen and McCarthy, 2003; Durif et al., 2003; Sandlund et al., 2017; Vøllestad et al., 1986) which makes 98   possible the development of predictive models (Smith, Fackler, Eyler, Villegas Ortiz, & Welsh, 2017; 99   Teichert, Tétard, Trancart, de Oliveira, et al., 2020; Trancart et al., 2013). Such predictions have already 100   been achieved e.g. for birds whose migration intensities were forecast with a regression model based 101   on meteorological data (Van Belle, Shamoun-Baranes, Van Loon, & Bouten, 2007) and also for silver eels 102   in the case of river catchment and based on commercial fishery data (Durif & Elie, 2008; Trancart et al., 103   2013; Teichert, Tétard, Trancart, Feunteun, et al., 2020). 108        Furthermore, climate change is likely to structurally change the eel migration patterns owing to its 109   effect on triggering factors of migration (Ficke, Myrick, & Hansen, 2007). For example, extreme 110   temperatures that are an acknowledged consequence of climate change (IPCC, 2018) can inhibit eel 111   migration (Lowe, 1952; Vollestad et al., 1986). Regarding hydrology, extreme precipitations and floods 112   are expected in Europe (Madsen, Lawrence, Lang, Martinkova, & Kjeldsen, 2014) which implies a strong 113   hydrologic variability. 131        Grand-Lieu Lake is the French largest plain lake in winter located in western France, southwest of 132   Nantes (47°05° N; 1°39° W, Fig. 1a, b). Its surface area ranges from 2 500 ha in summer to 6 300 ha in 133   winter whereas its depth ranges from 0.70–1.20 m in summer to 3.00–3.50 m in winter. The particularly 134   changing shape of the lake is due to its plain location, which leads to the flooding of the surrounding 135   wet meadows in winter. The lake is structured around a 10 km² open water stretch that constitutes the 136   permanently flooded zone with a surrounding region of floating-leaved plants.
