Article history:                                             The reuse of water for agricultural practices becomes progressively more important due to increasing demands for a Received 2 December 2021                                     transition to a circular economy. Treated wastewater can be an alternative option of blue water used for the irrigation Received in revised form 24 February 2022                    of crops but its risks need to be evaluated. This study assesses the uptake and metabolization of pharmaceuticals and Accepted 15 March 2022 personal care products (PPCPs) derived from treated wastewater into lettuce as well as the impact on root-associated Available online 19 March 2022 bacteria under a realistic and worst-case scenario. Lettuce was grown in a controlled greenhouse and irrigated with Editor: Yifeng Zhang                                         water or treated wastewater spiked with and without a mixture of fourteen different PPCPs at 10 μg/L or 100 μg/L. After harvesting the plants, the same soil was reused for a consecutive cultivation campaign to test for the accumula- Keywords:                                                    tion of PPCPs. Twelve out of fourteen spiked PPCPs were detected in lettuce roots, and thirteen in leaves. In roots, Pharmaceuticals and personal care products                   highest concentrations were measured for sucralose, sulfamethoxazole and citalopram, while sucralose, acesulfame (PPCP)                                                       and carbamazepine were the highest in leaves. Higher PPCP concentrations were found in lettuce roots irrigated Treated wastewater                                           with spiked treated wastewater than in those irrigated with spiked water. The absolute bacterial abundance remained Plant uptake                                                 stable over both cultivation campaigns and was not affected by any of the treatments (type of irrigation water (water Microbial ecotoxicology vs. wastewater) nor concentration of PPCPs). However, the irrigation of lettuce with treated wastewater had a signif- Plant growth-promoting bacteria icant effect on the microbial α-diversity indices at the end of the second cultivation campaign, and modiﬁed the struc- ture and community composition of root-associated bacteria at the end of both campaigns.
