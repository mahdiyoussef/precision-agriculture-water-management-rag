1. Explanation of the work carried out by the beneficiaries and overview of results for the species target by the project The FREECLIMB project is built to match topic 1.2.1 of the PRIMA (Sect. 2) framework in developing smart and sustainable farming systems in Mediterranean countries, to preserve natural resources by increasing production efficiency. This is pursued by advancing knowledge on mechanisms of plant environmental adaptation and biotic/abiotic stress resilience. The project targets major fruit tree species with the aim of improving the availability of breeding and germplasm material adapted to limited external input and future climatic scenarios predicted for the Mediterranean area, through the characterization and exploitation of local biodiversity. The project focuses on key ideotypes elaborated in collaboration with Fruit Farming Actors with the core objective of providing a toolkit (diverse germplasm, tools and methods) to accelerate exploitation, breeding and selection of resilient varieties in key traditional fruit crops of Mediterranean agriculture. To these ends, the project has pursued the following specific objectives: i) applying protocols and integrated tools (e.g. genotyping methods, data analysis) to support the characterization, exploitation and selection of varieties adapted to a range of agroecological and management conditions ii) dissecting the genetic bases of traits/processes linked to sustainability and plant resilience to biotic and abiotic stresses, with particular focus on disentangling genotype-by-environment-by- management (GxExM) interactions. iii) unraveling the molecular, biochemical and physiological basis of plant adaptation to different environmental (soil and climate) and agronomic conditions (water management) and to biotic/abiotic (single or multiple) stresses. iv) developing and applying genomics-based breeding methods to improve introgression and selection efficiency. v) devising adaptation strategies to cope with the combined effects of multiple stresses possibly co-occurring under field conditions (e.g heat waves and drought, pests and diseases). vi) exploiting germplasm resources, by identifying and characterizing spontaneous and domesticated sources of biodiversity. vii) transferring project results through training and dissemination activities dedicated especially to breeders (focusing on young scientists), nurserymen, growers and stakeholders, particularly in those countries where fruit production is less developed. In almond and Prunus hybrids, major efforts were dedicated to the fingerprinting of almond materials across the various germplasm collections, including wild almond, to characterize the population structure, genetic identity and origin. Considering the vulnerability of almond cultivation to climate challenges and dangerous diseases, priority traits included adaptation to environmental factors and resistance/tolerance to major diseases such as Sharka, brown rot and Fusicoccum canker. Promising materials tolerant to Fusicoccum were identified, but not for brown rot, although a certain range of variability for susceptibility was observed. The most outstanding results have been the identification of two sources of peach powdery mildew resistance from almond (Vr$_3$) and from P. davidiana, as well the mapping of two QTLs for Sharka resistance in a peach x almond cross. In olive, major efforts have been devoted to the characterization of sources of variability across olive germplasms for traits linked to environmental adaptation (reproductive phenology; drought, salinity and heat stress tolerance) as well as resistance/tolerance to pest and diseases, particularly olive fruit fly and Verticillium wilt. In grape, the activities focused on resistance to downy (Plasmopara viticola) and powdery (Erysiphe necator) mildew and rootstock drought tolerance. Prepayment of NGS services was completed by all partners and results are expected by the end of year 2023. Considering the complexity of a long-term maintenance of a database (often resulting in obsolescence and/or decommissioning, with data loss or unused) and the progressive centralization and specialization of worldwide public databases/repositories (continuously updated and upgraded), CNR in agreement with all partners decided to focus on a cloud repositories to maintain indefinitely all data from each species/partners pending their publication in peer-reviewed journals and associated resources (GitHub, Figshare, EBI, NCBI, Supplementary Files etc.). Unpublished data and FruitBreedomics database will maintained indefinitely into FREECLIMB cloud repository and publicly release after 2026. [SOURCE: Page_22]
