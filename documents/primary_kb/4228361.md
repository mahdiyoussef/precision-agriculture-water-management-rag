11   Abstract [SOURCE: Page_2]

12   Accurate evaluation of natural recharge is fundamental for the estimation of renewable [SOURCE: Page_2]

13   groundwater resources and constitutes a key issue for water resources management. Among [SOURCE: Page_2]

14   physical, chemical and numerical methods, the water-table fluctuation technique is simple [SOURCE: Page_2]

15   and easy to use. The main question is where, with respect to the draining stream, to locate [SOURCE: Page_2]

16   the monitoring well to effectively observe representative water level fluctuations. Storage and [SOURCE: Page_2]

17   discharged volumes in an unconfined aquifer during the water-table fluctuation application [SOURCE: Page_2]

18   are described with equations using the Dupuit-Forchheimer model. This leads to [SOURCE: Page_2]

19   determination of an optimal distance from the stream (0.4 times the length of the aquifer) for [SOURCE: Page_2]

20   siting observation wells. Beyond this distance, the computed volumes stored in the aquifer [SOURCE: Page_2]

1 [SOURCE: Page_2]

21   are overestimated by up to 20%. Less than this distance, the stored volume is highly [SOURCE: Page_3]

22   underestimated. [SOURCE: Page_3]

23   Keywords: water table fluctuation, piezometry, recharge, discharge, analytical solution [SOURCE: Page_3]

25       1. Introduction [SOURCE: Page_3]

26   The estimation of natural recharge, defined as the precipitation infiltration to the water table, is a [SOURCE: Page_3]

27   fundamental issue in hydrogeology. It provides a first step towards the estimation of renewable [SOURCE: Page_3]

28   groundwater resources and constitutes a key issue for overall water resources management. Several [SOURCE: Page_3]

29   methods are used to estimate the natural recharge: physical, chemical or numerical. The reliability of [SOURCE: Page_3]

30   recharge estimates using different techniques is variable (Scanlon et al. 2002; Scanlon 2011). Among [SOURCE: Page_3]

31   these methods, the water-table fluctuation (WTF) technique relies on the measurement of [SOURCE: Page_3]

32   groundwater storage and associated water level changes induced by recharge during a given period [SOURCE: Page_3]

33   (Healy and Cook 2002). The main advantages of this technique are that the measurement of water [SOURCE: Page_3]

34   table depth in a well is easy and accurate and that the method is direct (not as in chemical techniques). [SOURCE: Page_3]

35   The attractiveness of the WTF method lies in its simplicity and ease of use compared to other [SOURCE: Page_3]

36   quantitative methods such as numerical models which require comprehensive data sets on aquifer [SOURCE: Page_3]

37   parameters and flow fields (Sanford 2002). No assumptions are made on the mechanisms by which [SOURCE: Page_3]

38   water infiltrates vertically through the unsaturated zone; hence, the presence of preferential flow [SOURCE: Page_3]

39   paths (localized recharge) within the unsaturated zone does not restrict its application. Because the [SOURCE: Page_3]

40   water level measured in an observation well is representative of a given area (at least several square [SOURCE: Page_3]

41   meters), the WTF method can be viewed as an integrated approach and less a point measurement [SOURCE: Page_3]

42   than those methods that are based on data in the unsaturated zone (Healy and Cook 2002). [SOURCE: Page_3]

43   Applications at the watershed scale, using observation well networks have been used in numerous [SOURCE: Page_3]

44   studies in various geological and climatic conditions (Maréchal et al. 2006; Misstear et al. 2009; Jassas [SOURCE: Page_3]

45   and Merkel 2014; Ala-Aho et al. 2015; Chemingui et al. 2015; Labrecque et al. 2020). This method has [SOURCE: Page_3]

2 [SOURCE: Page_3]

46   been implemented through recent development of the tool ESPERE for computing recharge (Lanini et [SOURCE: Page_4]

47   al. 2016; Lanini and Caballero 2021). [SOURCE: Page_4]

48   The WTF method requires knowledge of the specific yield of the aquifer (Sophocleous 1991) and the [SOURCE: Page_4]

49   identification of a well where the groundwater level can be monitored . Wells should be located so [SOURCE: Page_4]

50   that the water levels they monitor are representative of the aquifer as a whole (Healy and Cook 2002). [SOURCE: Page_4]

51   In this Note, the location of observation wells is investigated by means of basic equations governing [SOURCE: Page_4]

52   groundwater flow in an unconfined aquifer under simplified assumptions. Storage and discharged [SOURCE: Page_4]

53   volumes during WTF application are described using analytical equations. The analysis provides simple [SOURCE: Page_4]

54   and practical criteria to quantify, on one hand, the accuracy on natural recharge evaluations based on [SOURCE: Page_4]

55   water level measurements in existing observation wells and, on the other hand, to define the ideal [SOURCE: Page_4]

56   position where a new well should be drilled in order to improve the accuracy of the estimation. [SOURCE: Page_4]

156   application of the method leads to an overestimation of the natural recharge ranging from 0 at the ideal [SOURCE: Page_9]

157   position (X = 0.4 L$_0$) to 21 % at the aquifer divide ( X = L$_0$). For observation wells located close to the stream, [SOURCE: Page_9]
