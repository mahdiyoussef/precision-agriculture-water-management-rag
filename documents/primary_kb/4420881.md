8   Abstract [SOURCE: Page_1]

O 9   The main goal of this work was to evaluate the potential of the Shuttleworth–Wallace (SW) model for mapping actual crop 10   evapotranspiration (ET) over complex surface located in the foothill of the Atlas Mountain (Morocco). This model needs [SOURCE: Page_1]

PR 11   many input variables to compute soil (rss ) and vegetation (rsv ) resistances, which are often diicult to estimate at large scale 12   particularly soil moisture. In this study, a new approach to spatialize rss and rsv based on two thermal-based proxy variables 13   was proposed. Land Surface Temperature ( LST) and Normalized Diference Vegetation Index (NDVI) derived from Landsat 14   data were combined with the endmember temperatures for soil (Tsmin and Tsmax ) and vegetation (Tvmin and Tvmax ), which [SOURCE: Page_1]

D 15   were simulated by a surface energy balance model, to compute the soil (Ts) and the vegetation (Tv) temperatures. Based on 16   these temperatures, two thermal proxies (SIss for soil and SIsv for vegetation) were calculated and related to rss and rsv , with TE 17   an empirical exponential relationship [with a correlation coeicient (R) of about 0.6 and 0.5 for soil and vegetation, respec- 18   tively]. The proposed approach was initially evaluated at a local scale, by comparing the results to observations by an eddy 19   covariance system installed over an area planted with olive trees intercropped with wheat. In a second step, the new approach 20   was applied over a large area which contains a mixed vegetation (tall and short) crossed by a river to derive rss and rsv , and EC [SOURCE: Page_1]

21   thereafter to estimate ET. A Large aperture scintillometer (LAS) installed over a line transect of 1.4 km and spanning the 22   total area was used to validate the obtained ET. The comparison conirmed the ability of the proposed approach to provide 23   satisfactory ET maps with an RMSE, bias and R$_2$ equal to 0.08 mm/h, 0.06 mm/h and 0.80, respectively.
