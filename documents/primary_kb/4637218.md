Combining field experiment and modelling to understand the effect of management practices on N2O emissions in Miscanthus  giganteus. C. Peyrard, F. Ferchaud , B. Mary, E. Gréhan, J. Léonard [SOURCE: Page_2]

Background Management practices of Miscanthus  giganteus, a promising energy crop, can impact the C and N cycles in the soil, influencing N2O emissions and the crop GHG balance. Late harvest (in February) results in the accumulation of senescent leaves on the soil surface (mulch) contrary to early harvest (in October). Presence of mulch but also the management of the N fertilizer form (NH$_4$/NO$_3$) may stimulate nitrification and denitrification differently. Question: Harvest time (early or late harvest) and fertilizer form (NH$_4$ or NO$_3$) do they impact soil properties and N2O emissions ? [SOURCE: Page_2]

Conclusion: Our study highlights the crucial impact of management practices on N2O emissions in Miscanthus crops through changes in physico-chemical parameters and soil processes on the short and long term. The effect of fertilizer form on N2O emissions remained limited. However, harvest management and mulching, whether intentional or not, should be carefully considered in terms of mitigation of emissions and to maximize the benefits of bioenergy crops.
