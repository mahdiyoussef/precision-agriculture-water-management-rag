Introduction 23   Monitoring the performance of water services is widely discussed in the literature. The International Water 24   Association (IWA) initiative (Alegre et al., 2000) to establish key performance indicators (KPIs) has led to the 25   emergence of mandatory national databases in several countries to improve the management of water 26   services and to ensure transparency for stakeholders and users. In France, we can cite Decree no 2007-675 27   of 2 May 2007 (MEDD, 2007) which defines mandatory performance indicators for water and wastewater 28   services, and the creation of the national SISPEA$_1$ database. However, since the KPIs are generally measured 29   a posteriori, one of the challenges of our work is to develop a decision-support model capable of predicting 30   KPIs in an ex-ante manner following the implementation of actions or decisions on the water network. The 31   water efficiency ratio (WER) of drinking water networks is one of the main KPIs. It is assessed by the ratio of 32   authorized water consumption to that of the water produced and imported. To improve this efficiency, human 33   and financial resources must be put into action to reduce water leaks, either by replacing assets such as pipes, 34   meters or connections, implying capital expenditures (CAPEX), or by conducting leak detection followed by 35   repairs or asset maintenance that leads to operational expenditures (OPEX). PRISM software (Prospective and Scenario 39   Research for Management), developed at ENGEES, balances asset actions with their impact on efficiency and 40   annual costs (CAPEX and OPEX) through a causality model, enabling managers to choose a cost-effective 41   policy. However, to obtain reliable predictions, an improvement in understanding water losses in the network 42   and their potential causes is necessary. Our theoretical model, validated by empirical analysis, enhances this 43   understanding by supplementing available data with estimates of flow rates, durations, and the number of 44   leaks. In the decision model used by PRISM software, the functions include analysis of data completeness 45   and improvement of asset knowledge, water balance and estimation of water losses, assessment of asset 46   policy, comparison with a reference solution, and the search for alternative policies. The asset policy is defined 47   as the combination of investment and maintenance efforts to be carried out within an accounting period. The 48   approach described in this article aims to provide arguments to justify public decision-making with factual and 49   objective elements to address the following question: How can the consequences of decisions made by water 50   service managers be anticipated? More specifically, we provide managers with a decision support tool that will 51   enable the efficient allocation of their budget over an accounting period (short-term) between operational 52   expenditures for leak detection (OPEX) and the renewal of various equipment: pipelines, connections, meters 53   (CAPEX). At this stage, the goal is not to precisely specify the actions to be taken on the network, but instead 54   to define the distribution of efforts to be made. We assume that the state of structural deterioration and the network’s level of performance will 174    heavily depend on the asset management policies adopted in recent years.
