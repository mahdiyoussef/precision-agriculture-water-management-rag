Abstract The objective of this study is to estimate the spatial deep percolation (DP) by combining remote sensing data and SAMIR (Satellite Monitoring of IRrigation) tool. DP was derived as the residual component of the water balance at the root zone. The computation of the water budget requires climatic data (reference evapotranspiration (ET$_0$) and rainfall), land cover, crop coefficient derived from Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI), and hydrodynamic soil parameters such as soil moisture at field capacity and wilting point. The main component of the water balance is the evapotranspiration which is spatialized based on the FAO-56 approach and the relationship between crop coefficient and NDVI.The developed approach has been tested over an irrigated area in the Haouz plain during the agricultural period (2011- 2012). The Results showed that DP amount follows water supply fluctuations (Irrigation provided by ORMVAH and rainfall). The high values of DP are observed during heavy rainfall in March by recording values around 36, 27 and 20 mm for the sugar beet, wheat and olive trees, respectively. While for the rest of the season from April to June, the vegetation cover was exposed to high water stress due to the mismatch of water supply. [SOURCE: Page_1]

## INTRODUCTION

In the Haouz plain, irrigation is a major component of water demand. It is estimated that about 83% of available resources is dedicated to agriculture with low efficiency (Chehbouni et al., 2008). Irrigation areas are often associated with excess deep percolation (DP) which is defined as the net amount of water percolating below the root zone (Bethune et al., 2008).Deep percolation under irrigation occurs when infiltrated water exceeds the storage capacity of the soil (Bethune et al., 2008). Deep percolation which is one of the most important fluxes of the water balance is a key factor for optimizing irrigation water management (Nassah et al., 2018).Direct measurement of DP is difficult under field conditions (Bond, 1998). Deep percolation is often calculated as a residual of the water balance (Wang et al., 2012). The amount of deep percolation will depend on water supply (Irrigation and precipitation), soil properties, and evapotranspiration (Sammis et al., 1983).Furthermore, remote sensing (RS) capabilities for monitoring vegetation and its physical properties on large areas have been identified for years now (Tucker, 1979; Caloz et Collet, 2001). In this study, regional Deep Percolation amounts was estimated with the SAMIR software (SAtellite Monitoring of IRrigation) by estimating the water balance (Simonneaux et al., 2009) using the FAO-56 dual crop coefficient water balance model fed with high resolution NDVI image time series providing estimates of both the actual basal crop coefficient and the vegetation fraction cover. In this study, regional deep percolation was estimated over an irrigated area located in the Haouz plain during the agricultural period (2011-2012) using the SAMIR model fed by SPOT high resolution time series. The accuracy of the SAMIR tool was assessed at both plot scale based on evapotranspiration ground measurements by eddy covariance device. [SOURCE: Page_1]

## MATERIALS AND METHODS

Study area [SOURCE: Page_2]

The study area which is called “R$_3$ zone” is located in irrigated region of Sidi Rahal in the Haouz plain (Figure 1), covering an area of 2800 ha approximately 40 km east of Marrakech city. The climate is typically Mediterranean, it is characterised by low and irregular rainfall with an annual average of about 240 mm with 70% recorded between November and April, while the evaporative demand (ET$_0$) can reach 1500 mm year−1(Duchemin et al., 2008). The local agricultural office (Office Régional de Mise en Valeur Agricole du Haouz or ORMVAH) manages the irrigation schedule through a network of concrete channels directly connected to a dam. Concerning the quantity, a water depth is attributed to each farm proportional to the farm size and an irrigation scheduling is negociated with the farmers. A survey of land use was carried out in 2012 growing season and shows that the dominant crop was wheat with 46% of total surface followed by sugar beet with 17%finally the remaining lands are occupied by tree plantations (Diarra et al., 2017). Experiment and data analysis [SOURCE: Page_2]

Half hourly meteorological measurements were recorded using an automated weather station installed in the study area. It includes measurements of air temperature and relative humidity using HMP45C, Vaisala, wind speed and direction using an anemometer (A100R anemometer, R.M. Young Company, USA); rainfall using an automatic rain gauge station (FSS500 tipping bucket automatic rain gauge, Campbell Inc., USA).Daily averaged values of climatic data were calculated in order to compute the daily reference evapotranspiration ET$_0$ (mm day−1), according to the standard FAO-56 Penman–Monteith equation (Allen et al., 1998).A flux station was installed in two plots wheat and beet sugar (Figure^1,c). It measures the various energy balance components using the eddy covariance (EC). These experiments allowed continuous computing of actual evapotranspiration (ETc) at a 30 min time step by measuring latent and sensible heat fluxes using Krypton KH20 hygrometer and a 3D sonic anemometer Campbell CSAT$_3$.Daily values for stations observations were obtained by summing up half-hourly data. The evaluation of eddy covariance measurements was carried out by checking the closure of the energy balance using Bowen ratio (BR) correction (Twine et al., 2000). Indeed, the obtained daily errors of this balance closure were generally less than 10% of available energy (see (Diarra et al, 2017) for more details).Six SPOT$_5$ images were acquired for 2011-2012 season (with 10 m resolution and 26-day revisit). The geometric correction of SPOT$_5$ images was performed using a FORMOSAT 2 image georeferenced in Lambert North Morocco (Duchemin et al., 2008). For the atmospheric correction, the traditional Empirical Line Calibration (ELC) method or pseudo-invariant method (Schott et al., 1988; Smith et Milton, 1999) was used [SOURCE: Page_2]

and evaluated based on the CROP-SCAN measurements. For further information and in particular on the correction of SPOT$_5$ images, readers are invited to refer to (Diarra et al, 2017). The SPOT-5 data were used to calculate the Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI). [SOURCE: Page_3]

SAMIR tool provides for each pixel, at the daily time step, the spatial estimates of the water balance of the crops. The originality of this approach is the use of series of images satellite, in order to better monitor the real development of vegetation over time. The FAO method (Allen, 1998) is used for evapotranspiration (ETc) calculation, linked with a soil modeling including three compartments, namely the evaporation, root and deep soil compartments (Zhang et Wegehenkel, 2006). SAMIR requires climatic data, land cover and vegetation development obtained by remote sensing to derive crop coefficients (Kc, Kcb) from the NDVI, and soil data to characterize hydrodynamic properties. The FAO-56 (Allen et al., 1998) approach has been largely used for modeling crop evapotranspiration (ETc). This approach is based on the concept of reference evapotranspiration for a standard well-watered grass, modulated by crop coefficients that can be derived from NDVI (Neale et al., 1989, Er-Raki et al., 2007) and considers crop types, crop stages of growth, soil conditions and irrigation practices. Mapping of Deep Percolation [SOURCE: Page_5]

SAMIR was run over the whole irrigated plain using the image time series for the season (2011-2012) to compute spatially distributed estimates of deep percolation using real rate of irrigation. Water balance components are computed for each pixel. Land use information is required for each pixel since many parameters of the model are crop specific. In this study we simulated the irrigation rate in order to compare it with the real one to get an idea of the volumes of water that are probably being pumped. Irrigation is modeled specifically for each land cover class, so as to reproduce the various irrigation practices applied by the farmers on the ground. These practices are described by several parameters that the user has to set. A major parameter is the threshold water content in the root layer to trigger a water input, MAD, management allowable depletion for irrigation triggering and the Irrigation height (Allen et al., 1998) (Table^1).The soil properties (θfc, θwp, REW, Ze, Zsoil, Difer, Difrd) in the study area were taken from the soil map. From the beginning of February, the values increased and reached its maximum in April. Then, the fraction cover decreases at the end of the cycle, especially for cereal crops, which correspond to the senescence period. [SOURCE: Page_5]

## CONCLUSION

The estimation of deep percolation losses beyond the root zone and their temporal and spatial evolution are the priorities of scientists and manager, particularly in arid and semi- arid regions with scarce water resources and characterized by frequent dry periods and a growing water deficit due to the high demand of water resources. The results showed that water requirements at the study area during the agricultural season (December-June) for the sugar beet, wheat and olive plots are 640, 477 and 445 mm, respectively. The comparison of the annual water requirements of these crops with the water supply (I+P) indicates two periods: the first one from December to March when the water supply exceeds the water requirement, which can lead to losses by deep percolation beyond the root zone. While the second period (from April to June), which is characterized by the mismatch of water supply, can lead to the water stress and groundwater pumping. These results are in accordance with the spatial variation of the deep percolation wherein, the maximum value is reached in March when the precipitation rate is highest (56.3 mm) by recording a relative deep percolation of 50, 44 and 22% of water supply for sugar beet, wheat and trees olive, respectively. Whereas the difference between actual and simulated irrigation gives an approximate estimate of the quantities of water probably pumped, which it must be compared with groundwater abstractions.
