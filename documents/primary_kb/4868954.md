Abstract [SOURCE: Page_3]

Metallic trace elements (MTEs) constitute a major source of chemical pollution and represent [SOURCE: Page_3]

a threat to aquatic ecosystems and organisms. Important variation in contamination may exist [SOURCE: Page_3]

at a local scale in relation to the environment (hydrosystem, trophic ressources) and individual [SOURCE: Page_3]

traits (age, sex). Heretofore, the factors influencing MTEs exposure of freshwater reptiles in [SOURCE: Page_3]

temperate regions are not fully understood. Freshwater turtles have a relatively high trophic [SOURCE: Page_3]

position and a long lifespan, thus being potentially highly exposed due to bioaccumulation and [SOURCE: Page_3]

bioamplification processes. We investigated MTE blood concentrations from two populations [SOURCE: Page_3]

of the European Pond Turtle Emys orbicularis in the Camargue wetland (France). These [SOURCE: Page_3]

populations, monitored since 1997, differ in their habitat and exposure (irrigation versus [SOURCE: Page_3]

drainage canal). In this study, we detected 7 MTEs (Cu, Fe, Hg, Mn, Pb, Se, and Zn) which [SOURCE: Page_3]

levels depended on site and individual characteristics. Hg was positively related to body size [SOURCE: Page_3]

and age, indicating an increase of exposure in older individuals. We found differences between [SOURCE: Page_3]

males and females with the interaction with body size for Pb and Se and with age for Pb. [SOURCE: Page_3]

Nitrogen and carbon stable isotopes varied only marginally between individuals and were [SOURCE: Page_3]

poorly associated with MTEs concentrations, showing that trophic position might not explain [SOURCE: Page_3]

MTEs contamination for these populations. At the individual level, Hg, Pb, Se blood values [SOURCE: Page_3]

were repeatable over years. Further studies should concentrate on potential ecophysiological [SOURCE: Page_3]

effects to such exposure, especially since we recently evidenced that these populations of E. [SOURCE: Page_3]

orbicularis are highly exposed to organic contaminants, which can lead to synergistic effects. [SOURCE: Page_3]

Introduction [SOURCE: Page_4]

Metallic trace elements (MTEs) constitute a significant part of environmental pollution at a [SOURCE: Page_4]

global scale (Ullrich et al., 2001; Pacyna et al., 2007; Ali and Khan, 2019). MTEs can originate [SOURCE: Page_4]

from natural sources, mainly volcanism and soil and rock erosion, but most of the [SOURCE: Page_4]

environmental contamination originates from anthropogenic sources, especially from industry, [SOURCE: Page_4]

agriculture and gas combustion (Ullrich et al., 2001; Pacyna et al., 2007; Ali and Khan, 2019). [SOURCE: Page_4]

In addition to their production from various and widespread human activities, MTEs can be [SOURCE: Page_4]

transported by atmosphere and water, leading to an expansion of the contamination by leaching, [SOURCE: Page_4]

transportation by soil particles and deposition of airborne emissions (Pacyna et al., 2007; [SOURCE: Page_4]

Ullrich et al., 2001; Vareda et al., 2019). Therefore aquatic environments constitute sinks for [SOURCE: Page_4]

MTEs, where the local physicochemical characteristics of the water and the sediments (pH, [SOURCE: Page_4]

redox potential, salinity, organic matters) can influence their bioavailability (Deb and [SOURCE: Page_4]

Fukushima, 1999; Zhang et al., 2014; Väänänen et al., 2018). In addition, in these ecosystems, [SOURCE: Page_4]

the methylation of mercury (Hg) by Fe- and sulfate-reducing bacteria enhances the uptake and [SOURCE: Page_4]

toxicity of Hg for organisms (Driscoll et al., 2013). [SOURCE: Page_4]

All the MTEs have a potential for toxicity at certain concentrations, still, some of them play [SOURCE: Page_4]

essential roles in the physiological functions of organisms, such as copper (Cu), iron (Fe), [SOURCE: Page_4]

manganese (Mn), selenium (Se) and zinc (Zn) (Grillitsch and Schiesari, 2010). In turn, [SOURCE: Page_4]

cadmium (Cd), mercury (Hg), lead (Pb) do not participate in molecular or physiological [SOURCE: Page_4]

processes and have detrimental effects on organisms (Grillitsch and Schiesari, 2010; Ali and [SOURCE: Page_4]

Khan, 2019). These non-essential elements, Cd, Hg, Pb can bioaccumulate with increasing age [SOURCE: Page_4]

in regularly polluted environments (Burger, 2008; Lavoie et al., 2013; Pain et al., 2019). [SOURCE: Page_4]

Furthermore, Hg has the specificity to biomagnify across the trophic chain, leading to higher [SOURCE: Page_4]

levels of concentrations for species in top trophic positions (Lavoie et al., 2013). Exposure to [SOURCE: Page_4]

these non-essential elements potentially increases oxidative stress (Ortiz-Santaliestra et al., [SOURCE: Page_5]

2018; Soldatini et al., 2020), endocrine system disruption (Burger, 2008; Tan et al., 2009; [SOURCE: Page_5]

Grillitsch and Schiesari, 2010; Tartu et al., 2013; Meyer et al., 2013; Pain et al., 2019) and [SOURCE: Page_5]

immune dysfunctions (Grillitsch and Schiesari, 2010). These effects could also be [SOURCE: Page_5]

transgenerational as maternal transfer of MTEs to the eggs has been shown in different species [SOURCE: Page_5]

such as birds and reptiles (Yu et al., 2011; B. C. Hopkins et al., 2013; Van Dyke et al., 2014; [SOURCE: Page_5]

Ackerman et al., 2016; Nilsen et al., 2020). [SOURCE: Page_5]

Stable isotopes can be useful to assess with MTE body burden (Le Croizier et al., 2016) as they [SOURCE: Page_5]

can inform on foraging habitat and position in the trophic chain (Post, 2002). Nitrogen stable [SOURCE: Page_5]

isotope values (δ15N) increase with trophic position because of the enrichment in 15N with the [SOURCE: Page_5]

diet in animals (Peterson and Fry, 1987; Post, 2002). In the case of carbon stable isotopes, the [SOURCE: Page_5]

ratio between 13C and 12C (δ13C) is used to determine the origin of carbon by the primary [SOURCE: Page_5]

producers in trophic chains (Peterson and Fry, 1987; Post, 2002). Thus, increased levels of Hg [SOURCE: Page_5]

are usually related to higher levels of δ15N, illustrating the biomagnification of Hg, as shown in [SOURCE: Page_5]

seabirds and freshwater fishes (Lucia et al., 2016; Liu et al., 2018; Mills et al., 2020; Jouanneau [SOURCE: Page_5]

et al., 2022), as well as in reptiles (Bergeron et al., 2007; Hopkins et al., 2013; Lemaire et al., [SOURCE: Page_5]

2021a). [SOURCE: Page_5]

Reptiles are particularly vulnerable to habitat degradation (Cox et al., 2022) and are considered [SOURCE: Page_5]

as relevant bioindicators of pollution (Sparling et al., 2010; Weir et al., 2010; Ortiz-Santaliestra [SOURCE: Page_5]

et al., 2018; Silva et al., 2020). While they are still largely understudied in ecotoxicological [SOURCE: Page_5]

studies, MTEs are among the most monitored contaminants for this group. Long lifespan, [SOURCE: Page_5]

spatial fidelity and low metabolism are typical characteristics of chelonian (turtles and tortoises) [SOURCE: Page_5]

that make them relevant to address long-term and local contamination of the environment. [SOURCE: Page_5]

Mercury levels in diffeent tissues exhibit strong correlation in turtle species, allowing the use [SOURCE: Page_6]

of red blood cells from minimal invasive blood samples (Hopkins et al., 2013). In addition, [SOURCE: Page_6]

analysis of MTEs in red blood cells reflects the levels that circulate in the organism and that [SOURCE: Page_6]

can thus impact organs and physiological functions (Hopkins et al., 2013). It also allows us to [SOURCE: Page_6]

assess recent contamination when compared to scales that represent long-term and chronic [SOURCE: Page_6]

exposure (Turnquist et al., 2011). [SOURCE: Page_6]

Freshwater turtles are particularly relevant models to grasp local contamination of aquatic [SOURCE: Page_6]

ecosystems. Previous assessments on MTEs levels in red-eared sliders, common snapping [SOURCE: Page_6]

turtles, Iberian pond turtles and European pond turtles have shown that local sources of [SOURCE: Page_6]

contamination or environmental characteristics were important factors explaining MTEs levels [SOURCE: Page_6]

in these species (Turnquist et al., 2011; Yu et al., 2011; Hopkins et al., 2013; Beau et al., 2019; [SOURCE: Page_6]

Ortiz-Santaliestra et al., 2019). However, assessing the bioaccumulation through the lifespan [SOURCE: Page_6]

of individuals was generally restricted to comparison between age classes (juveniles vs. adults) [SOURCE: Page_6]

in most of these studies. Long-term population monitoring provides a unique opportunity to [SOURCE: Page_6]

address determinants of exposure. We studied MTEs in two neighboring populations of E. [SOURCE: Page_6]

orbicularis in the Camargue wetlands, monitored by capture-mark-recapture since 1976 [SOURCE: Page_6]

(Olivier, 2002; Olivier et al., 2010). Only limited exchanges exist between these populations. [SOURCE: Page_6]

The area includes several potential sources of heavy metal pollution: irrigation by the Rhône [SOURCE: Page_6]

River (Dendievel et al., 2020; Ferrand et al., 2012) and the vicinity of the industrial zone of [SOURCE: Page_6]

Fos-sur-Mer on the other side of the Rhône River (Austruy et al., 2019). Our long-term [SOURCE: Page_6]

monitoring allows us to examine the influence of specific traits such as age and sex in two [SOURCE: Page_6]

contrasted sites (irrigation vs drainage areas). Recent studies have pointed that this species [SOURCE: Page_6]

appears to constitute a good bioindicator for chemical pollution (Burkart et al., 2021; Merleau [SOURCE: Page_6]

et al., 2024) with local variation between irrigation and drainage areas. However little is known [SOURCE: Page_6]

about exposure levels of MTEs for this vulnerable species (Beau et al., 2019). We determined [SOURCE: Page_7]

the blood concentrations of 14 MTEs (Ag, As, Cd, Co, Cr, Cu, Fe, Hg, Mn, Ni, Pb, Se, V, and [SOURCE: Page_7]

Zn) over three years (2018 to 2020) in 257 individuals, allowing us to investigate inter- and [SOURCE: Page_7]

intra-individual variations in MTE levels. We also measured two isotopes (δ13C and δ15N) to [SOURCE: Page_7]

test for trophic differences during the same period. We examined the following questions: [SOURCE: Page_7]

1) How the population, year and individual traits (sex, body size, age) shape MTE [SOURCE: Page_7]

contamination level? We expect significant variation among habitats with higher MTE values [SOURCE: Page_7]

in the drainage area. We also expect higher MTE concentrations in older individuals, especially [SOURCE: Page_7]

for Hg and Pb. [SOURCE: Page_7]

2) Do stable isotopes (δ15N and δ13C) correlate with MTE? Notably, are the trophic position or [SOURCE: Page_7]

the primary producers responsible for inter-individual differences in Hg and Pb levels? [SOURCE: Page_7]

3) Are MTE and stable isotope values in the blood similar among years at the individual level? [SOURCE: Page_7]

We expect MTE levels to fluctuate within the individuals across the years due to the variation [SOURCE: Page_7]

of environmental contamination. [SOURCE: Page_7]

### 1. Population monitoring and captures

The study area is situated in the Camargue wetland, in Southern France in the Natural Reserve [SOURCE: Page_7]

of the Tour du Valat (43°30’N, 4°40’E). The two populations are located in an area with [SOURCE: Page_7]

multiple sources of anthropogenic release of MTEs: the Rhône River, the industrial site of Fos- [SOURCE: Page_7]

sur-Mer and rice paddies (Ribeiro et al., 2005; Ferrand et al., 2012; Austruy et al., 2019; [SOURCE: Page_7]

Dendievel et al., 2020) but mainly differ by their hydrology (Olivier et al., 2010; Burkart et al., [SOURCE: Page_7]

2021; Merleau et al., 2024). The Esquineau wetlands are made up irrigation canals (whose water [SOURCE: Page_7]

is pumped from the Rhône) and associated marshes irrigated by this canal system (Burkart et [SOURCE: Page_7]

al., 2021; Merleau et al., 2024). The second site, the Faïsses, is crossed by drainage canals of [SOURCE: Page_7]

the Fumemorte basin, which irrigate the marshes in this area (Burkart et al., 2021; Merleau et [SOURCE: Page_8]

al., 2024). Using funnel traps or by hand, E. orbicularis were captured in canals and marshes [SOURCE: Page_8]

from the end of April to the end of July in 2018 and 2019 and from the beginning of May to the [SOURCE: Page_8]

beginning of August in 2020. As part of a long-term capture-mark-recapture program (1976- [SOURCE: Page_8]

2024), turtles are identified individually by notching the marginal and nuchal scales (Ficheux [SOURCE: Page_8]

et al., 2014; Olivier, 2002). We checked the traps daily and after being sampled in the laboratory [SOURCE: Page_8]

of the Tour du Valat, individuals were released on their exact site of capture (Fay et al., 2023; [SOURCE: Page_8]

Olivier et al., 2010). Captures of this protected species were authorized by French Departmental [SOURCE: Page_8]

Authorities (Permits: DREAL Cerfa 13616-01; N°13-2020-03-27-007). [SOURCE: Page_8]

### 2. Blood sampling

From 2018 to 2020, we collected 410 blood samples from 257 individuals (mass > 300g). We [SOURCE: Page_8]

collected 1.5 mL (< 1% of the individual body mass) from the dorsal coccygeal vein using a [SOURCE: Page_8]

previously heparinized Terumo syringe and a 25G needle. We centrifuged the samples to [SOURCE: Page_8]

retrieve red blood cells separately from plasma and the samples were then frozen at -18°C until [SOURCE: Page_8]

processing at the LIENSs laboratory of La Rochelle University (France). 143 individuals were [SOURCE: Page_8]

sampled once, 77 over two years and 37 over the three years of monitoring. Two individuals [SOURCE: Page_8]

were sampled twice in the same year in 2018. These samples were included in the general [SOURCE: Page_8]

analyses, but these two individuals were not included in intra-individual variation analysis. [SOURCE: Page_8]

Following the EU Directive 2010/63/EU requirements for animal experiments, the blood [SOURCE: Page_8]

sampling procedure was assessed by an Ethic Committee (Permit: APAFIS #17899- [SOURCE: Page_8]

### 3. Variables collected

Morphology [SOURCE: Page_9]

Using a precision scale (Mettler Toledo PB3001-S), turtles were weighed to the nearest g in the [SOURCE: Page_9]

laboratory and dorsal shell length (hereafter “carapace length”) was measured with a caliper [SOURCE: Page_9]

(cm) (Olivier et al., 2010). We estimated the body condition index with the residuals of a linear [SOURCE: Page_9]

regression of the log-transformed mass against the log-transformed carapace length with the [SOURCE: Page_9]

addition of sex as a control variable. As described by Castanet (1988) and Olivier (2002), we [SOURCE: Page_9]

counted the number of growth rings on the plastron scutes to determine the age in individuals [SOURCE: Page_9]

less than 5 years old. The age was known for 128 individuals including 78 females (age range: [SOURCE: Page_9]

5-43 years) and 50 males (age range: 4-25 years). The gravidity of the females was assessed by [SOURCE: Page_9]

pelvic palpation (Olivier, 2002; Beau et al., 2019). We recorded the presence of the eggs and [SOURCE: Page_9]

the number of palpations performed on a female by capture season. [SOURCE: Page_9]

Metallic Trace Elements analyses [SOURCE: Page_9]

Red blood cell aliquots were freeze-dried for 48h and homogenised. As described in Lemaire [SOURCE: Page_9]

et al. (2022), total Hg analyses were performed on aliquots weighing between 0.83 and 2.10 mg [SOURCE: Page_9]

using an atomic absorption spectrometer AMA-254 (Advanced Mercury Analyser-254; Altec) [SOURCE: Page_9]

with a limit of detection (LoD) of 0.1 ng. At least two replicates were analysed for each sample [SOURCE: Page_9]

until the relative standard deviation was <10%. Validation of the method was obtained by the [SOURCE: Page_9]

analysis of certified reference material (CRM) TORT-3 (Lobster hepatopancreas from the [SOURCE: Page_9]

National Research Council of Canada (NRCC) with certified Hg value: 0.292 ± 0.022 µg.g -1 [SOURCE: Page_9]

dw) at the beginning of the analyses and CRM DOLT-5 (Dogfish liver from the NRCC, with [SOURCE: Page_9]

certified Hg value: 0.44 ± 0.18 µg.g-1 dw). Following the protocol described in Bustamante et [SOURCE: Page_9]

al. (2008), all the other trace elements (Ag, As, Cd, Co, Cr, Cu, Fe, Mn, Ni, Pb, Se, V, and Zn) [SOURCE: Page_9]

were analysed with Inductively Coupled Plasma (ICP) 5800 VDV - Agilent Technologies and [SOURCE: Page_9]

Mass Spectrometry (Thermo Fisher Scientific Series II ICP-MS). Recoveries for Hg were [SOURCE: Page_10]

102.0±1.8% and 96.6±1.2% for TORT-3 and DOLT-5, respectively. Recoveries for the other [SOURCE: Page_10]

elements varied between 94% and 109% for TORT-3 and DOLT-5. The LoD for the different [SOURCE: Page_10]

elements analysed by ICP were 0.01 (Ag, Cd, Co, Cr, and Pb), 0.02 (Ni), 0.05 (Cu, Mn and Se), [SOURCE: Page_10]

0.1 (As), 0.2 (V) and 2 (Fe and Zn) µg.L-1. Trace element concentrations were further expressed [SOURCE: Page_10]

in µg.g-1 dry weight (dw). Hematocrit was not collected in this study but the average values for [SOURCE: Page_10]

E. orbicularis are usually 22-23% (Yilmaz and Tosunolu, 2010). [SOURCE: Page_10]

Stable isotope analyses [SOURCE: Page_10]

Carbon and nitrogen stable isotopes were quantified in aliquots of 0.3 to 0.5 mg of freeze-dried [SOURCE: Page_10]

red blood cells. The aliquots were processed with an elemental analyser (Flash 2000 or EA [SOURCE: Page_10]

Isolink, Thermo Scientific) coupled to a mass spectrometer (Delta V Plus with Conflo IV [SOURCE: Page_10]

Interface, Thermo Scientific). Results were expressed in the δ unit notation as deviations from [SOURCE: Page_10]

standards (Vienna Pee Dee Belemnite for δ13C and N$_2$ in air for δ15N) following the formula: [SOURCE: Page_10]

δ13C or δ15N = [(Rsample/Rstandard) - 1] x 1000, where R is 13C/12C or 15N/14N, respectively. [SOURCE: Page_10]

Two-point calibration was provided by standard of caffeine (USGS-61 & USGS-63). The [SOURCE: Page_10]

analytical precision was <0.10 ‰ for δ13C and <0.15 ‰ for δ15N (Thermo Scientific [SOURCE: Page_10]

specifications). We were able to determine stable isotope values of carbon and nitrogen in all [SOURCE: Page_10]

samples but two. [SOURCE: Page_10]

Statistical analyses [SOURCE: Page_10]

We conducted all statistical analyses with R software (4.2.2) and the following packages lme^4 [SOURCE: Page_10]

(Bates et al., 2022), emmeans (Lenth et al., 2023), DHARMa (Hartig, 2016) and rptR (Stoffel [SOURCE: Page_10]

et al., 2017). Models were progressively reduced through stepwise elimination of non- [SOURCE: Page_10]

significant variables (p > 0.05). Statistical values provided for non-significant variables are the [SOURCE: Page_10]

one obtained in the last step before their removal from the models. Pairwise comparisons were [SOURCE: Page_11]

conducted using Tukey post hoc tests in the emmeans package (Lenth et al., 2023). [SOURCE: Page_11]

Variation in ETM [SOURCE: Page_11]

We used LMMs (Linear Mixed Models) with individual identity as a random effect to control [SOURCE: Page_11]

for repeated sampling of individuals. We tested the effects of year, site, sex, carapace length, [SOURCE: Page_11]

body condition, and the interaction site x year on the concentration of Cu, Fe, Hg, Mn, Pb, Se [SOURCE: Page_11]

and Zn. Se concentrations were log-transformed to best fit the model assumptions. Because age [SOURCE: Page_11]

and body condition were strongly correlated, we also conducted the same LMMs by testing the [SOURCE: Page_11]

effect of age on the subset of known-aged individuals (n = 211). We also tested the effect of the [SOURCE: Page_11]

7 MTE abovementioned on the body condition, used as the response variable. The lme^4 package [SOURCE: Page_11]

(Bates et al., 2022) was used and model assumptions were checked by examining residual plots. [SOURCE: Page_11]

Variation in isotopic levels [SOURCE: Page_11]

We used LMMs (Linear Mixed Models) with individual identity as a random effect to control [SOURCE: Page_11]

for repeated sampling of individuals. We tested the effects of year, site, sex, carapace length, [SOURCE: Page_11]

Intra-individual variation analysis [SOURCE: Page_11]

To assess variations in individuals sampled multiple years, we compared groups of individuals [SOURCE: Page_11]

sampled by pair of years using paired t-test and one-way repeated measures ANOVA for the [SOURCE: Page_11]

individuals sampled during three years. The “repeatability” is thereafter defined by the stability [SOURCE: Page_11]

tests to assess the intra-individual variation between years, using the package rptR (Stoffel et [SOURCE: Page_12]

al., 2017). [SOURCE: Page_12]

### 1. Determinants of MTE concentrations

Among the 14 metallic trace elements examined, 7 were above the limit of detection: Cu, Fe, [SOURCE: Page_12]

Hg, Mn, Pb, Se, and Zn, and included in further analyses. Ag, As, Cd, Co, Cr, Ni, and V were [SOURCE: Page_12]

below the limit of detection in all or more than 80% of the samples analyzed in this study. We [SOURCE: Page_12]

found an effect of population on the concentrations of Hg, Pb, and Zn with higher contamination [SOURCE: Page_12]

in Esquineau for all three elements (Table 1, Fig. 1). However the effect for Pb concentrations [SOURCE: Page_12]

were mainly due to a few individuals in Esquineau, notably one individual having the maximum [SOURCE: Page_12]

concentration of 1 µg.g-1. The concentrations of Se were higher in Esquineau in 2018 but not [SOURCE: Page_12]

in 2019 and 2020 (Table 1, Fig. 1). We did not observe differences between the two sites for [SOURCE: Page_12]

the other MTEs (Cu, Fe, and Mn) (Table 1). We found an effect of the year of capture for each [SOURCE: Page_12]

MTE, but the differences between years depended on the MTE. Post-hoc tests showed that Cu, [SOURCE: Page_12]

Fe, Hg, Se, and Zn concentrations were the highest in 2018 compared to the two other years. [SOURCE: Page_12]

Fe, Se, and Zn levels were also higher in 2019 compared to 2020. Mn and Pb levels were [SOURCE: Page_12]

significantly lower in 2018 than in 2019 and 2020. Cu levels were significantly higher in 2019 [SOURCE: Page_12]

compared to the two other years. Mean concentrations depending on the year and the site are [SOURCE: Page_12]

presented in Supplementary Material. [SOURCE: Page_12]

We found effects of body size on Hg concentrations that were positively correlated with [SOURCE: Page_12]

carapace length whereas those of Mn were negatively correlated (Table 1, Fig. 2A, 2B). We [SOURCE: Page_12]

found an interaction between body size and sex only for Pb; while Pb concentrations [SOURCE: Page_12]

significantly increased with size for females they decreased for males (Fig. 2C). Se levels [SOURCE: Page_12]

increased with body size in males but not in females (Fig. 2D). Males had higher Cu levels than [SOURCE: Page_13]

females (Table 1). When considering known-aged individuals, Fe and Hg concentrations [SOURCE: Page_13]

increased in older individuals (Fig. 3A, 3B). Older females had higher Pb concentrations [SOURCE: Page_13]

whereas older males tended to have lower Pb ones (Fig 3C). This effect was still observed when [SOURCE: Page_13]

retrieving older females and considering the ages of the two sexes. We did not find an effect of [SOURCE: Page_13]

age on Cu, Mn, Se, and Zn concentrations. No MTE was related to the palpation of eggs in [SOURCE: Page_13]

females. Body condition as response variable was not correlated with MTE concentrations (all [SOURCE: Page_13]

p value > 0.09). [SOURCE: Page_13]

### 2. Relation with stable isotopes

We were able to determine stable isotope values of carbon and nitrogen in all samples but two. [SOURCE: Page_13]

We observed very few variations in stable isotope values: δ13C ranged from -29.19 to -23.45 ‰ [SOURCE: Page_13]

(mean ± SE = -27.27 ± 0.99 ‰) and δ15N ranged from 6.23 to 12.59 ‰ (mean ± SE = 8.59 ± [SOURCE: Page_13]

0.68 ‰). [SOURCE: Page_13]

We found significant but very slight differences for the δ13C values between the two populations [SOURCE: Page_13]

(Fig. 4A, LMER model: 2 = 9.417, df = 1, p = 0.002; mean ± SE: -27.45 ± 0.81‰ and -26.97 [SOURCE: Page_13]

### 3. Repeated measures and individual variation

Individuals sampled during the three years (n=36) exhibited significant variations in their Fe [SOURCE: Page_14]

and Mn levels (Fe^2018-2019-2020: ANOVA, F(2,108) = 11.242, p < 0.001; Mn^2018-2019-2020: ANOVA, [SOURCE: Page_14]

F(2,108) = 4.771, p = 0.01). Individuals sampled in 2018 and 2019 (n = 20) exhibited higher [SOURCE: Page_14]

concentrations of Pb in 2019 (paired t-test: t = -3.02, df = 19, p = 0.007). Individuals sampled [SOURCE: Page_14]

in 2019 and 2020 (n = 29), and in 2018 and 2020 (n = 29) exhibited higher Fe and Se [SOURCE: Page_14]

concentrations in 2019 and 2018 than in 2020 (paired t-tests: Se^2019-2020, t = 3.018, df=28, [SOURCE: Page_14]

p<0.01; Se^2018-2020, t = 2.96, df = 28, p< 0.01; Fe^2019-2020, t = 2.951, df=28, p<0.01; Fe^2018-2020, t [SOURCE: Page_14]

= 5.127, df = 28, p< 0.001). Three MTEs remained statistically constant over the years: Cu, Hg, [SOURCE: Page_14]

and Zn. [SOURCE: Page_14]

We found high intra-individual repeatability across time for Hg, Pb, and Se for each pair of [SOURCE: Page_14]

years or for all three years (Fig 5). The repeatability coefficient was respectively between 0.6 [SOURCE: Page_14]

and 0.9; 0.6 and 0.8; 0.57 and 0.84 depending on the pairs of years (see Supplementary [SOURCE: Page_14]

Material). In turn, the intra-individual repeatability was low and nonsignificant for Cu and Zn, [SOURCE: Page_14]

as well as for Fe, except in 2018-20219, and Mn except for 2018-2020 and 2018-2019-2020. [SOURCE: Page_14]

Discussion [SOURCE: Page_14]

Despite the ubiquity and toxicity of MTEs, factors influencing their levels in wild populations [SOURCE: Page_14]

are still insufficiently understood, notably freshwater turtle species (but see Turnquist et al., [SOURCE: Page_14]

2011; Yu et al., 2011; Hopkins et al., 2013; Guillot et al., 2018; Ortiz-Santaliestra et al., 2019). [SOURCE: Page_14]

Our study is the first to characterize a large set of MTEs, including both essential and non- [SOURCE: Page_14]

essential elements, in the blood of E. orbicularis and to investigate the environmental and [SOURCE: Page_14]

individual factors governing circulating levels. We were able to detect 7 MTEs out of the 14 [SOURCE: Page_14]

that were analyzed. Most of them were essential trace elements (Cu, Fe, Mn, Se, and Zn). [SOURCE: Page_14]

Variation in MTEs were primarily driven by individual characteristics (age, sex) and [SOURCE: Page_15]

population. [SOURCE: Page_15]

Occurrence of trace elements [SOURCE: Page_15]

Two trace elements of major concern were detected in all the samples: Hg and Pb. Blood levels [SOURCE: Page_15]

of Hg in our study were in the range of the concentrations found in other freshwater reptiles. In [SOURCE: Page_15]

several freshwater turtle species, studies found average blood Hg levels ranging from 0.02 to [SOURCE: Page_15]

4.35 µg.g-1 dw (Hopkins et al., 2013; Meyer et al., 2014, Slimani et al., 2018, Van Dyke et al., [SOURCE: Page_15]

2017). Similar concentrations were found in whole blood of wild caimans, from 0.07 to 2.19 [SOURCE: Page_15]

µg.g-1 dw (Lemaire et al., 2021a; 2022). However, our results were below the blood [SOURCE: Page_15]

concentrations of populations of Mauremys leprosa (3.37 to 8.83 µg.g-1 dw) living in a region [SOURCE: Page_15]

with former mine activities (Ortiz-Santaliestra et al., 2019) and Chelydra serpentina (0.05 to [SOURCE: Page_15]

24.95 µg.g-1 dw) living downstream a former industrial Hg releasing (Hopkins et al., 2013). [SOURCE: Page_15]

Previous studies in Brenne, France showed two or three fold higher levels in claws of E. [SOURCE: Page_15]

orbicularis compared to our results (Beau et al., 2019; Guillot et al., 2018). This difference, [SOURCE: Page_15]

also observed in other species (Schneider et al., 2011) could be explained by the high [SOURCE: Page_15]

bioaccumulation of Hg in keratinized tissues due to the affinity of their protein to metalloid [SOURCE: Page_15]

compounds (Grillitsch and Schiesari, 2010). Pb contamination is an issue in the Camargue [SOURCE: Page_15]

wetlands due to the use of lead shots for hunting for decades (Hoffmann, 1960; Pain et al., [SOURCE: Page_15]

2019). However, little oxidation of shots seems to occur (Pain, 1991) and Pb was rarely [SOURCE: Page_15]

detected in water analyses done by the National Reserve of Camargue (Cheiron, 2019; Cheiron [SOURCE: Page_15]

and Bricault, 2021, 2020) probably explaining the similar levels to the ones of freshwater [SOURCE: Page_15]

reptiles living in non-contaminated areas (Ortiz-Santaliestra et al., 2019; Lemaire et al., 2022). [SOURCE: Page_15]

Interestingly, As, Co, Ni, and V in the blood of of E. orbicularis were below the detection limit [SOURCE: Page_15]

of the method although these MTEs were detected in water samples from the main drainage [SOURCE: Page_15]

canal of the area in which the canals of the site of Faisses flow (Cheiron, 2019; Cheiron and [SOURCE: Page_16]

Bricault, 2021, 2020). Considering organotropism, levels of certain MTEs could be higher in [SOURCE: Page_16]

internal organs in E. orbicularis (e.g., Cd in the kidneys) than was what found in this study, [SOURCE: Page_16]

especially for trace elements non-detected here. [SOURCE: Page_16]

Variation among site and year of capture [SOURCE: Page_16]

We found that Hg, Pb, Se, and Zn varied among the two populations with Esquineau showing [SOURCE: Page_16]

the highest levels for each of these MTEs, except in 2020 for Se. A difference in trophic position [SOURCE: Page_16]

between these two populations is unlikely to explain the difference. Using δ15N to assess the [SOURCE: Page_16]

trophic level we found little variation (coefficient of variation: 8%) and individuals sampled [SOURCE: Page_16]

were distributed in one to two trophic levels only (Post, 2002). Although environmental levels [SOURCE: Page_16]

were not measured in our study, several hypotheses can be made. Esquineau site is made up of [SOURCE: Page_16]

irrigation canals and marshes irrigated by them. Thus, the water irrigating the wetlands of this [SOURCE: Page_16]

site is directly pumped from the Rhône River, 8 km upstream. The difference in MTE levels is [SOURCE: Page_16]

probably due to the exposure and the contamination of the site ecosystem by the Rhône River [SOURCE: Page_16]

which flows through a valley with many industries upstream of the pumping point (Cheiron, [SOURCE: Page_16]

2019; Cheiron and Bricault, 2021, 2020). Regarding Hg levels, another hypothesis could [SOURCE: Page_16]

explain that turtles from Esquineau exhibited higher levels: in this site, marshes have more dry- [SOURCE: Page_16]

out periods, which can increase Hg methylation in marshes (Feng et al., 2014). E. orbicularis [SOURCE: Page_16]

exhibited higher Hg levels in their claws in sites that had been year-long drained the latest (Beau [SOURCE: Page_16]

et al., 2019). Overall these results contrast with previous findings on legacy organic pollutants [SOURCE: Page_16]

and pesticides in the same populations where the drainage site is the more exposed (Burkart et [SOURCE: Page_16]

al., 2021; Merleau et al., 2024). The higher Pb levels at the Esquineau site could also be due to [SOURCE: Page_16]

its proximity to a communal hunting ground where hunters do not comply with regulations on [SOURCE: Page_16]

the use of non-toxic ammunition (Mondain-Monval et al., 2017, 2020). [SOURCE: Page_16]

We found an effect of the year for every trace element quantified. Differences between years [SOURCE: Page_17]

were generally limited (coefficients of variation ranged from 2.2% for Fe to 17.6% for Se). [SOURCE: Page_17]

These variations were supported when considering individuals with multiple measures across [SOURCE: Page_17]

years. Therefore, they illustrate temporal variations in individual exposure, and could be due [SOURCE: Page_17]

for instance to potential variations in environmental levels to the primary producers of the [SOURCE: Page_17]

trophic chain, as explained by the inter-annual variations of δ13C. However, considering the low [SOURCE: Page_17]

turn-over of red blood cells in reptiles, measured trace element concentrations probably reflect [SOURCE: Page_17]

exposure over several months (Stacy et al., 2011). [SOURCE: Page_17]

Influence of sex, body size and age [SOURCE: Page_17]

Several MTEs (Hg, Mn, Pb, and Se) were positively correlated with body size, likely because [SOURCE: Page_17]

of a higher exposition through food, with larger individuals expected to consume more (Shine [SOURCE: Page_17]

et al., 1998). The correlation between carapace length and levels of these MTEs may reflect [SOURCE: Page_17]

bioaccumulation although blood levels result of recent integration rather than accumulation [SOURCE: Page_17]

over several years (Grillitsch and Schiesari, 2010). We found no relation between ETM and [SOURCE: Page_17]

individual body condition. Previous studies found an adverse effect of heavy metals, and Hg in [SOURCE: Page_17]

particular, in reptiles (Finger et al., 2017; Nilsen et al., 2017). In Mauremys leprosa, populations [SOURCE: Page_17]

with average Hg blood concentrations similar to ours showed a greater body condition [SOURCE: Page_17]

compared to those with higher levels of Hg (8.83 ± 1.88 µg.g-1 dw) (Ortiz-Santaliestra et al., [SOURCE: Page_17]

2019). In birds, a recent meta-analysis revealed that Hg burden and body condition are [SOURCE: Page_17]

essentially not related (Carravieri et al., 2022). However, our results are consistent with a [SOURCE: Page_17]

previous study that did not find a relationship between Hg burden in claws and body condition [SOURCE: Page_17]

in E. orbicularis (Beau et al., 2019). [SOURCE: Page_17]

We found a strong effect of age on Hg and Pb concentrations. Our results rely on red blood [SOURCE: Page_18]

cells, a dynamic tissue and thus give complementary information on the exposure to trace [SOURCE: Page_18]

elements during the life of the individuals. When blood levels of Hg increase, this element is [SOURCE: Page_18]

likely deposited in developing keratinized tissues such as scute layers and claws (Beau et al., [SOURCE: Page_18]

2019; Villa et al., 2019) and thus lower the blood levels. Older individuals having higher [SOURCE: Page_18]

circulating levels of Hg and Pb suggest that these individuals keep being more exposed, [SOURCE: Page_18]

presumably through feeding. Our long-term study systems allowed to show for the first time [SOURCE: Page_18]

that older females are more contaminated than older males, and even though our dataset is [SOURCE: Page_18]

limited for old males (maximum age for males: 25 years), this effect remained significant when [SOURCE: Page_18]

considering females under 25 years. The effect of age was also detected for Fe and could be [SOURCE: Page_18]

explained by a higher number of red blood cells in older individuals. This hypothesis should be [SOURCE: Page_18]

tested in further studies using hematocrit values. Increasing concentrations in larger and older [SOURCE: Page_18]

females compared to males could be explained by differential absorption between sex, or linked [SOURCE: Page_18]

to the reproduction. [SOURCE: Page_18]

We found higher Pb concentrations in females than males and this difference increased with [SOURCE: Page_18]

the individual ages. This result was counterintuitive and although not known for Pb in [SOURCE: Page_18]

freshwater turtles, has been already shown in other reptiles (Burger et al., 2004; Ciliberti et al., [SOURCE: Page_18]

2011). No differences in feeding habits between males and females were found in some [SOURCE: Page_18]

populations of E. orbicularis (Çiçek and Ayaz, 2011; Ziane et al., 2020), including in the [SOURCE: Page_18]

Camargue (Ottonello et al., 2005) but (Ducotterd et al., 2020) showed that males and females [SOURCE: Page_18]

consumed different prey species. Even if little is known about the toxicokinetics of trace [SOURCE: Page_18]

elements in reptiles, the higher concentration of Pb in females could also result from a [SOURCE: Page_18]

difference in physiology, notably in the absorption of the trace elements. We also observed a [SOURCE: Page_18]

significant difference between males and females for Cu, however the means for each sex were [SOURCE: Page_18]

extremely close. In turn, we did not observe sexual differences in the concentration of Hg, as [SOURCE: Page_19]

previously reported in Brenne for this species (Guillot et al., 2018; Beau et al., 2019). Maternal [SOURCE: Page_19]

transfer of trace elements in the eggs has been shown in reptiles (Nilsen et al., 2020; Lemaire [SOURCE: Page_19]

et al., 2021b) including freshwater turtles (Guirlet et al., 2008; Yu et al., 2011; W. A. Hopkins [SOURCE: Page_19]

et al., 2013). However, this potential maternal transfer is not always a sufficient excretion [SOURCE: Page_19]

pathway to observe differences in trace element concentrations between males and females [SOURCE: Page_19]

(Schneider et al., 2011; Yu et al., 2011; Lemaire et al., 2021a). [SOURCE: Page_19]

Essential MTEs (Cu, Fe, Mn, and Zn) were less impacted by individual factors than Hg and Pb, [SOURCE: Page_19]

which can be explained by a highly controlled regulation of these MTEs. Since they are [SOURCE: Page_19]

involved in biological mechanisms, there are pathways of regulation, involving different [SOURCE: Page_19]

components like metallothioneins to help maintain optimal concentrations of these MTEs for [SOURCE: Page_19]

cell physiology (Deb and Fukushima, 1999). Fe was the MTE with the lower variation [SOURCE: Page_19]

coefficient (4.4 %), which probably reflects a high regulation due to its crucial role in the [SOURCE: Page_19]

production of hemoglobin. Se exhibited the highest coefficient of variation among the samples [SOURCE: Page_19]

(92%), which raised some concern on its potential impacts. In several Vertebrate species, [SOURCE: Page_19]

including reptiles, seabirds, and mammals, Se has been shown to mitigate the toxic effects of [SOURCE: Page_19]

Hg (Cuvin-Aralar and Furness, 1991; Manceau et al., 2021). However, high dietary exposure [SOURCE: Page_19]

to Se (15 to 30 µg. g-1) in the yellow-bellied slider Trachemys scripta scripta is correlated with [SOURCE: Page_19]

detrimental effects on several parameters such as red blood cells, immunity (Haskins et al., [SOURCE: Page_19]

2017). In the American alligator Alligator mississipiensis, dietary exposure to 1000 and 2000 [SOURCE: Page_19]

µg.g-1 lead to glucocorticoid release and decreased body condition (Finger et al., 2017, 2018). [SOURCE: Page_19]

Patterns of covariation and individual repeatability [SOURCE: Page_20]

No significant correlations were found between MTEs except for Hg and δ13C. This correlation [SOURCE: Page_20]

is not common but was also found in fish species (Ofukany et al., 2014) or seabirds (e.g. [SOURCE: Page_20]

(Binkowski et al., 2021; Carravieri et al., 2014). It might be explained by a slight difference in [SOURCE: Page_20]

Hg levels in primary producers and differences in foraging strategies, as E. orbicularis exhibit [SOURCE: Page_20]

a very varied diet (Ducotterd et al., 2020). In large aquatics ecosystems, differences in carbon [SOURCE: Page_20]

stable isotopes have been found between littoral and pelagic food webs (France, 1995). δ13C is [SOURCE: Page_20]

a more sensitive measure than δ15N, only a change of 1 ‰ being necessary to change the level [SOURCE: Page_20]

of primary producers. We would have expected a correlation between Hg and δ15N considering [SOURCE: Page_20]

the biomagnification property of Hg, but the variation was limited in δ15N (6.23-12.59 ‰). [SOURCE: Page_20]

Our study allowed us to access the intra-individual variations of the levels of MTEs along time. [SOURCE: Page_20]

No variation between years was found for Cu, Hg, and Zn in the subset of individuals that were [SOURCE: Page_20]

sampled multiple times. We also found high and significant repeatability across years for Hg [SOURCE: Page_20]

showing that these individuals tended to be exposed consistently to Hg over time. The intra- [SOURCE: Page_20]

individual repeatability was also high and significant for Pb and for Se. At the group scale, [SOURCE: Page_20]

MTEs values could be different enough to be statistically detectable; yet the concentrations [SOURCE: Page_20]

were similar throughout the years for most individuals, exposing them to potential chronic [SOURCE: Page_20]

effects. Even if concentrations were rather low, being exposed to such levels of Hg and Pb for [SOURCE: Page_20]

several decades could be detrimental to the individuals. Fe levels seemed affected by external [SOURCE: Page_20]

factors that impact individuals globally, illustrating the highly conserved nature of the [SOURCE: Page_20]

regulation of this element (Kaplan and Ward, 2013). Cu and Zn, on the other side, seemed to [SOURCE: Page_20]

be less highly regulated than Fe, as we found no difference in their levels in groups sampled for [SOURCE: Page_20]

a pair of years but low and non-significant repeatability at the individual scale. [SOURCE: Page_20]

Conclusion [SOURCE: Page_21]

We demonstrate that habitat plays a role in exposure to the most toxic MTEs (Hg and Pb) with [SOURCE: Page_21]

the irrigation site exhibiting the highest levels. Our results also highlight the effect of individual [SOURCE: Page_21]

characteristics (size, sex, age) on MTE blood burden, however MTE contamination were poorly [SOURCE: Page_21]

related to stable isotope values. Our work is also the first to detect a differential effect of the [SOURCE: Page_21]

sex depending on the size and the age on the circulating levels of Pb in a freshwater turtle [SOURCE: Page_21]

species. Remarkably, blood levels of Hg, Pb, and Se are highly reproducible at the intra- [SOURCE: Page_21]

individual level over sampled years. Our study unequivocally supports the need of long-term [SOURCE: Page_21]

population monitoring to better understand i) the temporal intra-individual variation of MTE [SOURCE: Page_21]

burden and ii) the influence of age on different MTEs. Future work should focus on the [SOURCE: Page_21]

toxicokinetics of MTEs in E. orbicularis, and on the effects of such exposure, particularly on [SOURCE: Page_21]

physiology, reproduction and population dynamics. [SOURCE: Page_21]
