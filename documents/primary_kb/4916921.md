Results [SOURCE: Page_18]

• Yield increased as energy input increased : land and energy inputs are substitutable • Energy use efficiency decreased as energy input increased • The optimism regarding high-tech solutions to feed the world is challenged by their very low energy use efficiency. • Despite slightly better values for EROI, heated greenhouse systems also had a very low energy use efficiency. • Energy saving systems allow lower energy use for similar (area-based) yield, resulting in higher energy use efficiency compared to classic heated greenhouses, but still lower efficiency compared to unheated systems. [SOURCE: Page_18]

IFSA 2024, Trapani                                                              p. 17 01 July 2024 / Antonin Pépin [SOURCE: Page_18]

Implications [SOURCE: Page_19]

• Land and energy are critical points of a transition towards more sustainable farming systems  major challenge to develop systems with satisfactory yields and a limited energy use. • The (area-based) yield should not be looked at without energy efficiency indicators. • Tomatoes in winter ?  The transition of the farming systems is connected to the transition of the food system. • Outdoor production or unheated greenhouses have lower yields compared to the most productive heated greenhouses or plant factories, but they need little energy and are more energy efficient.  In a context where energy becomes more expensive, scarce while still contributing to climate change, those low-tech systems, including small organic farming, may be promising transition pathways, calling for more research and development effort in this direction.
