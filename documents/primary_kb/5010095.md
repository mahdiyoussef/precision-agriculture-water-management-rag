Abstract We investigate an optimal control problem of crop irrigation with non-autonomous and non-smooth dynamics. Depending on contexts and objectives, several formulations associated to different constraints and criteria can be derived. Our work aims at providing optimal feedback solutions for these problems by deriving and analyzing the optimality necessary conditions. To this end, we assemble the different problems into a common formulation, and we carry out a dedicated way of handling state constraints. We show that all optimal irrigation strategies belong to a family of simple parameterized time-varying feedback controls, independently of the context and objective, and suitable for computational purposes. 1     Introduction The development of agriculture plays a significant role in the worldwide management of natural resources. Among all the consequent challenges, the management of water resources is critical especially in areas where the scarce precipitations need to be more and more supported by irrigation. It is possible to save water through different agronomic and technological approaches, such as selecting stress resilient species, upgrading equipment performances, improving irrigation scheduling. Practices based on enhancing irrigation planning [24], coupled with a framework of deficit irrigation [8, 20] show promising potential to increase efficiency. Numerous studies tackle the agricultural water management by incorporating modeling tools, such as complex numerical models to describe precisely the physical processes of plant growth [1, 4, 13]. They typically present a detailed structure for predefined crop or regional context [17, 30], and contain significant constraints due to biological requirements. Their complexity implies computational limitations, it is thus reasonable to allow a more parsimonious description in order to improve flexibility and analytical capacity. To this end, irrigation planning is seen as a control problem, and using advanced control theory show good potential to improve efficiency in agriculture [7, 21]. Some studies have explored model predictive control [6, 22, 25] targeting adaptive and robust strategies regarding unknown entries like weather or model uncertainties, through numerical simulations. In a more deterministic approach towards analytical strategies, some studies addressed the irrigation problem using optimal control tools [2, 16, 18, 26]. These works explore models based on simple dynamical systems and decision making through only a few variables, and re- quire a limited number of hypotheses on the physical processes. According to their various assumptions and objectives some models consider more or less detailed soil water balance and more or less constrained contexts. The desired benefit of using analytical models is to aim for strategies depicting optimal irrigation thanks to time- varying feedback controls suitable for the low number of model inputs and state variables that are related to quantities accessible in real world. Several major agronomic objectives match with a formulation of a constrained optimization problem : maximizing the biomass production with a water availability constraint, minimizing the consumption of water to reach a yield target, maximizing a financial cost related to production sales and water expenses, avoiding harmful stress due to critically low level of soil moisture. In this work, we investigate these problems with a unified methodological point of view, and we employ a model that we call Controlled Crop Irrigation (CCI) model, inspired from [2, 14, 19]. This model is prone to analytical optimal control techniques (even though it presents non-smooth features) and is thought to possess enough physical sense to describe the order-1 biophysical processes, especially for greenhouse application. The various objectives discussed previously lead to the formulation of three optimization problems (according to three different couples of criterion and target, all fulfilling a state constraint) that we study in a unified procedure. In order to look for a common structure of the optimal irrigation strategies in terms of feedback, we shall establish and derive the optimality necessary conditions. There exists various ways to deal with state constraints ([11, 23]), but in our study we introduce an original ”compensation technique”, and we work out an equivalent problem formulation without state constraint on which we can apply a standard unconstrained Pontryagin Maximum Principle [5]. In Section 2, we provide a description of the CCI model and its hypotheses, then we present the different criteria and targets that we will study and we formalize their associated optimal control problems. After explaining the com- pensation technique, that we use, leading to equivalent problem formulations freed from state constraints, we show in Section 3 some necessary properties of optimal solutions. Then we apply in Section 4 the non-smooth Pontryagin Maximum Principle and we derive the optimality necessary conditions to finally get optimal structures of irrigation strategies. [SOURCE: Page_3]

5    Conclusion We dealt with a family of optimal control problems, and we showed, using dedicated tools (in particular an applica- tion of the extended bang-bang principle), that optimal irrigation strategies for these problems share the same structure of optimal feedback. These strategies can be interpreted as threshold-based strategies, where the values of thresholds correspond to non-differentiable points of the dynamics. This result allows to compute numerically the optimal solu- tions as a simple low dimensional optimization of the parameters of the structured optimal policies, hence enabling large numerical model exploration. Such threshold-based irrigation strategies can also be tested on more details models (for which the analytic study of the optimal feedback strategies would not be possible), and are suited to closed-loop experimental implementation, typically using soil moisture sensors.
