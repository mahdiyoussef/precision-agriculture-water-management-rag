Will the groundwater resources of the Kunene and Omusati regions (Namibia) be able to meet water demand in the context of climate change in 2050? [SOURCE: Page_1]

Abstract. [SOURCE: Page_1]

The Kunene and Omusati regions, northern Namibia, are two contrasted areas in terms of geology, hydrogeology and economical development. The Kunene region extends from the ephemeral Ugab river in the south to the perennial Kunene river which forms the Angolan border. It is a particularly arid area with rainfall ranging from less than 50 mm in the west to 300 mm in the east, whose landscape is shaped by a rugged geology resulting from the break-up of the Gondwana supercontinent. Its eastern neighbour, the Omusati region is part of the Cuvelaï-Etosha basin. Getting more precipitation (300-500 mm), it is a flat area composed of sedimentary rocks with detrital material. At the border between the two regions stand the dolomite formation of the Otavi fold belt. A recent study by the French Geological Survey has gathered and interpreted local information to issue thematic maps in the field of aquifer recharge and groundwater use. [SOURCE: Page_1]

Both regions are facing serious water scarcity. In Kunene region, the population is sparse and very rural, and the economy is based on livestock farming (pop. Density 2021: 0.9/km^2). The recharge is low (around 0,5% of precipitation) and water supply of cities as Khorixas, Opuwo or Outjo is problematic. [SOURCE: Page_1]

In the Omusati region, the population density is ten times higher (10,1/km^2). The region is semi arid and the recharge is estimated at 6% of precipitation. However soil salinity and underground geochemistry degrade the quality of the infiltrated water. In most of the area, groundwater is not suitable for human consumption and water supply is done through a surface water canal coming from the Calueque Dam in Angola. [SOURCE: Page_1]

Climate projections from CORDEX Africa were applied to compute the future evolution of potential groundwater recharge. In the mid-term (2041-2070), CORDEX future projections for mean annual precipitation and Potential Evapotranspiration would lead to a limited recharge increase (lower than 2 mm) along the coastal regions of Kunene compared to present situation, with locally more substantial increase for alluvial formations. The rest of the study zone could experience potential recharge decrease of between 0.5 and 4 mm for Omusati and less than 3 mm southeast of Kunene, In the long term (2071-2100), projected recharge is a general decrease on the two regions. [SOURCE: Page_1]

In parallel, the water demand will increase following several factors: demography (population will be 173 000 in Kunene and 288 000 in Omusati by 2050), urban infrastructure, rural domestic and non domestic water demand, irrigation, industry and tourism, environment and wildlife requirement. [SOURCE: Page_1]

The thematic maps provide an overview of the water resources situation in these two regions at horizon 2050. The comparison of water demand and climate projections is highlighting sectors of [SOURCE: Page_1]

tension, mainly around urban areas. Future mining exploitation projects which are blooming in the regions will require quantities of water which cannot be supplied from groundwater resources. [SOURCE: Page_2]
