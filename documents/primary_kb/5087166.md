A robust adaptive observer for a class of nonlinear time-varying systems with application to a crop fertigation model M.G. Dadjo^1,3 A. Rapaport^1 , J. Harmand^2 , R. Ushirobira^3 and D. Efimov^3 May 27, 2025 [SOURCE: Page_1]

Abstract. We propose an adaptive observer that can estimate both the state and parameters for a class of nonlinear time-varying systems, for which the regressor depends on the known input-output signals and all the unmeasured states. We also allow state disturbances and measurements that are corrupted by noise. We use the Lyapunov function method to prove that the estimation error dynamics fulfills the state-independent input-to-output stability property with unknown inputs. These results are motivated and illustrated with a three-dimensional crop irrigation model. [SOURCE: Page_1]

Key words. State and parameters estimation; Adaptive observer; SIIOS-stability; Crop fertigation model. [SOURCE: Page_1]

1    Introduction Monitoring and supervising controlled systems often requires complete knowledge of the state vector at any time. However, in many cases, the available sensors cannot provide real-time access to the full state of the system for many reasons, such as economic factors (sensors are too expensive) or technical constraints (noisy environment or lack of reliable technology, for instance, nitrogen measurements in agriculture). Since only a part of the state vector (called the observation or output) is measured, the goal of an observer is to estimate the other part of the state vector that is not available with measurements. Historically, Kalman first tackled this problem in a linear stochastic framework, leading to the so-called Kalman filter [18], and Luenberger solved it for the case of deterministic linear systems [22]. Nonlinear systems describe most physical systems, and there is no systematic method for constructing observers in a general nonlinear setting. For example, an observer was recently designed in [16] for a class of non-autonomous state-affine uncertain nonlinear systems with sampled output measurements based on a high-gain approach. In [32], a novel chain observer was designed for a class of Lipschitz nonlinear systems. Observer for dynamic systems remains a central challenge in control theory, especially when system dynamics are nonlinear and parameters are partially unknown. The estimator design problem becomes even more difficult for time-dependent dynamics or in the presence of exogenous perturbations. In this context, observers for particular classes of nonlinear models have been proposed and developed to estimate the state and the parameters simultaneously in real-time, coping with some uncertainty in the model. Such observers are called adaptive, and many contributions are available in the literature; see, for instance, [1, 3, 25, 6]. However, most of these approaches either assume complete knowledge of the system parameters or are limited to time-invariant systems and rarely investigate the effect of perturbations. The problem of simultaneous reconstruction of unmeasured state variables and unknown parameters, with both disturbances and measurement perturbations for a class of nonlinear time-varying systems, is considered in this work. For this purpose, adaptive observers have already been proposed in the literature in linear and nonlinear frameworks, starting from earlier works [23, 24], where two main families of design techniques have been developed when the unknown parameters appear in the derivative of the output, or when it appears first in higher order derivatives, this later case requires additional filters to compensate the high relative degree obstruction. In particular, a conceptual approach to the synthesis of adaptive state observers is proposed in [33] for linear time-varying or state-affine nonlinear systems (in the former case, the regressor is a time-varying matrix of known signals, whereas in the latter case, the regressor can depend on the input and output). The core idea is to incorporate an additional filter and an auxiliary term that is time-varying and proportional to the time derivative of the estimated parameter vector. Then, under persistent excitation, this approach ensures exponential convergence of state and parameter estimation errors to zero. It is worth highlighting that an important restriction for a majority of these results is the requirement of dependence of the regressor (i.e., the gain function that multiplies the vector of unknown parameters) on measured input and output signals only, which was solved in [11] for the cases of linear and nonlinear parameterization. The system considered in [11] is a specific class of time-invariant nonlinear systems in which the regressor depends partially on the unmeasured state. The proposed adaptive observer is primarily based on the high-gain approach from [14], as well as the approach of [33], where the corrective term is divided into two components: one using high-gain state observers and the other updating the unknown parameters as described in [33]. Non- autonomous nonlinear dynamics and noisy measurements have not been addressed so far in the literature, even though many practical adaptive observer problems exhibit these features. Other challenges are the need to analyze the estimation error in the presence of state disturbances and measurement noises, where only special cases were studied [10], without extensions to time-varying systems. Motivated by the above considerations, the present work makes the following key contributions. A new adaptive observer for a class of non-autonomous nonlinear dynamics with unknown inputs is proposed, for which the regressor depends not only on the known input-output signals but also on the unmeasured state, and the measurements are noisy. The analysis uses the theory of input-to-state stability (ISS) and other related properties. A Lyapunov function is synthesized to prove that the error dynamics is state-independent input-to-output stable from unknown inputs, relying on a condition of persistent excitation of the control variables. Under some constraints, these conditions are formulated in terms of linear matrix inequalities (LMIs). This work has been motivated by a concrete problem of crop irrigation. In recent work, we addressed the problem of minimizing the amount of water delivered during a season while satisfying the viability constraint [9]. Usually, soil humidity and crop biomass can be measured or estimated online [19]. However, soil nitrogen content knowledge is often much more challenging, while it is crucial for growth prediction [31]. These variables’ dynamics are nonlinear, time- varying, and rely on several parameters that must be estimated for each soil-crop system [5]. Moreover, the available measurements are often noisy, and the state dynamics contain uncertain external inputs. We show here how our adaptive estimation approach applies to a crop fertigation model, which falls outside the scope of previous works due to its unique characteristics. To rigorously reconstruct unavailable nitrogen, preliminary research was conducted in [7]. The proofs of results presented in the latter are elaborated in this work for an enhanced model to address agronomic significance. Comparisons with the high-gain approach used in [11] prove that our proposed adaptive observer offers better robustness. The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the preliminary concepts and notations. The considered class of nonlinear systems and the general problem formulation are presented in Section 3. The proposed adaptive observer is developed in Section 4, which is divided into three subsections. Sub- section 4.1 describes the structure of the observer. Subsection 4.2 addresses the ISS-Lyapunov stability analysis of the estimation errors. Subsection 4.3 shows how the observer design problem can be recast as an LMI feasibility problem. Section 5 discusses a crop irrigation model, while Subsection 5.1 focuses on synthesizing an adaptive observer tailored for crop fertigation, aiming at reconstructing both un- known constant parameters and nitrogen content. Simulation results and performance comparisons with other approaches, conducted on a fertigation application to illustrate the robustness of our method, are presented in Section 6, and concluding remarks are given in Section 7. 7     Conclusion This work proposed an adaptive observer for a class of nonlinear time-varying systems with disturbances and measurement noises, extending existing results by considering a generic and complex framework. The originality of this contribution is that the regressor depends on the unmeasured states, with time dependency on the system. The analysis of convergence and robustness of this type of adaptive observer used the SIIOS-Lyapunov function method. Using numerical simulations, a crop irrigation model was used to illustrate the observer’s efficiency. Our motivation to consider this estimation problem is related to the environmental risk of irrigation with the addition of nitrogen, the excess of which can contaminate the soil and pollute the groundwater. In recent work, we solved the problem of minimizing the total amount of water for irrigation, which guarantees the maximum biomass at the harvest date [8]. Optimal solutions are expressed as feedback depending on the nitrogen in the soil, which a priori is unavailable. Another difficulty is related to the various uncertainties in the model parameters. Applying our proposed recursive algorithm for the joint estimation of nitrogen and uncertain parameters brings concrete answers to these challenges and will be a matter of future investigations.
