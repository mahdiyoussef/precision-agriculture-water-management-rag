## A R T I C L E I N F O                                         A B S T R A C T

Handling editor: Lixiao Zhang                                 Marinas are semi-enclosed environments with low hydrodynamic energy, high sedimentation rates, and reduced oxygen levels, making them prone to contaminant accumulation. In the Mediterranean, where tidal amplitude is low, these conditions lead to infrequent dredging and prolonged pollutant buildup. This study assessed the impact of sediment dredging on trace metal elements (TMEs) and organotin compounds in the water column of a Mediterranean marina. A 21-month monitoring campaign was conducted, covering pre-dredging (12 months), dredging (4 months), and post-dredging (5 months) phases. Such long-term, high-frequency monitoring of the water column is rarely performed, particularly in marinas, where environmental assessments typically focus on sediments. The results revealed significant changes during dredging, including increased pH and oxygen levels, and frequent exceedances of turbidity thresholds set by French regulations. TME concentrations in the water column generally decreased during and after dredging, especially for arsenic (As), nickel (Ni), lead (Pb), and lithium (Li), while organotin levels remained stable. Aluminum (Al) concentrations stayed high throughout, and cadmium (Cd) and copper (Cu) showed temporary reductions that were not sustained post-dredging. In sediments, TME levels decreased, but organotin concen­ trations rose, particularly in technical zones. This study highlights the need to revise current dredging regulations by expanding monitoring beyond turbidity to include resuspended contaminants and hydrodynamic conditions. The use of predictive tools, adaptive management strategies, and collaborative approaches among scientists, port authorities, and regulators is essential to improve environmental risk assessments and promote sustainable dredging practices. ### 4. Discussion

Port dredging operations in France often require a prefectural authorization. This authorization is essential to ensure that dredging activities comply with environmental regulations and international conventions, such as the OSPAR and the Barcelona conventions. For the dredging operations in Carnon harbor, the prefectural authorization was delivered to the dredging operator with the obligation that the turbidity did not exceed a threshold value fixed as a function of the natural environmental conditions observed in the harbor. For the Carnon har­ bor, the threshold value was fixed at 20.4 NTU according to the turbidity range measured before the dredging period. The prefectural authoriza­ tion imposes the obligation for the dredging operator to stop the dredging operations if the turbidity reached a maximal value repre­ senting 1.5 times the threshold value, i.e., 30.6 NTU. No other requirement was associated with the prefectural authorization. In addition, the prefectural authorization did not describe the methodol­ ogy that should be used to measure turbidity nor describe the frequency and the localization of the measurement. The only obligation imposed on the dredging operator is that the dredging operations must be stopped if the turbidity is greater than 30.6 NTU. In this context, discrete measurements of turbidity were conducted during the dredging opera­ tions in station C$_1$ near the harbor mouth to the open sea; turbidity was estimated with a portable turbidimeter that determined turbidity by ratios, using a primary signal of nephelometric scattered light compared to the transmitted scattered light signal. Eleven discrete measurements were performed during the 4 months of the dredging operations, and values never exceeded 20 NTU (Fig. S$_1$). Based on these discrete mea­ surements, dredging operations were never stopped during the whole dredging operations in compliance with the prefectural authorization. Nevertheless, to complete the discrete measurements, continuous mea­ surements of turbidity were performed with an FTU probe positioned near the grab and near the dumping zone.
