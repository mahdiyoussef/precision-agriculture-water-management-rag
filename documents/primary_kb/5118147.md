We conducted a study on an organic market-garden farmer’s farm to compare the Biological activity                                     effects of animal traction versus motorized traction operations on soil health over a season of potato cultivation, using various physical (compaction, aggregation), hydrophysical (infiltrability) and biological (biomass and microbial activity) parameters. The results of the three sampling and measurement campaigns carried out on the passage areas (tractor wheel or horse hoof) clearly differentiate the two practices, with the animal traction characterized by a higher microbial biomass, soil aggregation and infiltrability. We have thus observed a negative correlation between the number of passes under motorized traction and soil microbial biomass, while there is no correlation with the passes’ number under animal traction. Moreover, the third campaign results, integrating measurements on passages and ridges, showed effects from the first season of differentiation of the technical itineraries, with a greater difference in penetrometry, bulk density and infiltrability between passages and ridges under motorized traction compared to animal traction operations. Finally, infiltrability was not only faster but more variable on the animal traction passages, whereas it was weaker and more homogeneous along the motorized traction passages. [SOURCE: Page_2]
