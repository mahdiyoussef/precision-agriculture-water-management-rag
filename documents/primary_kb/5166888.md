Evaluation of WAPOR for the Estimation of the Annual Rainfed Crops Biomass, Tunisia [SOURCE: Page_1]

Itidel Alaya, Insaf Mekki, Mariem Boukari, Salah Ben Youssef, Frederic Jacob, and Rim Zitouna-Chebbi [SOURCE: Page_1]

I. Alaya (&) I. Mekki M. Boukari S. B. Youssef R. Zitouna-Chebbi University of Carthage, 1054 Carthage, Tunisia email: [EMAIL_REDACTED] F. Jacob IRD/UMR LISAH, Montpellier, France [SOURCE: Page_1]

Abstract [SOURCE: Page_1]

In the Mediterranean region, considered as the prominent hotspot in future climate change projections, water scarcity becomes the main challenge for the agricultural sector. This is mainly the case of the Tunisian agriculture that is substantially based on rainfed crops to ensure food security. Thus, monitoring and assessing the vegetation conditions may provide accurate information, which allows improving water productivity and helps decision-making including water management. Currently, satellite-derived vegetation data has been widely used to assess the crop production and the effect of water shortage and extreme climatic events. In this context, the FAO portal to monitor water productivity through open-access of remotely sensed derived data (WaPOR) WAPOR database was developed to monitor the water productivity through remotely sensed derived data. The aim of this work is to evaluate WAPOR ability to estimate the biomass of rainfed annual crops in the Cap Bon region in the North-East of Tunisia. The WAPOR products covering the study area are available at the national level (Level 2) with a spatial resolution of 100 m. Measured above-ground of local cereals varieties of wheat, barley and oat and mixed fodder crops (fababean, triticale and vetch) was compared to the estimated actual evapotranspiration and interception (ETa_WAPOR), during four years with different climatic conditions. The area of the considered agricultural fields varies from less than one hectare to around 10 ha. The estimated cumulative actual evapotranspiration (ETa_WAPOR) calculated from the predicted decadal ETa shows an acceptable correlation with the monitored dry biomass (R$_2$ = 0.78) but with high RMSE value of around 2.7 ton/ha. The performance of WAPOR estimation doesn’t seem to vary with seasonal climatic conditions. [SOURCE: Page_1]

1 Introduction [SOURCE: Page_1]

With the water scarcity aggravated by the climate change effects on the Mediterranean region, an accurate monitoring of the biomass production is necessary to improve water productivity and crops’ management (Fu et al., 2014). As conventional measurements are time-consuming and labor intensive, their application for large areas is often infeasible (Boschetti et al., 2007). On the other hand, satellite-derived vegetation data offer an alternative to assess crop production and the effect of water shortage at low costs and on near real time as the case of the water productivity open-access portal “WAPOR”. WAPOR database was developed by the FAO (Food and Agricultural Organization of the United Nations) to monitor the water productivity through remotely sensed derived data. This work aims to evaluate WAPOR ability to estimate the biomass of rainfed annual crops in the Cap Bon region in the North-East of Tunisia. With absence of suf cient data about the seasonal total biomass production, we assessed the possible correlation between WAPOR actual evapotranspiration product and the measured aboveground biomass during the growing season. [SOURCE: Page_1]

2 Materials and Methods [SOURCE: Page_1]

2.1 Study Area and Experimental Fields [SOURCE: Page_1]

The experiment is realized on pluvial fields distributed over a Mediterranean watershed“Lebna” (36° 43ʹ N–36° 53ʹ N; 10° 45ʹ E–10° 58ʹ E) located in the Cap Bon Peninsula in northeast of Tunisia. The study area is divided into three bioclimatic stages from upstream to downstream: Humid temperate, sub-humid mild and semi-arid mild. The mean annual rainfall and the mean annual evapotranspiration (Penman–Monteith reference crop) vary from 450 to 800 mm and from 1000 to 1500 mm, respectively (IAO, 2002; Zitouna-Chebbi et al., 2012). The watershed, characterized by the wide variability of topography, parent material and soil properties, is mainly a rural zone with the dominance of annual crops that cover half of its area (Mekki et al., 2018). [SOURCE: Page_1]

The selected fields distributed over seven locations in the catchment are cultivated with annual crops focusing on cereals, faba-bean and mixed fodder crops. The considered species are wheat, barley and oats for cereals, faba-bean and combined cropping of faba-bean, vetch and triticale. [SOURCE: Page_2]

Despite the high topographic heterogeneity on the watershed, most of the selected plots have moderate slope (5–10%) as 36% of Lebna arable land. Belonging to three different soil classes (Entisols, Al sols and Inceptisols according to the USDA classi cation), the fields represent a variety of parent material, texture, depth and hydraulic characteristics. The plots’ sizes represent also a large range from 0.4 to 9.9 ha, but more than a half of them are smaller than 1 ha (Table 1). [SOURCE: Page_2]

2.2 Data Collection and Analysis [SOURCE: Page_2]

The experimental fields’ aboveground biomass (AGB) was surveyed during four agricultural seasons (2017, 2019, 2020 and 2021) at a decadal time step. Biomass monitoring was carried out using the destructive method with sampling of 1 m^2 of fresh matter weight. The collected data covers in total 17 plots. [SOURCE: Page_2]

During the four considered seasons, the recorded rainfall amounts (from November to May) vary from 335 (season 2021) to 535 mm (season 2020) with divergency of intra-season repartition and the presence of high intensity rainfall events (Fig. 2). During 2017, the region encountered drought from mid-February. While 2019’s season has benefited from a balanced rainfall pattern. The two last seasons (2020 and 2021) are characterized by a dry period at the mid-cycle (January–February). The Food and Agricultural Organization of the United Nations (FAO) portal to monitor water productivity through open-access of remotely sensed derived data (WaPOR) provides actual evapotranspiration and interception (ETa_WAPOR) data at a decadal time step across Africa and the Middle East. ETa_WAPOR is estimated based on the ETLook model (Bastiaanssen et al., 2012) that uses the Penman-Monteith (P-M) equation, adapted to remote sensing input data. Since WAPOR (Version 2) is recently developed, there were very rare works on evaluating its products over arid and semi-arid regions (Blatchford et al., 2020; Geshnigani et al., 2021; Javadian et al., 2019). The WAPOR data covering the study area are available at the level 2 with a resolution of 0.000992° (* 100 m). [SOURCE: Page_2]
