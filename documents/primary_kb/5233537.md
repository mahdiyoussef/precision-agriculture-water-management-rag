Solar stills are used as wastewater treatment and cooling is needed for water vapour recovery : radiative cooling is an option, if extended to daytime conditions. [SOURCE: Page_2]

Radiative cooling is achieved by using materials with selective spectral emissivity : very low in the solar spectrum and very high in the atmospheric infrared window : the cooling potential is estimated using an ideal spectral emissivity profile. Although the cooling potential can be high during daytime, spontaneous dew formation primarily occurs at night, as commonly observed in natural environments. [SOURCE: Page_2]

At night, cooling is easily achieved in cooled boxes : the cooling efficiency depends on design strategies, including varying interior wall emissivities and using multi-stage configurations. Efficiencies varies from 10 up to 75 % according to cooler architectures. During daytime, solar incomes have to be drastically reduced to maintain temperatures inside the boxes below the ambient levels. Insulation and reflector studies are conducted for the design optimisation in the first step. [SOURCE: Page_2]

The increase in temperature and airflow rate behind a solar still leads to a higher cooling demand for water recovery. The design of a solar still cooler is therefore not solely related to the temperature inside the cooling box, but also to the need to overcome latent heat rejection. Hence, dynamic strategies, such as nightime storage, requires further investigation in the second step for properly sizing radiative coolers for solar still applications. HBM model will be used for optimzing the coupling between the evaporative solar still and the condensation units of the 3Dstellar prototype. [SOURCE: Page_2]
