## 1 ABSTRACT

Since 2014, France has legally recognised Territorial Food Projects (TFPs) as a framework to engage food system (FS) stakeholders, foster the development of territorial food chains, and strengthen the establishment of more resilient FS in response to climate change. The growing urgency of challenges such as water scarcity underscores the need for FSs that can adapt to these pressures. This study examines whether TFPs contribute to the agroecological (AE) transition, with a particular focus on water management, using the case study of the Carcassonne agglomeration. Two key documents of the selected TFP - the diagnosis and the action plan - were analysed, and the initiatives and actions were categorised according to the 13 AE principles defined by HLPE (2019). An AE score was subsequently calculated. The results indicate that initiatives and actions addressed all 13 AE principles, achieving medium-to-high AE scores for both documents, thereby demonstrating a clear engagement in AE transition. Additionally, some measures emphasised an attention to preserving water. The diagnosis report primarily concentrated on water quality, particularly through the reduction of phytosanitary products. Meanwhile, the action plan focused on water quantity through the establishment of water reservoirs that can store water for agricultural purposes. Overall, the Carcassonne TFP acknowledges the significance of water resource protection and reveals emerging connections between AE transition and water management at the territorial level. ## 2.1 FOOD SYSTEMS AND MAIN STAKES

Food systems (FSs) encompass the production, processing, distribution, and consumption of food. They are shaped by the presence of different stakeholders, such as producers, retailers, and consumers (Ericksen 2008; FAO 2014; Rastoin 2016; HLPE 2019), and by interactions within the biophysical and human environments (Ericksen, 2008). However, nowadays, FSs face multiple challenges. On one side, monoculture, intensive farming systems and long food chains are negatively contributing to the increase of greenhouse gases and loss of biodiversity (Iglesias et al.2012; Vermeulen et al. 2012; Béné et al.2019). On the other side, the consequences of climate change, such as prolonged droughts, heatwaves and precipitation concentration in a short time, are seriously damaging crop production (Ceglar et al. 2019; Ray et al. 2019). ## 2.2 ALTERNATIVE FOOD SYSTEMS IN FRANCE

Since 1990, France has started discussions on developing local FSs (Lamine et al. 2023). It culminated in 2014 with the creation of the national law “Future for Agriculture and Forestry”, which refers to the Territorial Food Projects (TFPs) (Law n° 1170 2014). TFPs aim to develop FSs that address social, economic and environmental dimensions (González De Molina and Lopez-Garcia 2021; von Braun et al. 2021; Felici and Mazzocchi 2022). Their objectives are to strengthen the interaction among the different stakeholders of the short local food chain, through a territorial food production and consumption (Figure 1) (OECD/FAO/UNCDF 2016; Banzo et al. 2024; Liu et al.,2024). Therefore, TFPs represent interesting case study references to study alternatives which could face the challenges mentioned above. [SOURCE: Page_7]

## 2.3 AGROECOLOGY IN FOOD SYSTEMS AND WATER MANAGEMENT

As water resources become scarcer (Mekonnen and Hoekstra 2016) and as FS need to become more resilient in the face of climate change, it is interesting to study relationships between water and its use in AE FS (Altieri et al. 2015; Aguilera et al. 2020; Altieri and Nicholls 2020). Although there are different definitions concerning agroecology, there is an agreed objective regarding diversifying FS while using few external inputs (Migliorini et al. 2018; Wezel et al. 2020). Regarding water management in alternative FS, there are a few studies. It is often associated with intensive agricultural FS rather than alternative ones (Pérez Neira 2016; Gross and Jaubert 2019; Leauthaud et al. 2025). For this reason, it is interesting to study how the TFPs help the FS to be more AE and the relationship between water management and agroecology in the TFPs, with a specific attention to the TFP of the Carcassonne agglomeration. ## 3.1 CASE STUDY SITE DESCRIPTION

Carcassonne agglomeration, with a population of 113.827 inhabitants (INSEE 2025), is located in the Occitanie French administrative region. This territory is part of the Mediterranean area where main FS stakes are particularly enhanced by negative effects of climate change (Giorgi and Lionello 2008; Tuel and Eltahir 2020; Guion de Meritens et al. 2019; Raymond et al. 2018) such as severe water constraints, droughts and heatwaves (Fabre 2015; Mekonnen and Hoekstra 2016; Meritens et al. 2019; Soubeyroux et al. 2020; DREAL - Occitanie and Cerema 2021). The TFP of the Carcassonne agglomeration was legally recognised in 2021. In 2022, it produced a diagnosis of the already existing FS, which enhanced the strong partnership with the FS stakeholders of the area. Then, an action plan for the desired FS was published in 2024. Therefore, it has been chosen as an appropriate case study to explore and discuss how AE transition and water management are considered in an alternative FS case study. [SOURCE: Page_9]

## 3.4 CATEGORISATION AND SUMS OF INITIATIVES AND ACTIONS IN EACH AE PRINCIPLE

For this second analysis, the goal was to classify initiatives and actions in each AE principle as defined by Wezel et al. (2020). A list of initiatives and actions considered as AE was compiled based on the diagnosis and action plan. These initiatives and actions were pre- listed as AE because they referred to the 13 AE principles and were relevant to their definitions (Wezel et al. 2020). During this phase, initiatives and actions referring to water-related AE practices were also checked and classified under the 13 AE principles. Then, interview data were used to supplement and confirm available information in order to help in the classification of initiatives and actions. ## 5.1 CARCASSONNE AGGLOMERATION FOOD SYSTEM

Although much of the quantitative data on the FS scheme is missing, it is evident that vineyard monocultures limit territorial food production, leading to importing some food, such as vegetables, from areas outside the agglomeration. Nevertheless, certain actions, such as the rehabilitation of the Domaine des Rougeats, demonstrate the territory's commitment to producing some local food and restoring connections between animals and agricultural diversification. A key asset of this TFP is also the collaboration that has been developed among farmers who engage in developing a food processing space in the agglomeration, supporting the establishment of short food supply chains. These efforts contributed to economic diversification but also to fostering the relationship with consumers, who are informed about where the food they consume is produced. However, the absence of comprehensive data on food transformation, consumption patterns, and food waste management hinders the evaluation of the FS as a whole. For this reason, stronger collaboration with local food waste authorities could be established in the future to find out more about food waste and the amount of food that is recycled. This could contribute to enriching TFP knowledge and identifying potential improvements to the recycling process. [SOURCE: Page_39]

## 5.2 AE TRANSITION OF THE TFP OF THE CARCASSONNE AGGLOMERATION

5.2.1 AE initiatives and actions and AE score [SOURCE: Page_39]

When comparing the number of initiatives and actions linked to the 13 AE principles and their corresponding AE score obtained for each, it becomes evident that a greater number of initiatives does not necessarily lead to higher AE scores. For instance, a few initiatives have been reported for recycling, reducing inputs, improving soil and animal health, co-creating knowledge and fairness. However, they received a score close to 6 or higher, indicating that they have both a medium-to-high (equivalent to 3 or 4) AL and the AEL. Meanwhile, the biodiversity principle reported many initiatives (5 initiatives) and actions (4 actions), its AL or AEL has sometimes got a low score (equivalent to 2). For instance, in some cases, stakeholders' participation was limited, or from an AE perspective, only a plan had been developed. [SOURCE: Page_39]

34 [SOURCE: Page_39]

Similarly, several actions were reported for social values and diets and land and natural resource governance, but sometimes their AL or AEL scores were quite low (equal to 2). This is because either more stakeholders need to be involved, or because, from an AE perspective, the actions have the potential to support an AE transition, but only a plan has been established. [SOURCE: Page_40]

5.2.2 AE transition [SOURCE: Page_40]

The TFP of the Carcassonne agglomeration does not explicitly frame its initiatives and actions within the concept of agroecology; however, analysing them, many align with the 13 AE principles defined by Wezel et al. (2020). This suggests that the TFP of the Carcassonne agglomeration is a meaningful example of a FS engaged in the AE transition (Lagacherie et al. 2018; Aguilera et al. 2020). The TFP appears to be highly active in developing a territorial FS. Specifically, since its early stage, the TFP has fostered strong participation with citizens, farmers, and local agricultural institutions. However, both the documents and the interviews emphasised some barriers, which slow down the TFP's efforts to develop a more AE FS. Specifically, difficulties occurred in convincing vineyard farmers to develop a more diversified agroecosystem. These barriers are due to vineyards’ monocultures and farmers’ reluctance to diversify their production without a positive economic incentive. Nevertheless, the territory has already started to develop initiatives for establishing a partnership with farmers on agro- environmental measures that reduce the use of phytosanitary products in order to improve water quality. Additionally, to support a more diversified FS that relies less on external resources, the TFP's action plan has built upon the initiative identified in the diagnosis phase: a partnership with various stakeholders to support agricultural diversification, including agroforestry plantations, which may help reduce water consumption (Lin et al. 2018; Région Occitanie 2023). [SOURCE: Page_40]

5.2.3 Water and AE transition [SOURCE: Page_40]

The diagnosis report has initiatives that refer to water quality, while the action plan developed actions to preserve water from a quantitative point of view (Carcassonne Agglo 2022; 2024). This leads to observing the complementarity of the diagnosis and action plan in developing initiatives and actions that refer to water, both from a qualitative and quantitative point of view. Even though a few initiatives and actions concern water, they received a [SOURCE: Page_40]

35 [SOURCE: Page_40]

medium-to-high AE score. These include different stakeholders of the FS, such as local agricultural organisations and farmers, to develop a water scheme in the area. For instance, the water scheme action aims to introduce water reservoirs in different areas of the agglomeration, allow for water storage and use it for farming activities, as well as for wildfire episodes. However, as shown in the article of Aguilera et al. (2020), some other water conservation techniques exist in the Mediterranean basin, such as terracing and pozas, a water technique that allows conserving water on the slope (Ballais et al. 2013). Furthermore, agroforestry plantations are another example of AE practice, which has been shown to reduce the amount of water consumed by plants (Lin et al. 2018). In territories such as the Carcassonne agglomeration, where a decrease in water availability is expected, these practices represent a key asset. Additionally, the above-mentioned techniques and practices allow for the preservation and better water management of natural resources at limited financial costs (Aguilera et al. 2020). The examples could be introduced through a partnership of the TFP, farmers and local agricultural institutions while also supporting the AE transition. The stakeholders involved in the TFP are aware of the importance of developing actions to preserve water resources, both from quantitative and qualitative perspectives. They acknowledge the relationship between the necessity to preserve this resource and the introduction of crops that could require less water. However, they are also aware that economic constraints prevent farmers from changing their agricultural practices, for example, to introduce agroforestry or adopt drought-resistant crops. These new practices could demand less water, but farmers sometimes do not perceive them as economically profitable if markets for selling these products are not established. Finally, the TFP of Carcassonne agglomeration, together with those of the Tarn-Agout, Sicoval and Vidourle Camargue (all of which are located in the Occitanie region), is one of the few to have established activities with farmers to support agricultural practices, such as introducing drought-resistant crops that could require less water^17. This leads to think that this TFP has some attention to water and AE practices and transition. [SOURCE: Page_41]

## 5.3 ABOUT THE APPROACH

The data were collected from the diagnosis report and action plan of the TFP of the Carcassonne agglomeration. For the diagnosis report, we selected the initiatives that referred to the 13 AE principles, as the TFP was presented in a descriptive style, which could lead to interpretation bias. In contrast, the action plan included an action sheet for each action, reducing the interpretation bias and facilitating the actions’ classification in the 13 AE principles. [SOURCE: Page_42]

The interviews enabled the enrichment of the case study and the collection of further information on the initiatives and actions reported under the 13 AE principles. Concerning the interviews’ data collection, the selection of the interviewees was based on purposive sampling. This method enabled to meet the selected interviewees who have provided comprehensive answers, as they are the referents on the initiatives/actions. A limitation of this selection method is that interviewees may be more inclined to highlight positive aspects while downplaying potential constraints that initiatives/actions may have encountered. However, during the interviews, all stakeholders provided complete answers explaining the problems or barriers of the specific initiative/action, so it appears that this constraint did not occur. [SOURCE: Page_42]

To categorise the initiatives/actions within the 13 AE principles, we used the definition of the 13 AE principles provided by Wezel et al. (2020). Despite the efforts to obtain detailed information on the initiatives/actions categorised under the 13 AE principles, it is possible that some of them could have been linked to more AE principles compared to what we identified, due either to limited data or to potential interpretation bias. [SOURCE: Page_42]

Additionally, we reported initiatives/actions in all 13 AE principles to which they referred. This results in some of them being included in multiple AE principles, rather than only being reported to the closest AE principle. We made this decision because we believed it was essential to clarify the relationship between all the initiatives/ actions within the 13 AE principles. For example, we categorised the action supporting the catering industry in sourcing quality, organic and local production (action 7) in multiple AE principles, as this action [SOURCE: Page_42]

37 [SOURCE: Page_42]

concerns diets, but also the establishment of a relationship between farmers and food catering services. [SOURCE: Page_43]

The developed AE score allowed the evaluation of the elements (initiatives/actions) in terms of their participation and of the AE principle to which they referred. This helped to recognise that a high presence of different stakeholders who are motivated by the element can positively influence its advancement to have an impact on the agroecosystem and the FS dimension of the AE principle. We decided to assign a weight to each element to avoid over- or underestimation. However, we are aware that this type of evaluation cannot fully capture the complexity and interactions among all elements. In addition, we are aware that the AE score that we developed should be improved. This study did not develop subcategories or very detailed criteria within each AE principle. The AEL assigned for each AE principle is not as detailed as other AE evaluation methods (FAO 2019; Peeters et al. 2021; Darmaun et al. 2023). For instance, other methodologies assessing the farms' AE transition have used more comprehensive frameworks, including 70 to 88 indicators and have collaborated with local FS stakeholders in defining subcategories under each AE principle (Peeters et al. 2021; Darmaun et al. 2023). A future version of the evaluation grid could include subcategories within each AE principle. This would allow for more accurate categorisation of the initiatives and actions within the 13 AE principles. Also, the AL can be improved by adding a benchmark of participants who are involved in the initiative or action, to provide a clearer idea of the number of stakeholders involved. Finally, the scoring system was developed internally within the institute, and the results were only presented and discussed with the coordinator of the TFP. While it would have been useful to co-construct it with local stakeholders, as other AE assessments use this approach. This would have allowed feedback to be incorporated and improved the AE score created. However, in this case study, it was not possible due to time constraints. [SOURCE: Page_43]

## 6 CONCLUSION

This study showed that the TFP of the Carcassonne agglomeration has developed various initiatives/actions addressing all 13 AE principles (HLPE 2019; Wezel et al. 2020), reaching the 4th level of Gliessman’s classification. The AE score indicates that the TFP has developed initiatives/actions relating to agroecology and AE transition, achieving a medium- to-high AE score. However, the terms 'agroecology' and 'AE transition' were not often mentioned in the documents. [SOURCE: Page_44]

Despite the agglomeration facing water scarcity issues, few initiatives/actions have been pursued to preserve water resources. However, the few initiatives/actions developed received a medium-to-high AE score. This suggests an attempt to integrate water into the AE transition, even though it is not directly mentioned in the diagnosis and action plan of the TFP. [SOURCE: Page_44]

The TFP has demonstrated strong collaboration with the governance of the Carcassonne agglomeration and all the stakeholders involved in the TFP. For instance, this has allowed to integrate actions developed by the agglomeration Green Plan into the TFP action plan, strengthening the integration of environmental, land and natural resource dimensions. This strong participation could allow to support the establishment of a robust water governance structure that includes local governance authority, farmers and citizens to build together strategies that can save water and use it for agricultural purposes. In particular, the present study could be used by the Carcassonne agglomeration TFP as a tool to enhance or continue the discussion with stakeholders about the margins of improvement in supporting a higher agricultural diversification or about actions to be continued to preserve water quality and quantity, while also supporting AE systems. Finally, this study provides a useful AE score that shows the margin of improvements to reinforce the relationship between water and agroecology, but also in keeping a strong stakeholders' participation in a TFP that can be involved in AE transition and to be more resilient in the face of the negative consequences of climate change. [SOURCE: Page_44]

## APPENDIX 3 FIGURES OF THE THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK

The FS scheme illustrates the elements that comprise it, including food production, processing, consumption, and waste. It also represents the biophysical and human environments, as well as its inputs and outputs that can influence it. The FS scheme shows four main phases of the FS. Starting from the left, there is the food production space, composed of the agricultural production, food production, livestock feeding, and non-food production. The food production specifically refers to food intended for human consumption. The non-food production concerns crops such as ornamental and medicinal plants that are not consumed as food, but can be used for other purposes, such as the production of medicinal products. The livestock feeding space refers to the food nutrients used by animals. Then, there is the food transformation and distribution phase, during which food is processed either locally or within a long food chain involving various stakeholders in the FS, such as wholesalers and retailers. During this phase, some food arrives at the restaurant and food catering facilities, where it is transformed and cooked, or it goes directly to the consumption phase. This phase concerns spaces where people can eat, both in private and public areas. Finally, there is the food waste management, during which the waste is managed through recycling, discarding, and reusing. Specifically, recycling activities refer to the food waste that, after being treated, can return to agricultural production as compost. Discarding occurs when it is not possible to treat resources differently, while reusing refers to food that arrives at the consumption level, which is not consumed but is clean and safe to redistribute through organisations such as food banks. Furthermore, each FS has its own biophysical and sociocultural environment, which shapes the FS. In all FSs, inputs such as chemical compounds used in agricultural as well as natural resources are often added in food production. Additionally, some outputs are produced, which refer to the food production as well as the greenhouse gas emissions that the FS is producing in its different phases. [SOURCE: Page_58]

## APPENDIX 5 JUSTIFICATION OF ACTION CLASSIFICATION IN EACH AGROECOLOGICAL PRINCIPLE

To conduct interviews with the various stakeholders who participated in structuring the TFP's action plan, the information reported in the document was analysed. Based on this analysis, we developed questions for actions that, in our opinion, lacked quantitative information and for which further insight was required to understand their connection with the agroecological transition. The questions were formulated for the following actions: 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 12, 14, 15, and 16. No questions were formulated concerning actions 3, 9, 10, 11, and 13. While these actions are relevant to the Carcassonne case study area, they were not extremely helpful in understanding the AE transition in terms of both the agroecosystem and the FS dimension. Action 3 relates to the development of an application to inform farmers about available land and options to purchase it. While this action can support the agricultural activities, it is not particularly relevant in understanding the agroecological areas. Action 9 refers to food provision for young people, to address food insecurity among this part of society. However, this was not taken into account, as Action 8 already addresses food aid for the community. Furthermore, Action 9 clearly stipulated the planned activities and referred to the number of young people who have and will continue to benefit from the food aid. Action 10 involves the local community in activities related to FSs, such as screenings and debates on the FS topic. Additionally, this action refers to the organisation of cooking activities and workshops. The activities developed for this action are very clear and already quantified. For this reason, it was decided not to further investigate it during the interviews. Action 11 focused specifically on children and their families, offering cooking workshops. As for Action 10, the planned actions and the activities developed are clear and have already been quantified. For this reason, no questions were prepared for this action. Action 13 pertains to supporting local food fairs. It was observed that the actions developed focus specifically on enhancing the communication of food fairs, which is not extremely pertinent to the research objectives. For this reason, it was decided not to investigate this action. Finally, we are aware that personal expectations could also have unintentionally influenced how the data was collected, interpreted, and presented. For instance, the student has a more profound understanding of FS compared to the agroecosystem dimension. This disparity in expertise has the potential to result in the inadvertent exclusion of agroecosystem-related initiatives and actions. Finally, interpretation bias could have occurred during the utilisation of the 13 AE principles as defined by Wezel et al. (2020). This could have resulted in an underrepresentation of the initiatives and actions within some of the aforementioned principles. For example, initiatives or actions were categorised based on the AE principles, as determined by the information obtained from the two documents and the data collected during the interviews. However, the information was not always very detailed, so it is possible that some initiatives and actions could have fit under multiple AE principles and that these were not recognised during the data collection and analysis.
