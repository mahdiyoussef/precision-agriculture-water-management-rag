Summary [SOURCE: Page_3]

In drylands groundwater is often the only perennial source of freshwater to sustain domestic water [SOURCE: Page_3]

supplies and irrigation. Knowledge of the pathways and dynamics of groundwater discharge and [SOURCE: Page_3]

recharge is essential to inform sustainable and rational management of limited water resources. The [SOURCE: Page_3]

lower valley of the Dallol Maouri in Niger represents a large fossil tributary (i.e. paleochannel) of the [SOURCE: Page_3]

River Niger and drains groundwater regionally from the Iullemmeden Basin through coarse-grained [SOURCE: Page_3]

Quaternary sediments. The objective of this paper is to quantify groundwater discharge within this [SOURCE: Page_3]

paleochannel using piezometry and surface geophysics (TDEM : Time Domain Electromagnetic, MRS : 1 [SOURCE: Page_3]

Magnetic Resonance Sounding). TDEM and MRS experiments were conducted at 21 sites along 3 [SOURCE: Page_4]

transects show respectively the mean thickness of Quaternary alluvium varies from 11 m to 18 m with [SOURCE: Page_4]

the effective porosities ranging from 20% to 33% and hydraulic conductivity of 2 × 10 -3 m/s. Dense [SOURCE: Page_4]

piezometric surveys along drainage channel reveal hydraulic gradients of 0.2 ‰ to 0.3 ‰ that indicate [SOURCE: Page_4]

Darcy fluxes of 1,000 to 2,000 m^3/day (dry season, i.e. minimum value). Paleochannel discharge, which [SOURCE: Page_4]

currently provides baseflow to the River Niger is the focus local demand to increase access to water for [SOURCE: Page_4]

drinking, livestock watering, and irrigation. [SOURCE: Page_4]

1    Introduction [SOURCE: Page_4]

Drylands are characterised by ephemeral rivers (D’Odorico and Bhattachan 2015 ; Tarnavsky et al. [SOURCE: Page_4]

2013 ; Koch and Missimer 2016 ; Davies et al. 2016). The Sahel of West Africa is characterized by [SOURCE: Page_4]

strong seasonal variations in rainfall, causing intense runoff to topographic depressions that facilitate [SOURCE: Page_4]

focus recharge (Favreau et al. 2009). An intensification of precipitation, resulting in fewer but heavier [SOURCE: Page_4]

rainfalls, has been observed in the Sahel (Taylor et al. 2017) in response to global warming and is [SOURCE: Page_4]

expected to amplify focused groundwater recharge (Cuthbert et al. 2016 ; 2019 ; Taylor et al. 2013a). [SOURCE: Page_4]

Recent high-resolution modelling of Sahelian climate suggests further that the projected intensification [SOURCE: Page_4]

of rainfall by climate change is even greater than that estimated previously by General Circulation [SOURCE: Page_4]

Models (Berthou et al. 2019). Notwithstanding potential increases in groundwater recharge from the [SOURCE: Page_4]

amplification of heavy rainfalls, growing demand for freshwater is placing increasing pressure on [SOURCE: Page_4]

perennial groundwater resources (Taylor et al. 2013b; DeGraaf et al. 2019) whose discharges sustain [SOURCE: Page_4]

dryland ecosystems. [SOURCE: Page_4]

In the drylands of Niger, groundwater is a vital source for supplying drinking water to most large cities [SOURCE: Page_4]

and rural areas as well as for supplying livestock and irrigation (Nazoumou et al. 2016). Recent [SOURCE: Page_4]

hydrogeological studies of the unconfined aquifer of Continental Terminal in the southwestern [SOURCE: Page_4]

Iullemmeden Basin have shown a rise in the water table since the 1960s (Favreau 2000; Leduc et al. [SOURCE: Page_4]

2 [SOURCE: Page_4]

2001; Favreau and al. 2002; Leblanc et al. 2008, Favreau et al. 2009). In this dryland region, knowledge [SOURCE: Page_5]

of the pathways and dynamics of groundwater discharge contained in Quaternary alluviums from valleys [SOURCE: Page_5]

of fossil tributaries is essential for the sustainable and rational management of these vital resources. [SOURCE: Page_5]

Several studies have demonstrated the efficacy of geophysical surveys for characterizing the geometry [SOURCE: Page_5]

and lithology of paleochannels in drylands in order to assess the availability of groundwater. Oladunjoye [SOURCE: Page_5]

et al. (2020) characterized the geometry of the Ilora Valley in southwestern Nigeria using vertical [SOURCE: Page_5]

electric soundings. Similarly, Owen and Dahlin (2005) delineated the extent and thickness of the [SOURCE: Page_5]

Quaternary infill of the Umzingwane Valley in Zimbabwe using Vertical Electric Soundings (VES). [SOURCE: Page_5]

Othman et al. (2018) characterized the geometry of Pliocene aquifer using VES and TEM sounding at [SOURCE: Page_5]

the west Nile Delta in Egypt. In drylands of Australia, Airbome Electromagnetic (AEM) geophysical [SOURCE: Page_5]

surveys and piezometry have been used to define the dimensions of Gallingarra Paleochannel, and [SOURCE: Page_5]

evaluate the groundwater discharge to the Moore River (Speed and Killen 2020). In the arid Heihe Basin [SOURCE: Page_5]

of China, paleochannel groundwater discharges to the rivers are considered critical to regulating the [SOURCE: Page_5]

hydrological cycle and ecosystem health (Yao et al. 2016; 2018; Zhao et al. 2016). [SOURCE: Page_5]

Magnetic Resonance Sounding (MRS) is a non-invasive geophysical method (Legchenko and Valla [SOURCE: Page_5]

2002) which makes it possible to obtain hydrogeological parameters of an aquifer by directly measuring [SOURCE: Page_5]

a signal emitted by the hydrogen protons of the water molecule (Vouillamoz et al. 2008). TDEM (Time [SOURCE: Page_5]

Domain Electromagnetism) is an electromagnetic geophysical sounding method that allows determining [SOURCE: Page_5]

the resistivity as a function of depth from the diffusion of a transient electromagnetic field (Fitterman [SOURCE: Page_5]

and Stewart 1986; Descloitres 1998). The combination of these two methods has already been successful [SOURCE: Page_5]

applied in Niger in the study of the superficial aquifer of the Continental Terminal (Vouillamoz et al. [SOURCE: Page_5]

2008; Boucher et al. 2009a, Boucher et al. 2009b, Boucher et al. 2012) and on the unconfined Quaternary [SOURCE: Page_5]

aquifer in the Komadugu Yobé valley of the Lake Chad basin (Descloitres et al. 2013). [SOURCE: Page_5]

The lower valley of Dallol Maouri is one of the fossils tributaries of the River Niger (paleochannel) [SOURCE: Page_5]

which drains regional groundwater through Quaternary alluvium. This area is one of the most suitable [SOURCE: Page_5]

regions of Niger for irrigation (Cochand 2007; Dambo 2007) and has been the subject of several [SOURCE: Page_5]

3 [SOURCE: Page_5]

hydrogeological studies since the 1970s. Early studies carried out by FAO 1970 exploited deep [SOURCE: Page_6]

boreholes and constructed several piezometers to characterize the hydrodynamic parameters of aquifers [SOURCE: Page_6]

in the area through pumping tests ; the overall direction of groundwater flow and recharge areas were [SOURCE: Page_6]

also mapped (FAO 1970). Gaoh (1993) characterized the aquifers of the Continental Terminal through [SOURCE: Page_6]

Recently, Abdou Ali         (2018) characterized aquifers through electric soundings, increasing [SOURCE: Page_6]

Applying MRS and TDEM, the objective of this paper is to quantify the discharge of groundwater in [SOURCE: Page_6]

3.1    Surface geophysics : combined use of Time Domain Electromagnetism and Magnetic [SOURCE: Page_11]

Resonance Sounding [SOURCE: Page_11]

TDEM (Time Domain Electromagnetism) makes it possible to determine the vertical distribution of soil [SOURCE: Page_11]

resistivity with depth (Descloitres 1998). Detailed descriptions of the method can be found in Fitterman [SOURCE: Page_11]

and Stewart (1984), Descloitres (1998), and Reynolds (2011). Magnetic Resonance Sounding (MRS) is [SOURCE: Page_11]

a non-invasive geophysical method (Legchenko and Valla 2002). This method makes it possible to [SOURCE: Page_11]

obtain hydrogeological parameters of an aquifer by directly measuring a signal emitted by the hydrogen [SOURCE: Page_11]

protons of the water molecule (Vouillamoz et al. 2008). Details of the method can be found in [SOURCE: Page_11]

9 [SOURCE: Page_11]

(Legchencko and Valla 2002). This method has the advantage of having information that is directly [SOURCE: Page_12]

related to the mobile water content in the subsoil and a relaxation time related to the size of the pores [SOURCE: Page_12]

(Schirov et al. 1991). [SOURCE: Page_12]

The hydrogeophysical parameters from MRS are the water content, which represents the part of the [SOURCE: Page_12]

investigated volume occupied by mobile water (Legchenko et al. 2004; Lubczynski and Roy 2005), and [SOURCE: Page_12]

the proton relaxation time constants T$_1$ and T$_2$, which provide information on the average pore size [SOURCE: Page_12]

(Legchenko et al. 2002). [SOURCE: Page_12]

The water content (𝜃𝑀𝑅𝑆 ), given by MRS, is comparable to the effective porosity (Lubczynski and Roy [SOURCE: Page_12]

2005). [SOURCE: Page_12]

the following equations 1 and 2 (Kenyon 1997): [SOURCE: Page_12]

located on the plateau 1.5 km from the sounding site. This formation has also been identified by VES [SOURCE: Page_21]

with resistivity values of 5 Ω·m, (CGG 1968) and a range of values from 2 to 60 Ω·m (Abdou Ali 2018). [SOURCE: Page_21]
