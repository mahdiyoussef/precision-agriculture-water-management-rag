ABSTRACT For full list of author afﬁliations and declarations see end of paper Context. Rapid agrarian changes in north-west Cambodia have led to extensive deforestation for upland crop cultivation. Aims. This study assessed the impact of four land-use types on soil chemical *Correspondence to: Pao Srean and biological properties and indicators of soil health. Methods. The four land uses were fallow Faculty of Agriculture and Food Processing,     (16 years since forest conversion), maize–cassava (14 years), mango (16 years), and mango–cassava National University of Battambang,              intercropping (15 years). Soil organic carbon (C) and nitrogen (N) stocks, chemical properties, and Battambang, Cambodia macro-/meso-fauna were measured at four depths. Soil health indicators were assessed at 0–10 cm: Email: [EMAIL_REDACTED] permanganate oxidisable carbon (POXC), aggregation, water inﬁltration, and visual evaluation of soil Handling Editor:                                structure. Key results. Agricultural lands showed lower topsoil C than fallow, with mango–cassava Brian Wilson                                    intercropping exhibiting a C depletion rate of −1.42 Mg C ha−1 y−1. Signiﬁcant N depletion occurred under maize–cassava and mango–cassava (−250 and −260 kg N ha−1 y−1, respectively). Maize–cassava had a higher C/N ratio due to lower N concentrations. Water aggregate stability and POXC were signiﬁcantly lower under mango–cassava and maize–cassava. Soil fauna abundance and richness were reduced in agricultural lands. Conclusions. The forest conversion to agriculture negatively impacted topsoil C (38% loss), labile C (35–70% loss), soil aggregation, and soil fauna. Implications. These ﬁndings highlight the need to evaluate the costs of soil fertility depletion and explore and promote alternative management practices such as conservation agriculture and agroforestry, alongside ﬁnancial mechanisms for soil restoration.
