Abstract:                                 A Decision Support System has been set up as the result of a fruitful cooperation between several public and research institutions in the framework of a large cooperation program. The DSS aims to compare spatially and temporally sectorial water demands of the Haouz-Mejjate plain (Morocco) in regard to available surface and groundwater resources. It is composed of a tool for satellite estimation of Agricultural Water Demand (SAMIR), a tool for integrated water resources planning (WEAP) and a groundwater model (MODFLOW) each of them relying on a common Geographical Information System not described here. The DSS is operating on a monthly time scale. Agricultural water demand accounts for about 80% of the total demand. In areas where groundwater abstraction is difficult to quantify by direct methods, multitemporal remote sensing associated to the FAO methodology is a simple and efficient alternative to [SOURCE: Page_1]

An important part of the paper deals with the validation of these estimates with eddy covariance flux measurements installed on different irrigated crops of the region. Introduction 6 7 8     Arid and semi-arid areas are characterized by their high sensitivity to climatic hazards. Their water resources 9     can hardly meet an increasing demand driven by irrigated agriculture, population growth, improved living 10 11     conditions and economic development. Global water indicators are inherently bold (Rijsberman 2006). 12     However, projections of the widely accepted Falkenmark indicator show that by 2025, Morocco will reach 13 14     other water scarce countries (Margat & Vallée 1997, FAO 2003). The agricultural sector represents between 15     12 and 20% of GDP and employs about 47% of the workforce. Irrigated areas represent less than 20% of 16 17     cultivated areas but contribute to nearly half of agricultural value added, three quarters of agricultural exports 18     and provide more than a third of employment in rural areas (Belghiti 2008). As in many countries, the second 19 20     part of the XXth century witnessed a great increase in dam construction to control the water offer. Alas, 21     severe droughts weakened the newly created water security while, simultaneously, the access to drilling and 22 23     pumping was facilitated, finally driving to a hitherto unknown pressure on groundwater. The Haouz-Mejjat 24     belongs to the Moroccan overexploited aquifers like Souss-Massa or Oum Er Bia (Bzioui 2004). 25 26     The main characteristics of the modeled area are: (1) A flat and arid plain (2) Agricultural water demand is 27 difficult to assess; (3) Several water rights and management are superposed; (4) A well-developed hydraulic 28 29     network. The setting of the model should be accessible to non-specialists, and ease the simulation of planning 30 scenarios (McKinney 2004). By contract, several operational systems aiming to help the management of 31 32     regional water resources have been developed in recent years (Feflow, Modflow, Mike-Basin), but most 33 remain unsatisfactory due to a lack of flexibility or not dealing with the whole of the water systems including 34 35     surface and groundwater abstractions. 36 37     A conceptual allocation model was preferred to hydrologic models inadequate for this flat arid and highly 38     anthropized area. Earlier implementations of complete systems with graphical modeling tools like VENSIM 39 40     or STELLA (Johannes 2004) have existed for decades, but several tools are now fully dedicated to basin 41     modeling. A detailed comparison between MIKE BASIN, MODSIM, RIBASIM, WBaLo and WEAP may be 42 43     found in (Assaf 2008), although a system like REALM (Perera 2005) could also do the work. 44     This previous analysis added to the existence of an already calibrated and validated yearly groundwater 45 46     model for a transient state, lead to the coupled WEAP-MODFLOW package (Water Evaluation and Planning 47     System ", www.weap21.org for details). WEAP calculates the water balance at watershed or demand sites, 48 49     allocating available water resources based on the expression of demands and allocation priorities through a 50     linear optimization procedure and has been used in several recent papers (Mutiga et al. 2010; Höllermann et 51 52     al. 2010; Droubi 2008; Al-Omari et al. 2009), but without a coupled approach. The establishment of a 53     conceptual model is facilitated by a graphical interface allowing the user to define and connect model 54 55     elements in a simple and intuitive way. A major part of the study area land use is not monitored. Year to year variation of annual crops is significant, 1     and a large extension of irrigated areas in the last decade is induced by intense development of pumping. 2 3     Those facts limit the use of the classical estimation of water demand relating FAO standard Crop Coefficient 4     trapezoids to crop surfaces (Allen et al 1998). Conclusion 6 7     A groundwater model has been set up on a 6000 km2 plain for a period of 8 years on a monthly time step. It 8 9     heavily relies on freely available remote sensing time series for the determination of evapotranspiration. ET 10     is put into a conceptual model which is used to allocate superficial water and determine net groundwater 11 12     demand. The conceptual model is coupled to a groundwater model at each monthly time step. The final 13     results are tangible both on superficial and subterranean references, although no calibration was made for 14 15     surface flows due to the complexity of the model and lack of gauge stations. The model thus highlights (1) 16 the need to intensify measurements particularly on river outlets, and (2) the need for further studies, 17 18     particularly on recharge coefficients, substratum knowledge and mountainous underflows. 19 20 The solution is cost effective and the workflow is relatively simple making it reproducible for other semi-arid 21 22     areas. Nevertheless, it should be accompanied with a good network of agroclimatologic ground stations and 23 24     ground truth surveys for validation of land use. 25 26     Also the simplicity of the approach leaves certain flaws. The method for evapotranspiration estimate is quite 27 28     bold and does not account for eventual water stress. A fully automatic hydric budget at the pixel level can be 29     applied if a set of irrigation rules is spatialized. In the conceptual superficial model, physical processes are 30 31     extremely simplified (eg infiltration coefficient) and should be deepened. Further improvement would also be 32     to link water balance models of the Atlasic watersheds at the entrance to the plain, this way resulting in a 33 34     fully-integrated model of the basin. 35 36     As for today, the whole constitutes a system for operational decision support, enabling the study of 37 38     alternative strategies for planning, and facilitating communication with water users toward an Aquifer 39     Contract. 40 41 42 43 Acknowledgment 44 45 This study was realized within the framework of the Joint International Laboratory TREMA, AGIRE project 46 47     and ACSAD/BGR cooperation.
