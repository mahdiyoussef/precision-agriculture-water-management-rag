## 4. KEY ISSUES & STATUS

4.1 Implementation Status and Key Decisions The project's performance in achieving its development objectives and implementation is rated moderately satisfactory. Significant progress have been made in expanding coverage and utilization of the minimum Reproductive, Maternal, Neonatal, Child, Adolescent Health and Nutrition (RMNCAH+N) package. The procurement and delivery of nutritional inputs (Pumply Sup and Lipid-Based Nutritional Supplements) and anthropometric equipment have been completed. Supplying these resources to new regions and community sites has strengthened service provision, particularly for growth monitoring and care of children aged 0 to 59 months, surpassing the 2025 target by reaching 127%. At the Basic Health Centers (CSB), indicators for antenatal care and assisted deliveries have exceeded the 2025 targets in some regions, achieving 86% of the annual target, reflecting improved access to prenatal and neonatal care. Capacity building for community health workers (CHW) is well advanced: over 5,800 CHW have been trained in Integrated Management of Childhood Illnesses at the Community Level (IMCI-CL), and nearly 900 have received training in Infant and Young Child Feeding (IYCF), Women's Nutrition and Management of Moderate Acute Malnutrition (MMAM). The CommCare platform has been further developed, with new modules for stock management and job aids. Multisectoral interventions have been launched in Vakinankaratra and Itasy regions, with encouraging results. The establishment of 178 vegetable gardens, organization of 163 women's groups, and the validation of 725 business plans for income-generating activities by CHWs and community relays demonstrate positive momentum. Synergies have been established with the World Bank funded Food Systems Resilience Programme (FSRP) for the provision of seeds and agricultural equipment, as well as support from agricultural technicians of the Ministry of Agriculture and Livestock (MINAE).
