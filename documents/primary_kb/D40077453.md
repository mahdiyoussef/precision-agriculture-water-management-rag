## I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING

A. Introduction 1. This restructuring paper seeks the approval of the Division Director on a proposed Level II Restructuring of the FCPF Carbon Fund: Lao PDR Northern Laos Emission Reductions Payments Project (P165751). The proposed restructuring includes: i) an extension of the closing date by 18 months to June 30, 2027 (from the original December 31, 2025), and ii) change of institutional arrangement which is reflected in point 2 of the legal document. This will be the first restructuring of the project, and it is based on the official request from the Ministry of Finance (MoF) and the Ministry of Agriculture dated September 30, 2025. [SOURCE: Page_6]

B. Program Background 2. The Lao People’s Democratic Republic (Lao PDR) (“Program Entity”) and the International Bank for Reconstruction and Development (IBRD), as trustee of Tranche A of the Carbon Fund of the Forest Carbon Partnership Facility (FCPF) (“Trustee of Tranche A of the Fund”), entered into an Emission Reductions Payment Agreement (ERPA), dated December 30, 2020 (TF0B4674), for the Northern Laos Emission Reductions Payments Project. The Program Entity (PE) also entered into a separate ERPA with the IBRD, acting as trustee of Tranche B of the Carbon Fund of the FCPF (“Trustee of Tranche B of the Fund”), dated October 30, 2020 (TF0B4673) for the same Project. The total value of the ERPAs is US$42 million for a delivery of 8.4 million tonnes of CO2e Emission Reductions (ERs) for the crediting period of January 1, 2019 – December 31, 2024. [SOURCE: Page_6]

3. The ER Program is referred to by the government as the Northern Lao Governance, Forest Landscapes and Livelihood (GFLL) program and is comprised of six northern provinces^1 covering an area of 8.1 million hectares. The ER Program is results-based, i.e., payments are made to the PE for monitored, reported, and verified ERs resulting from actions and measures to reduce emissions from deforestation and forest degradation in accordance with the ER Program design, and payments are made by the Bank in accordance with the terms and conditions of the ERPAs. The ERPAs have two reporting periods (reporting period 1: January 1, 2019 – December 31, 2021; reporting period 2: January 1, 2022 – December 31, 2024) during which ER monitoring reports are submitted and verified. ER payments are distributed to beneficiaries in accordance with an approved Benefit Sharing Plan (BSP) for the ER Program. 77% of the gross ER payment is allocated as performance-based payments to communities, sub-national government agencies and pilot initiatives, 18% allocated to cover operational costs at national and sub-national government and 5% as a set aside as performance buffer. [SOURCE: Page_6]

C. Implementation Status and Program Performance Ratings 4. In October 2023, the PE successfully completed an independent third-party validation and verification of its first Emission Reductions Monitoring Report (ERMR-1), covering the first reporting period January 1, 2019 – December 31, 2021. The verification resulted in the issuance of 3,204,614 ERs, with a corresponding payment of US$16,023,070 made in July 2024 (of which an advance payment of US$3,000,000 was made in June 2022). 5. The overall achievement of the development objective and implementation progress rating is Moderately Satisfactory as of the ISR of October 7, 2025. The rating was underpinned by the progress made by the PE in the delivery of the first ERMR-1, payment transferred from World Bank on July 17, 2024, submission of the second ER monitoring report (ERMR-2) was delivered on schedule on September 15, 2025 for third party verification and validation, completion of Community Action Plans (CAPs) by and distribution of payments to target villages under the ERMR-1 started on March 31, 2025. CAPs were developed through a two-step community engagement process (CEP) and are a pre-requisite for villages to receive ER payments. This process has taken a significant amount of time which has caused delays in getting the payments distributed to villages. Further, many target villages are remote and inaccessible during the rainy season (June–October) i.e., prone to flood and landslides, thereby, delaying fieldwork needed to develop and complete the CAPs. The World Bank task team has worked closely with the PE including to provide regular guidance on improving efficiency through streamlining the CEP process and better budget planning and allocation of resources at the sub-national level to speed up CAP development and distribution of payment to villages. As of October 2025, a total of 92 CAPs has been completed, and an additional 43 are under preparation, placing the Program on track to achieve its target of 135 CAPs by December 2025 and remaining 112 CAPs and payment before December 2026. ER payment transfer to 52 additional villages with completed CAPs, including upfront payment to villages that have completed the first step of CEP is also underway with Ministry of Finance (MoF). The streamlining of CEP is institutionalized in government systems, and implementation momentum has notably increased across provinces prior to the first distribution of payment. Department of Forestry’s (DOF) leadership and coordination among the National Program Management Unit (NPMU), Provincial Agriculture and Environment Offices (PAEOs) and District Agriculture and Environment Offices (DAEOs) have been strengthened. [SOURCE: Page_7]

6. Environmental and Social Safeguards' performance is rated Satisfactory. The project environmental and social safeguard system is in place with all required instruments adequately complied with. The project is ensuring the monitoring of safeguards related to BSP implementation and forest management activities supporting ER. All agreed actions were completed or largely on track. Safeguard instruments including Environmental and Social Management Framework (ESMF), Ethnic Group Development Framework (EGDF), Process Framework and associated instruments have been updated to ensure safeguard requirements are consistent and mainstreamed into project activities. A Feedback and Grievance Redress Mechanism (FGRM) is in place in 135 (i.e target for 20205) villages where CEP$_1$ has been completed of the 247-target village under ERMR-1. Safeguard and CEP implementation capacity has been strengthened with project staff and consultants adequately mobilized and trained. Safeguards report was submitted in July 2024 and the recent report submitted through Annex 2 of the second ER monitoring report which reports on safeguards implementation. [SOURCE: Page_7]

7. The PE has submitted ERMR-2 report, which has undergone review by the World Bank task team and the FCPF Facility Management team. The independent verification is expected to begin in December 2025. Verification is estimated to take around eight months after which the second payment can be made to the PE. [SOURCE: Page_8]

8. The BSP is also being revised to increase the operational cost to 23% by reallocating funds from the performance buffer. The program exhausted the original 18% allocated operational costs faster than anticipated due to unforeseen implementation challenges, including the high cost of supervising remote project areas, higher-than-expected cost for capacity building at provincial, district, and community levels, and the need to tailor of the community engagement process for the selected 247 villages. The GoL has consulted all relevant stakeholders and worked collaboratively with the World Bank to incorporate the revisions into the BSP. The revised BSP will be completed by January 31, 2026, reviewed and cleared by the World Bank (including the FCPF) and disclosed by February 28, 2026. [SOURCE: Page_8]

9. While no new risks are introduced by the proposed restructuring, residual implementation and reputational risks remain and are being actively managed. These include potential delays in Community Action Plan (CAP) completion due to seasonal accessibility constraints in remote villages, execution risks associated with the accelerated sequencing of BSP implementation for ERMR-2 villages, and reputational risks should benefit distribution be perceived as delayed or inequitable. These risks are mitigated through a sequenced and time-bound action plan, strengthened sub-national coordination, streamlined CEP procedures, enhanced financial management support to villages, and close World Bank implementation support and supervision throughout the extension period. [SOURCE: Page_8]

D. Rationale for Restructuring 10. On September 30, 2025, the PE submitted a letter requesting for the extension of the ERPA closing date by 24 months to enable completion of ERMR-2 verification and ensure continued and complete distribution of payments through the implementation of the BSP for both the first and second ERPA payments. Following discussions with the PE on the progress made to date, including increasing efficiency and accelerating payment distribution to villages — an 18- month extension was proposed and agreed. [SOURCE: Page_8]

11. The proposed extension will enable Lao PDR to fully leverage World Bank support to deliver on the ERPA and meet the PDO while ensuring fiduciary, BSP and safeguards compliance in line with the updated FCPF Directive of December 2024. Specifically, the extension will enable: (i) completion of first ERPA payment distribution to all 247 villages before December 2026 from the ERMR-1, (ii) completion of ERMR-2 verification, and (iii) completion of second ER payment distribution to a further 140 target villages; and (iv) sufficient time for the PE to prepare for transition of its FCPF REDD+ program to other standards and potential access to carbon markets or other bi-lateral results-based payments. Lao PDR has also received a Bank-executed technical assistance funding through the Scaling Climate Actions by Lowering Emissions (SCALE) trust fund to assess ER program implementation, including the effectiveness of ER payments, in addressing the drivers of deforestation and forest degradation and the potential for scaling up the program. The proposed extension will support this transition by allowing the program to incorporate findings from the SCALE TA. [SOURCE: Page_8]

## II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

12. The objective of the restructuring is to extend the closing date of the project. This also requires an amendment of the ERPAs to extend the closing date by 18 months, from December 31, 2025, to June 30, 2027. The change of institutional arrangement is also reflected in the amendment of the ERPAs, to update that the Ministry of Agriculture and Environment is the successor to the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry (as referenced in both Tranche A and Tranche B ERPAs). [SOURCE: Page_9]

13. A detailed action plan (January 2026–June 2027) is presented below to ensure full achievement of the three PDO indicators: (i) Verification of measured and reported ERs generated under the Government’s GFLL program (ERMR-1 and ERMR-2); (ii) Payments for verified ERs under ERMR-1 and ERMR-2; and (iii) Distribution of the ER payments in accordance with the BSP. This action plan focuses on completing benefit distribution to all 247 villages from the first ER payment and expanding implementation to an additional 139 villages under ERMR$_2$, bringing the total to 386 villages. It prioritizes accelerated CAP completion, strengthened financial management, and timely delivery of both the first and second ER payments to all eligible villages with approved CAPs. ERMR-2 verification is expected to be finalized by July 30, 2026, with BSP implementation for the expanded village set continuing through June 2027. All activities are sequenced to ensure completion of benefit-sharing, safeguards, financial reporting, and the PE Closing Report by June 30th, 2027. [SOURCE: Page_9]
