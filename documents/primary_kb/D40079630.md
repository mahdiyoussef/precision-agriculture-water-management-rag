I. PROJECT STATUS AND RATIONALE FOR RESTRUCTURING A. Project Status 1. The Strengthening the Statistical System of Uzbekistan (SSSU) Project was approved on February 16, 2022, for an amount of $50 million and became effective on September 15, 2022. However, implementation only began in March 2024, following the issuance of Presidential Decrees No. 113 and 114, which authorized project spending and defined the legal status of the implementing agency. An initial implementation plan—focused on procuring a data center and launching the Population and Housing Census—was agreed in June 2024 to accelerate disbursements and progress toward the PDO. However, this plan was disrupted when the government shifted priorities and reorganized the implementing agency under Presidential Decree No. 75 and Presidential Resolution No. 27 in February 2025. These changes transformed the National Statistics Office from an agency under the President’s Office into an autonomous National Statistics Committee (NSC), introduced new modernization priorities for Uzbekistan’s statistical system, and appointed new leadership. The census strategy was revised to combine the Population and Housing Census with the Agriculture Census, using a mix of Computer-Assisted Web Interviews (CAWI)—self-administered by respondents—and Computer-Assisted Personal Interviews (CAPI) conducted by local “Mahalla” officials using tablets provided by their agencies. Specifications for the data center were also changed to a mobile data center solution, as government functions will relocate to New Toshkent City within two years (per Presidential Decree No. 162, April 19, 2024). Consequently, procurement of the data center was delayed, and tablet procurement was put on hold. As of June 2025, project disbursement was at 3%. [SOURCE: Page_6]

2. The project was restructured in June 2025 to align with the revised census modality and the government’s new priorities by reallocating expenditures within components and updating the results framework. Major changes were introduced under Component 2 (Data Production), which combined the Population and Housing Census with the Agriculture Census, reduced census financing to reflect the new approach, and redirected funds toward expanding administrative data integration, improving primary data collection through sample surveys, harmonizing statistical methodologies, and strengthening digital capabilities. Minor adjustments were made to Component 1 (Institutional Reform and Capacity) to include development of a new National Statistics Development Strategy (NSDS), and to Component 3 (Equipment and Dissemination) to reinforce the creation of a comprehensive data dissemination policy. The PDO indicators were revised to capture the integrated census under the new modality, and intermediate results indicators were updated accordingly to reflect changes in sub-activities. [SOURCE: Page_6]

3. Since the first restructuring, significant progress has been achieved. The NSC launched the tender for the data center, began drafting a new NSDS, completed an institutional functional review, established a transformation office, and hired key staff. Crucially, the government approved Presidential Resolution No. 173 in September 2025, granting legal authorization for the census, setting its dates for January–February 2026, and formalizing its implementation modality, including institutional structures for coordination at national and local levels. This provided clarity on census funding and financing needs from the project. A pilot census was conducted, and international experts—including the World Bank, UNFPA independent international expert, The Interstate Statistical Committee of the CIS (CIS-STAT), and census and IT specialists from the National Statistics Office of Georgia (Geostat) and National Statistics Office of the Republic of Belarus (Belstat)— reviewed implementation readiness during November 12–15, 2025, confirming overall preparedness while identifying areas for improvement that can be addressed within the remaining timeline. B. Rationale for Restructuring 4. This second restructuring builds on recent momentum to accommodate new types of expenditures needed for implementing the integrated Population, Housing, and Agriculture Census while accelerating project disbursement. The revised census modality aims to minimize incremental costs by leveraging existing government infrastructure, hardware, and personnel. Mahalla officials – the Mahalla of 7 - will serve as enumerators, receiving a one-time incentive payment instead of hiring new enumerators. They will use tablets already available through their agencies, with only a limited number of reserve tablets procured by the NSC which will also be used for future data collection activities. This shift reduces hardware costs and changes incremental expenses from contractual enumerator fees to incentive payments, which are currently ineligible under the financing agreement. The restructuring therefore amends the agreement to redefine incremental operating costs to include these payments, which will represent the largest share of the census budget and be disbursed after enumeration concludes in February 2026—ensuring successful census implementation, significant cost savings compared to the original plan (centered in high volume of tablets and contractual enumerator costs), and a substantial increase in project disbursement in FY26. [SOURCE: Page_7]

## II. DESCRIPTION OF PROPOSED CHANGES

C. Project Scope and activities 5. The project's scope and activities remain the same. However, eligible expenditures will be revised to include one-time incentive bonus payments, up to a total of USD 8.5 million, for civil servants engaged in census enumeration, supervision and coordination during the implementation period, as defined in Presidential Decree No. 173 (September 19, 2025). To this end, a definition of a bonus payment is added to the Financing Agreement, and the definition of incremental costs is revised to read as follows: [SOURCE: Page_7]

•     “Bonus payments” means the one-time incentive bonus payments to be paid for the civil servants engaged in the 2026 census enumeration, supervision and coordination. [SOURCE: Page_7]

•     “Incremental Operating Costs” means incremental operating expenditures incurred by NSC on account of activities related to the management, monitoring, evaluation, coordination and implementation of the Project, including office equipment maintenance and repair, vehicles, local travel, communication, translation and interpretation, bank charges, consulting services, salaries of the PMU staff (excluding salaries of the Recipient’s civil servants), Bonus payments, and other costs of similar nature directly associated with the Project, all based on periodic budgets acceptable to the Association. [SOURCE: Page_7]
